Does HDMI ARC Support Uncompressed 5.1 Dolby Digital?

An HDMI ARC (Audio Return Channel) connection can transmit a standard 5.1 Dolby Digital bitstream without adding extra compression, but it cannot deliver completely uncompressed, artifact-free audio. Because the standard Dolby Digital format (AC-3) is inherently a lossy compression codec, the audio inherently contains minor compression artifacts regardless of the cable. Furthermore, HDMI ARC lacks the bandwidth required for true lossless 5.1 formats such as Dolby TrueHD or multi-channel uncompressed LPCM, both of which require the newer HDMI eARC standard.

HDMI ARC Bandwidth and Capabilities

HDMI ARC was introduced with the HDMI 1.4 specification to simplify home theater cabling by allowing audio to travel upstream from a television to an AV receiver or soundbar. However, it operates under strict bandwidth limitations, maxing out at roughly 1 Megabit per second (Mbps).

Because of this limited throughput, HDMI ARC natively supports only:

  • Uncompressed stereo audio (2.0 PCM)
  • Compressed bitstream audio, including standard Dolby Digital (AC-3) up to 640 kbps
  • DTS Digital Surround up to 1.5 Mbps (on compatible hardware)

The Nature of Dolby Digital (AC-3)

Standard Dolby Digital is fundamentally a lossy audio codec. It uses perceptual coding algorithms to remove acoustic data that the human ear cannot easily perceive, reducing file sizes for broadcasting and streaming.

When you pass a standard 5.1 Dolby Digital signal over an HDMI ARC connection:

  • The connection itself does not add compression: HDMI ARC has sufficient bandwidth to transmit the maximum 640 kbps Dolby Digital stream fully intact without further degradation or downsampling.
  • The audio is still compressed: Any artifacts present in the original Dolby Digital encode remain, as the format itself is not lossless.

What Happens With Lossless Audio Sources?

If you are playing a source with pristine, uncompressed multi-channel audio—such as a 4K Blu-ray with a 5.1/7.1 Dolby TrueHD track or uncompressed 5.1 LPCM—an HDMI ARC connection cannot carry that signal.

In this scenario, the source device or TV will handle the limitation in one of two ways:

  1. Transcoding/Downscaling: It will strip the lossless TrueHD data and output a lossy Dolby Digital (AC-3) core, introducing compression artifacts to a track that was originally pristine.
  2. Downmixing: It will convert the multi-channel lossless track to a standard 2.0 uncompressed stereo PCM track, maintaining audio fidelity but losing your surround channels.

The Solution for Truly Uncompressed 5.1: HDMI eARC

To experience 5.1 surround sound entirely free of compression artifacts, you must upgrade to HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel), introduced with HDMI 2.1.

HDMI eARC expands the audio bandwidth from roughly 1 Mbps up to 37 Mbps. This massive increase allows the connection to effortlessly transmit:

  • Uncompressed 5.1 and 7.1 LPCM audio
  • Lossless multi-channel formats, including Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio
  • Object-based audio metadata like uncompressed Dolby Atmos

If you are listening to standard streaming services (Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video), HDMI ARC is adequate because those platforms only stream lossy Dolby Digital Plus or Dolby Digital audio. However, if you require completely uncompressed 5.1 audio without any compression artifacts, you must use HDMI eARC or connect your source device directly to an AV receiver via a standard HDMI input.
