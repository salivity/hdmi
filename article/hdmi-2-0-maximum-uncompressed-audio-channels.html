HDMI 2.0 Maximum Uncompressed Audio Channels

This article provides a direct overview of the audio capabilities introduced with the HDMI 2.0 specification, focusing specifically on uncompressed multi-channel audio delivery. Readers will learn the exact channel limitations for uncompressed Linear Pulse-Code Modulation (LPCM) sound, the total sample frequencies supported, and how these capabilities compare to earlier HDMI standards to accommodate modern immersive surround-sound formats.

The HDMI 2.0 specification supports a maximum of 32 uncompressed audio channels. Transmitted as uncompressed Linear PCM (LPCM), this marked a significant upgrade from HDMI 1.4, which capped uncompressed audio output at 8 channels (a standard 7.1 surround-sound configuration).

To support up to 32 discrete channels without compression artifacts, HDMI 2.0 expanded the aggregate audio sample frequency up to 1536 kHz. This bandwidth allows the interface to dynamically allocate sample rates across active channels. For example, it can deliver 32 channels at 48 kHz each, or 8 channels at 192 kHz, maintaining high fidelity across complex speaker arrays.

The expansion to 32 audio channels was primarily engineered to facilitate multi-dimensional, object-based, and high-channel-count audio setups, such as 22.2 surround sound formats and advanced commercial theater configurations. While modern formats like Dolby Atmos and DTS:X often travel as bitstream metadata over fewer channels in consumer setups, the HDMI 2.0 hardware layer possesses the raw capacity to transmit up to 32 separate, fully uncompressed LPCM streams simultaneously, provided both the source device and the audio-video receiver (AVR) support the feature.
