HDMI 1.4b vs HDMI 2.0: 3D Video Differences

This article examines the core technical differences between HDMI 1.4b and HDMI 2.0 with a specific focus on 3D video transmission. While HDMI 1.4b established the foundational standards for high-definition consumer 3D content, HDMI 2.0 significantly expanded available bandwidth, enabling higher frame rates, richer color depths, and advanced multi-stream features for stereoscopic displays.

Bandwidth Capacity and Throughput

The primary differentiator between HDMI 1.4b and HDMI 2.0 is total bandwidth throughput:

  • HDMI 1.4b: Caps at an aggregate bandwidth of 10.2 Gbps (with an effective maximum data rate of 8.16 Gbps after 8b/10b encoding overhead).
  • HDMI 2.0: Expands the maximum bandwidth to 18.0 Gbps (yielding an effective data rate of 14.4 Gbps).

Because 3D video effectively requires sending two distinct video signals—one for each eye—bandwidth dictates the resolution, frame rate, and color fidelity achievable without compression artifacts.

3D Resolution and Frame Rate Support

HDMI 1.4 established the mandatory 3D video formats, and HDMI 1.4b refined frame packing timings. However, its bandwidth limitations imposed strict ceilings on resolution and refresh rates:

  • HDMI 1.4b Limitations:
    • Full HD 3D (1080p frame packed) is practically restricted to 24 Hz per eye (ideal for 3D Blu-ray film content, but inadequate for high-motion gaming).
    • To achieve 50 Hz or 60 Hz per eye under HDMI 1.4b, the resolution must drop to 720p 3D or use half-resolution formats like Side-by-Side (Half) or Top-and-Bottom.
  • HDMI 2.0 Capabilities:
    • Easily transmits Full HD 1080p 3D at 60 Hz per eye (totaling 120 Hz playback), delivering smooth motion for stereoscopic 3D gaming and live broadcasting.
    • Theoretically supports 4K 3D at up to 24/30 Hz, a format impossible under the bandwidth limits of HDMI 1.4b.

Dual-Stream Video Transmission

HDMI 2.0 introduced native hardware support for simultaneous dual-video streaming to a single display:

  • HDMI 1.4b: Designed to deliver a single stereoscopic 3D signal to be combined and viewed by one user (or multiple users viewing the same 3D perspective).
  • HDMI 2.0: Enables independent concurrent video streams. This allows displays to offer features like full-screen two-player gaming (Dual Play), where each player wears polarized 3D glasses to see an independent 2D full HD image simultaneously on the same screen, accompanied by multi-stream audio delivery.

Color Depth and Chroma Subsampling

Stereoscopic 3D signals demand substantial data, often forcing compromises in color fidelity on earlier standards:

  • HDMI 1.4b: High-frame-rate 3D content is restricted to standard 8-bit color depth and often relies on 4:2:0 or 4:2:2 chroma subsampling to fit within the 10.2 Gbps channel.
  • HDMI 2.0: Supports deep color (10-bit and 12-bit) with uncompressed 4:4:4 chroma subsampling for 1080p 3D at standard refresh rates, eliminating color banding and preserving fine image detail across both stereoscopic views.
