Signaling Pixel Graphics via HDMI IT Content Flag

This article explains how HDMI sources utilize the IT Content (ITC) bit within the Auxiliary Video Information (AVI) InfoFrame to signal that an incoming video signal consists of pure, computer-generated pixel graphics. By setting this flag, a transmitting device instructs a sink display to bypass consumer-video enhancements—such as overscan, motion smoothing, and edge sharpening—ensuring text and pixel-aligned graphics render with bit-accurate precision.

The Role of the AVI InfoFrame

HDMI transmits metadata alongside active audio and video data during the horizontal and vertical blanking intervals, known as Data Island periods. The Auxiliary Video Information (AVI) InfoFrame, defined under the CTA-861 (formerly CEA-861) standard, is the primary data structure used to convey format and colorimetry metadata. It informs the sink device (such as a TV or monitor) about color space, aspect ratio, color range, and the intended content type.

The IT Content (ITC) Bit

Within the payload of the AVI InfoFrame, the transmission of computer graphics is primarily controlled by the IT Content (ITC) bit:

  • Location: Data Byte 3, Bit 7 of the AVI InfoFrame.
  • ITC = 0 (No Data / Consumer Video): The source signals standard consumer television or video content (e.g., movies, broadcast television, or optical disc playback). The display is permitted to apply typical CE (Consumer Electronics) processing.
  • ITC = 1 (IT Content): The source explicitly signals that the stream is "Information Technology" content, which denotes pixel-based computer graphics, desktop interfaces, or productivity software.

Content Type (CN) Signaling

When the ITC bit is set to 1, the CTA-861 standard allows the source to further categorize the content using the Content Type (CN) bits, located in Data Byte 5 (Bits 5 and 4):

  • CN = 00: Graphics (Text, CAD, desktop interfaces)
  • CN = 01: Photo
  • CN = 10: Cinema
  • CN = 11: Game

For pure pixel graphics, the source sets ITC = 1 alongside CN = 00 (Graphics), explicitly instructing the display that the incoming frames require text clarity and high-contrast edge fidelity rather than continuous-tone photographic smoothing.

Display Behavior Upon Receiving the IT Content Flag

When an HDMI sink detects ITC = 1, it adjusts its internal video processing pipeline to maintain 1:1 pixel fidelity:

  1. Disabling Overscan: Consumer displays often apply a 2% to 5% zoom to hide broadcast artifacts at the edges of the frame. The IT Content flag forces the display into an exact 1:1 pixel mapping mode (often labeled "Just Scan," "Dot by Dot," or "Screen Fit").
  2. Bypassing Picture Enhancements: Artifact-removal filters, dynamic contrast adjustments, temporal noise reduction, and aggressive edge enhancement are disabled or minimized to prevent haloing around crisp text and user-interface elements.
  3. Color and Subsampling Handling: IT content expects full RGB or 4:4:4 color representation. While colorimetry is signaled separately in the InfoFrame, signaling IT content ensures the display processes fine, single-pixel lines and text characters without chroma smearing.
