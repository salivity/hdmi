How HDMI 1.4 Implements HDMI Ethernet Channel

The HDMI Ethernet Channel (HEC) introduced in HDMI 1.4 allows connected home entertainment devices to share a standard 100 Mbps bidirectional Ethernet connection over the same HDMI cable used for audio and video. By combining networking capabilities with audio/video transmission, HEC was designed to eliminate the need for dedicated Ethernet cables running to smart TVs, gaming consoles, and streaming set-top boxes. This article explains the physical pin reassignments, electrical signaling techniques, and cable construction required to implement HEC under the HDMI 1.4 specification.

Pin Allocation and the HEAC Pair

Prior to HDMI 1.4, the 19-pin Type A HDMI connector contained several unused or single-purpose pins. HDMI 1.4 repurposed two of these pins to create a new hybrid data channel:

  • Pin 14: Formerly designated as "Reserved" (NC), this pin was repurposed as the Utility pin.
  • Pin 19: Originally dedicated solely to Hot Plug Detect (HPD), which detects when a sink device is connected.

Together, Pin 14 and Pin 19 form a balanced differential transmission line known as the HDMI Ethernet and Audio Return Channel (HEAC) pair. Pin 17 provides the reference ground and shielding for this pair.

Signal Multiplexing and Differential Signaling

The primary technical challenge in implementing HEC was transmitting high-speed bidirectional network data without interfering with the existing Hot Plug Detect mechanism or the simultaneously introduced Audio Return Channel (ARC).

HDMI 1.4 solves this by separating DC and AC signaling across the HEAC pair:

  1. Hot Plug Detect (DC): Pin 19 maintains its original HPD function using a steady DC voltage level. Sink devices pull this line high to signal their presence to the source device.
  2. HEC Data (Differential AC): Ethernet communication operates as a differential AC signal (100BASE-TX equivalent) transmitted across both Pin 14 and Pin 19. Capacitive coupling isolates the high-frequency Ethernet signals from the DC voltage used by the HPD signal.
  3. ARC Integration: When single-mode Audio Return Channel is active, it runs over Pin 14 (Utility) as a single-ended signal. When common-mode ARC is used alongside HEC, the audio and network traffic are combined across the differential pair via digital modulation without disrupting the DC state of Pin 19.

This hybrid physical layer allows devices to maintain 100 Mbps full-duplex Ethernet data transfer simultaneously with audio return and connection detection.

Physical Cable Construction

Standard HDMI cables manufactured prior to version 1.4 often wired Pin 14 and Pin 19 as simple, unshielded single conductors. Because Fast Ethernet requires impedance-controlled, noise-resistant transmission lines, HDMI 1.4 introduced new cable categories:

  • Standard HDMI Cable with Ethernet
  • High Speed HDMI Cable with Ethernet

In cables certified for Ethernet, the wires connected to Pin 14 and Pin 19 are manufactured as a dedicated shielded twisted pair (STP), with Pin 17 acting as the drain/shield wire. This internal shielding prevents crosstalk between the high-frequency TMDS video lines and the Ethernet channel, ensuring reliable 100 Mbps throughput over cable lengths up to standard specification limits.
