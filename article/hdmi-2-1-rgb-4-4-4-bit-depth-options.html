HDMI 2.1 RGB 4:4:4 Bit-Depth Options

HDMI 2.1 significantly expands video bandwidth up to 48 Gbps, enabling support for multiple bit-depth options when transmitting uncompressed and compressed RGB 4:4:4 video signals. Depending on the connected display, source device capabilities, and resolution refresh rate targets, users can output RGB 4:4:4 at 8-bit, 10-bit, 12-bit, and 16-bit color depths. This guide breaks down each available bit-depth option, how it functions within the HDMI 2.1 specification, and the role of bandwidth and compression.

Available Bit-Depth Options for RGB 4:4:4

The HDMI 2.1 specification supports four distinct bit depths when transmitting full-bandwidth RGB 4:4:4 video:

  • 8-bit (24 bits per pixel): The standard baseline for consumer video and standard dynamic range (SDR). An 8-bit RGB signal delivers 256 shades per color channel, resulting in approximately 16.7 million possible colors. It requires the least bandwidth and is universally supported across all HDMI 2.1 displays and legacy devices.
  • 10-bit (30 bits per pixel): The industry standard for High Dynamic Range (HDR) content, including HDR10 and HDR10+. A 10-bit RGB signal provides 1,024 shades per color channel, producing roughly 1.07 billion colors. This eliminates noticeable color banding in gradients and is the most common configuration for modern 4K gaming consoles and high-performance PC graphics cards.
  • 12-bit (36 bits per pixel): Primarily utilized by premium dynamic HDR formats such as Dolby Vision. A 12-bit RGB signal offers 4,096 shades per channel, totaling over 68.7 billion colors. While very few consumer display panels possess native 12-bit hardware, HDMI 2.1 allows source devices to output 12-bit RGB signals to preserve color fidelity during signal processing.
  • 16-bit (48 bits per pixel): The maximum Deep Color ceiling defined by the HDMI specification. A 16-bit RGB signal provides 65,536 shades per channel, allowing for over 281 trillion colors. This option is rarely used in consumer applications and is primarily reserved for specialized commercial, medical imaging, and post-production workflows.

Bandwidth and Compression Considerations

While HDMI 2.1 supports up to 16-bit RGB 4:4:4, the availability of these options in your system settings depends on the transmission mode and data rate:

  • Fixed Rate Link (FRL): HDMI 2.1 utilizes FRL to achieve up to 48 Gbps of uncompressed data transmission. At 4K resolution at 120Hz, an uncompressed RGB 4:4:4 10-bit signal requires approximately 40.1 Gbps, fitting comfortably within the maximum 48 Gbps ceiling. Uncompressed 12-bit at 4K 120Hz exceeds standard uncompressed margins unless specific timing intervals are reduced.
  • Display Stream Compression (DSC 1.2a): For higher bandwidth scenarios—such as 4K at 144Hz/240Hz, or 8K at 60Hz in RGB 4:4:4—HDMI 2.1 employs visually lossless DSC. With DSC enabled, both 10-bit and 12-bit RGB 4:4:4 can be output without sacrificing color detail, frame rate, or chroma resolution.
