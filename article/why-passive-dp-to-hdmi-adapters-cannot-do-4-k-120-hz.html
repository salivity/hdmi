Why Passive DP to HDMI Adapters Cannot Do 4K 120Hz

Passive DisplayPort to HDMI adapters cannot achieve HDMI 2.1 speeds—such as 4K resolution at a 120Hz refresh rate—due to physical hardware limitations, obsolete internal fallback protocols, and a fundamental shift in signal encoding. While passive adapters rely on the graphics card to switch its output to legacy HDMI signaling via DisplayPort Dual-Mode (DP++), this mechanism is strictly capped at older HDMI 2.0 performance levels. Delivering 4K at 120Hz requires modern HDMI 2.1 architecture, which passive cables lack the hardware to produce.

The Limitation of DisplayPort Dual-Mode (DP++)

A passive adapter contains no internal processing chipset; it is essentially a physical pin-to-pin mapping cable with basic level-shifting resistors. For a passive adapter to work, the video source (the GPU) must support DisplayPort Dual-Mode, commonly marked as DP++.

When a DP++ port detects a passive adapter, the GPU itself alters the signal it outputs. Instead of sending native DisplayPort packetized data, it outputs HDMI-compatible Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS). However, the DP++ standard was finalized during the era of HDMI 1.4 and HDMI 2.0. The standard maxes out at a 600 MHz TMDS character clock, providing a maximum data throughput of approximately 18 Gbps. This bandwidth is only sufficient for 4K at 60Hz (8-bit) and falls far short of the requirements for 4K at 120Hz.

The Shift from TMDS to Fixed Rate Link (FRL)

To achieve the bandwidth required for 4K at 120Hz—which demands between 32 Gbps and 48 Gbps depending on color depth and chroma subsampling—HDMI 2.1 completely abandoned the legacy TMDS protocol in favor of Fixed Rate Link (FRL) technology.

FRL operates similarly to DisplayPort’s native packet-based transmission, using different signaling voltages, link training routines, and clock distributions compared to TMDS. Modern GPUs do not feature a "DP++ 2.1" mode capable of switching their internal transmitters to output native HDMI 2.1 FRL signaling directly over DisplayPort pins. Because a passive adapter cannot alter or translate protocols on its own, it cannot force the GPU to transmit an FRL signal.

Clock and Bandwidth Deficits

A passive adapter passed directly to an HDMI 2.1 display leaves the display and source mismatched:

  • Passive DP-to-HDMI Ceiling: 18 Gbps (HDMI 2.0 spec limits via DP++).
  • HDMI 2.1 4K 120Hz Requirement: Minimum ~32.08 Gbps (4:2:0 8-bit) to 40.1 Gbps (4:4:4 10-bit HDR uncompressed).

Even if a source GPU has DisplayPort 1.4 or DisplayPort 2.1 output capabilities capable of driving high bandwidth, a passive adapter forces the GPU to drop down to the lowest common denominator: legacy HDMI 2.0 TMDS mode.

Why an Active Adapter Is Required

To bridge the gap between a modern DisplayPort output and an HDMI 2.1 input at 4K 120Hz, an active adapter is mandatory.

Active adapters do not use DP++ mode. Instead, they instruct the GPU to output native DisplayPort 1.4 or DisplayPort 2.1 signals at full speed. An onboard protocol converter chip inside the active adapter intercepts the DisplayPort packets, decodes the stream, and actively re-encodes the data into an authentic HDMI 2.1 FRL bitstream before sending it to the display. Active adapters also handle features like Display Stream Compression (DSC), which are often necessary to fit 4K 120Hz HDR pipelines within the physical capabilities of the interface.
