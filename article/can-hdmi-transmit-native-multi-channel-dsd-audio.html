Can HDMI Transmit Native Multi-Channel DSD Audio?

Yes, an HDMI port can transmit high-resolution, multi-channel Direct Stream Digital (DSD) audio directly without converting it to Pulse-Code Modulation (PCM). This functionality has been an official part of the HDMI standard since version 1.2 was introduced in 2005. However, successfully maintaining an unconverted DSD path requires compatible source hardware, proper output settings, and an AV receiver (AVR) capable of native DSD digital-to-analog processing.

The HDMI Standard and DSD

The HDMI 1.2 specification added native support for "One-Bit Audio," the technical architecture behind Super Audio CD (SACD) and DSD formats. Over a standard HDMI connection, the protocol can carry up to 8 channels of uncompressed DSD64 (2.8224 MHz sampling rate). The data travels across the interface encapsulated in native DSD audio sample packets rather than standard LPCM packets.

Source Device Requirements

To transmit DSD without PCM conversion, the source device (such as a universal Blu-ray/SACD player, network streamer, or specialized PC interface) must support DSD bitstreaming over HDMI.

In the source device's audio settings, the HDMI audio output must be set to "Bitstream" or "DSD," rather than "PCM." If set to PCM, the player will decode the 1-bit DSD stream into multi-channel multi-bit PCM internally before transmitting it over the cable.

Receiver and DAC Capabilities

The receiving device—typically a multi-channel AVR or audio processor—must feature an HDMI receiver chip and an internal Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC) capable of handling raw DSD bitstreams. When receiving native DSD, most compatible receivers will display "DSD" on the front panel instead of "PCM."

Avoiding Internal PCM Conversion (The DSP Trap)

While HDMI can deliver raw DSD into the receiver, many modern AVRs automatically convert incoming DSD to PCM internally before converting it to an analog signal. This internal conversion occurs because standard Digital Signal Processors (DSP) cannot apply equalization, speaker distance delays, bass management, or room correction systems (such as Audyssey or Dirac Live) to a 1-bit DSD stream.

To ensure true native DSD-to-analog conversion without any intermediate PCM conversion inside the AVR:

  • The receiver must be placed in a "Direct," "Pure Direct," or "DSD Direct" mode.
  • Engaging this mode bypasses all internal DSP processing, sending the raw 1-bit stream straight to a DSD-capable DAC architecture.
