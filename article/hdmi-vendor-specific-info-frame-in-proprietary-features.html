HDMI Vendor-Specific InfoFrame in Proprietary Features

The HDMI Vendor-Specific InfoFrame (VSIF) is a specialized data packet defined within the CTA-861 standard and HDMI specifications that allows manufacturers to transmit custom, non-standardized metadata across an HDMI link. This article explains how hardware developers use the VSIF to deploy proprietary audio/video enhancements, dynamic HDR formats, specialized gaming modes, and closed-ecosystem hardware handshakes without breaking compatibility with standard consumer displays.

The Architecture of the Vendor-Specific InfoFrame

Standard HDMI communication relies on predefined InfoFrames—such as the Auxiliary Video Information (AVI) InfoFrame and Audio InfoFrame—to communicate basic parameters like color space, aspect ratio, and audio channel mapping. Because standard InfoFrames are strictly defined by standard bodies, they cannot accommodate experimental, proprietary, or rapidly evolving features.

The VSIF acts as an extensible container for metadata. It includes standard header bytes, followed by an Organizationally Unique Identifier (OUI)—a 24-bit IEEE registration code assigned to a specific manufacturer or consortium—and a variable payload. The sink (display or receiver) reads the OUI to determine whether it understands the payload. If the display matches the OUI, it decodes the custom data; if it does not recognize the OUI, it safely ignores the packet without interrupting normal audio or video playback.

Enabling Proprietary and Emerging HDR Formats

One of the most notable uses of the VSIF is the transport of dynamic High Dynamic Range (HDR) metadata before or alongside formal industry standards:

  • Dolby Vision: Before HDMI standardization included native dynamic metadata support, Dolby used custom VSIFs (using Dolby's OUI) to transmit scene-by-scene dynamic tone-mapping instructions over standard HDMI 1.4b and 2.0 interconnects.
  • HDR10+: Before broader standard updates, Samsung and other HDR10+ consortium members utilized vendor-specific data structures to negotiate and stream dynamic HDR metadata to supported displays.

Gaming Technologies and Variable Refresh Rates

Before HDMI 2.1 introduced standard HDMI VRR, hardware vendors utilized the VSIF to negotiate proprietary synchronization protocols:

  • AMD FreeSync over HDMI: AMD utilized vendor-specific InfoFrames to identify compatible displays, communicate variable refresh boundaries, and activate low-framerate compensation (LFC).
  • Auto Low Latency Modes: Early iterations of custom low-latency handshakes between consoles and specific TV brands relied on proprietary VSIF flags to trigger game modes automatically before the standardization of ALLM.

Ecosystem-Specific Hardware Communication

Manufacturers with integrated hardware ecosystems (e.g., soundbars, game consoles, and smart televisions) use VSIFs to unlock exclusive interactions:

  • Audio/Speaker Synchronization: Features that combine television speakers with an external soundbar (such as Samsung Q-Symphony or Sony Acoustic Center Sync) use proprietary metadata to manage phase, latency, and channel assignment.
  • Custom Color Profiling and Calibration: Professional broadcast monitors and proprietary gaming displays can receive custom Look-Up Tables (LUTs), sensor telemetry, or factory calibration flags embedded in proprietary InfoFrames.
  • 3D and Custom Formats: In early 3D video implementations, the HDMI Licensing Administrator utilized its own OUI (0x000C03) within a VSIF to specify frame-packing and top-and-bottom 3D structures.

By providing a safe, backward-compatible bypass around rigid standard bodies, the HDMI Vendor-Specific InfoFrame serves as the primary mechanism for manufacturers to deploy cutting-edge hardware features, field-test new capabilities, and maintain exclusive ecosystem features.
