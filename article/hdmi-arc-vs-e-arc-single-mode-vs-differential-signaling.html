HDMI ARC vs eARC: Single-Mode vs Differential Signaling

This article examines the physical layer differences between HDMI Audio Return Channel (ARC) and Enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC). While both standards route TV audio upstream to an external soundbar or AV receiver over a standard HDMI cable, they rely on fundamentally different transmission architectures. HDMI ARC uses a low-bandwidth, single-mode signaling approach, whereas HDMI eARC employs a high-speed differential signaling pair to dramatically increase data capacity, eliminate noise, and support uncompressed modern audio formats.

What Is Single-Mode HDMI ARC?

Introduced in HDMI 1.4, HDMI ARC (Audio Return Channel) was designed to reduce cable clutter by allowing a television to send audio back to an audio receiver over the same HDMI cable used for video input.

ARC's underlying architecture is based on the legacy S/PDIF (IEC 60958-1) standard. In its basic hardware implementation, ARC operates using single-mode signaling over a single physical conductor within the HDMI cable: Pin 14, designated as the "Utility" line.

Key characteristics of single-mode ARC include:

  • Single-Ended Transmission: Single-mode ARC relies on a single wire relative to a shared ground reference. Because the receiver measures the voltage difference between the conductor and ground, the signal is vulnerable to external electromagnetic interference (EMI) and ground loops.
  • Bandwidth Limitations: Operating on an S/PDIF-style protocol, ARC provides an effective bandwidth of roughly 1 to 2 Megabits per second (Mbps).
  • Audio Format Constraints: Due to its low bandwidth, ARC only supports two-channel uncompressed PCM audio or lossy, compressed multi-channel formats such as standard Dolby Digital and DTS Digital Surround. It cannot carry uncompressed 5.1/7.1 surround sound or lossless object-based formats like Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio.
  • Reliance on HDMI-CEC: Single-mode ARC depends on the HDMI Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) protocol for device handshaking, discovery, and lip-sync correction, which often leads to interoperability issues across different manufacturers.

What Is HDMI eARC Differential Signaling?

Introduced in HDMI 2.1, HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) completely redesigns the physical transport layer to overcome the throughput and stability bottlenecks of ARC. Instead of a single conductor, eARC uses a differential signaling structure.

Differential signaling works by transmitting two complementary signals simultaneously over a balanced pair of wires: Pin 14 (Utility) and Pin 19 (Hot Plug Detect / HPD). One wire carries the positive signal, while the other carries an exact inverted replica. The receiver decodes the transmission by measuring the voltage difference between the two conductors rather than checking the voltage against ground.

Key advantages of the differential structure include:

  • High Common-Mode Noise Rejection: Any electromagnetic noise picked up along the cable path affects both conductors equally. When the receiver calculates the differential voltage, the identical noise on both lines cancels out, enabling clean signal integrity over longer distances.
  • Massive Bandwidth Increase: eARC provides an effective bandwidth of up to 37 Mbps—more than thirty times that of ARC.
  • Support for Lossless Formats: The increased throughput allows eARC to carry uncompressed 5.1 and 7.1 Linear PCM audio, as well as lossless high-bitrate formats such as Dolby TrueHD, Dolby Atmos, DTS-HD Master Audio, and DTS:X.
  • Integrated Data Channel: The differential link incorporates a dedicated bidirectional data channel. This channel handles discovery, capability reporting, and lip-sync compensation independently of HDMI-CEC, resulting in far more reliable device communication.
