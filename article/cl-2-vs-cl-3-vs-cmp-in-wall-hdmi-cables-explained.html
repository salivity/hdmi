CL2 vs CL3 vs CMP In-Wall HDMI Cables Explained

Running HDMI cables inside walls or ceilings requires strict adherence to fire safety regulations defined by the National Electrical Code (NEC). CL2, CL3, and CMP ratings designate a cable's resistance to fire, smoke production, and electrical voltage, ensuring it does not become a fire hazard or channel toxic gases throughout a building. While CL2 and CL3 are designed for standard residential and light commercial in-wall installations, CMP cables provide the highest level of flame resistance and low-smoke emission, making them mandatory for commercial air-handling plenum spaces.

The Purpose of Cable Ratings

Standard HDMI cables use PVC jackets that emit dense, toxic smoke and burn rapidly when exposed to fire. When cables run behind drywall or above ceilings, they can act as a wick, spreading fire from room to room. NEC fire-safety standards test cables for flame propagation, heat release, and smoke toxicity to mitigate this risk.

CL2 (Class 2) Rated HDMI Cables

CL2 cables are designed for general residential in-wall use.

  • Voltage Capacity: Rated to safely carry up to 150 volts.
  • Fire Safety: The outer jacket is formulated to self-extinguish when burning and prevent flames from traveling vertically between floors in a standard wall cavity.
  • Primary Use: Running video connections behind drywall, such as from an AV receiver to a wall-mounted TV in a home setting.

CL3 (Class 3) Rated HDMI Cables

CL3 cables are very similar to CL2 cables regarding fire and smoke resistance, but they support higher electrical capacity.

  • Voltage Capacity: Rated to carry up to 300 volts.
  • Fire Safety: Meets the same flame-retardant burn tests as CL2 cables.
  • Primary Use: Commercial installations or long-distance residential runs where active HDMI signal boosters or external power injectors require higher voltage. CL3 is backward-compatible and can always be used in place of CL2.

CMP (Plenum-Rated) HDMI Cables

CMP stands for "Communications Multipurpose Cable, Plenum." This is the strictest rating among the three.

  • Fire and Smoke Safety: CMP cables feature jackets made from specialized low-smoke, flame-retardant materials (such as Teflon or specialized fluoropolymers). They produce minimal smoke and do not release high levels of toxic fumes when burned.
  • Air-Handling Compliance: Designed specifically for plenum spaces—the open areas above drop ceilings or beneath raised floors used by HVAC systems to circulate environmental air. Because these spaces have high oxygen levels and can quickly circulate toxic fumes throughout a building, ordinary in-wall cables are prohibited by building codes.
  • Primary Use: Commercial buildings, schools, and offices where HDMI wiring must cross HVAC-return air paths.

Key Differences Summary

  • Location: CL2 and CL3 are strictly for non-plenum in-wall installations (inside vertical stud cavities). CMP is mandatory for any space handling environmental air (drop ceilings, ductwork).
  • Smoke Generation: CMP cables emit significantly less smoke and toxicity than CL2 and CL3 cables during a fire.
  • Voltage Rating: CL2 supports up to 150V, CL3 supports up to 300V, and CMP cables meet specialized low-voltage communications standards with superior fire resistance.
  • Hierarchy: CMP cables can replace CL2 or CL3 cables, but CL2 or CL3 cables can never be used in a plenum space.
