HDMI InfoFrame Colorimetry Standards Explained

HDMI interfaces transmit critical color rendering metadata from a source device to a display using Auxiliary Video Information (AVI) InfoFrames and Dynamic Range and Mastering (DRM) InfoFrames. This signaling informs the sink device of the exact color space, encoding format, and gamut to render, ensuring accurate reproduction of standards like BT.601, BT.709, and wide-gamut formats such as BT.2020. Without these InfoFrame packets, displays would be unable to distinguish standard dynamic range Rec.709 content from wide color gamut HDR content.

The Role of the AVI InfoFrame

The primary mechanism for communicating colorimetry across an HDMI link is the AVI InfoFrame, standardized under CTA-861 (formerly CEA-861). Bytes within this packet define both standard and extended colorimetric profiles using dedicated flag bits: the Colorimetry bits (C0 and C1) and Extended Colorimetry bits (EC0, EC1, and EC2).

When a source transmits standard-definition or early high-definition formats, it utilizes basic colorimetry flags (C0, C1):

  • No Data: The display falls back on default resolution-based assumptions.
  • ITU-R BT.601 (SMPTE 170M / ITU-R BT.470): Used for standard-definition (SD) content, accommodating both NTSC and PAL color reproduction matrices.
  • ITU-R BT.709: The universal standard for standard dynamic range (SDR) high-definition video (HDTV), establishing fixed RGB primary coordinates and a D65 white point.

Extended Colorimetry Standards

When the C0 and C1 bits are set to indicate "Extended Colorimetry," the source activates data bits EC0 through EC2 to communicate wider gamuts and specialized computer graphics color spaces. Supported extended standards include:

  • xvYCC601 and xvYCC709 (IEC 61966-2-4): Extended-gamut YCbCr color spaces designed to convey colors outside the traditional BT.601 and BT.709 gamuts while maintaining backward compatibility.
  • sYCC601: A color space matching the standard sRGB gamut while utilizing YCbCr encoding.
  • AdobeYCC601 and AdobeRGB: Primarily used for still photography workflows, enabling displays to render the broader Adobe RGB color gamut natively.
  • ITU-R BT.2020 Non-Constant Luminance (Y'C'bC'r): The standard encoding for Ultra HD (4K and 8K) broadcast and consumer media, providing a wide color gamut (WCG) capable of representing significantly more visible color spectrum than BT.709.
  • ITU-R BT.2020 Constant Luminance (Y'cC'bcC'rc): An alternative encoding method within the BT.2020 specification where luminance is calculated linearly from tristimulus values.
  • ITU-R BT.2020 R'G'B': Used when BT.2020 wide-gamut signals are transmitted uncompressed in full RGB color space rather than sub-sampled YCbCr.

HDR and the DRM InfoFrame

Beginning with HDMI 2.0a, the Dynamic Range and Mastering (DRM) InfoFrame was introduced to support High Dynamic Range (HDR) workflows defined under CTA-861.3 and CTA-861-G. While the AVI InfoFrame continues to signal the BT.2020 color primaries, the DRM InfoFrame signals:

  • Electro-Optical Transfer Functions (EOTF): Identifies whether the signal uses traditional SDR gamma, SMPTE ST 2084 (Perceptual Quantizer / PQ used in HDR10 and Dolby Vision), or Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG / BT.2100).
  • Static Metadata Type 1: Conveys the color volume of the mastering display, including the chromaticity coordinates of the red, green, and blue primaries, white point, and maximum/minimum mastering display luminance.

By pairing the AVI InfoFrame’s colorimetry flags with the DRM InfoFrame’s transfer functions, modern HDMI connections ensure precise color space mapping from source to screen.
