HDMI 2.1 eARC Maximum Audio Bandwidth

HDMI 2.1 eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) significantly upgrades home theater audio capabilities by expanding the data pipeline between a display and an audio system. This article outlines the maximum audio bandwidth available over an eARC connection, explains what that data rate enables in terms of sound formats, and contrasts it directly with the limitations of older audio return standards.

The maximum audio bandwidth available over an HDMI 2.1 eARC connection is approximately 37 megabits per second (Mbps).

This 37 Mbps threshold represents a massive leap over the original ARC (Audio Return Channel) standard, which topped out at roughly 1 Mbps. While the basic ARC protocol used previously unused pins in the HDMI connector to carry standard S/PDIF-grade audio, eARC utilizes dedicated conductors (pins 14 and 19) to create a high-speed, bidirectional communication link that supports high-bitrate data.

With 37 Mbps of dedicated audio bandwidth, eARC can transmit uncompressed and lossless high-resolution audio formats without downmixing or compression. Specifically, eARC supports:

  • Uncompressed Multichannel LPCM: Up to 8 channels (7.1 surround) of uncompressed 24-bit/192 kHz audio.
  • Lossless Object-Based Audio: Full-bandwidth Dolby TrueHD and uncompressed Dolby Atmos.
  • DTS High-Resolution Formats: DTS-HD Master Audio and uncompressed DTS:X.

In addition to pure data capacity, the eARC specification incorporates its own data channel. This integrated channel handles lip-sync correction automatically—ensuring audio matches video timing without manual receiver adjustments—and handles device discovery without relying on HDMI-CEC (Consumer Electronics Control), which often caused interoperability issues in older hardware.

To achieve the full 37 Mbps bandwidth, both the transmitting device (such as an eARC-enabled television) and the receiving device (an AV receiver or soundbar) must support the eARC standard. The connection also requires an HDMI cable with Ethernet support, such as a Standard High Speed HDMI Cable with Ethernet or an Ultra High Speed HDMI cable.
