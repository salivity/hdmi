100-Ohm Differential Impedance in HDMI Cable Design

The 100-ohm differential impedance specification is a foundational standard in HDMI cable engineering that ensures reliable, high-speed digital audio and video transmission. Maintaining this exact impedance prevents signal reflections, preserves waveform integrity, minimizes electromagnetic interference (EMI), and guarantees cross-compatibility across all certified HDMI sources and displays. Without strict adherence to this standard, modern display resolutions such as 4K and 8K would suffer from severe data corruption, jitter, and display dropouts.

The Physics of Differential Signaling in HDMI

HDMI relies on Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) and Fixed Rate Link (FRL) architectures to transmit gigabits of data per second. Instead of sending a single signal down one wire referenced to ground, differential signaling sends two equal and opposite signals down a twisted pair of conductors.

Differential impedance refers to the total impedance measured between these two paired signal conductors. While each individual line typically exhibits a 50-ohm single-ended characteristic impedance relative to the reference plane, the pair together forms a balanced 100-ohm transmission line.

Preventing Signal Reflections via Impedance Matching

In high-frequency transmission lines, any deviation in impedance along the signal path creates an impedance mismatch. When electrical signals encounter an impedance boundary, a portion of the signal energy reflects back toward the source rather than continuing cleanly to the receiver.

The HDMI transmitter (source) and receiver (sink) are both designed with internal terminations calibrated to 100 ohms differential. If the interconnecting cable deviates from this 100-ohm standard:

  • Signal Reflections Occur: Echoed energy bounces between the display and the source, interfering with subsequent data bits.
  • Eye Diagram Degradation: The signal "eye diagram"—the visual representation of digital signal clarity—collapses, leading to timing jitter and inter-symbol interference (ISI).
  • Transmission Errors: At the consumer level, this manifests as visual artifacts ("sparkles"), audio dropouts, or complete loss of handshake (a black screen).

Balancing Attenuation, Geometry, and Manufacturing

The 100-ohm value is an optimized compromise between electrical performance and physical manufacturing constraints:

  1. Lower Impedances (<100 Ohms): Would require thicker conductors or extremely thin dielectric layers between wires. This increases capacitance and conductive losses, significantly limiting the maximum functional length of the cable.
  2. Higher Impedances (>100 Ohms): Would require widely spaced conductors, increasing the overall thickness of the cable and making it vulnerable to external noise while degrading differential coupling.

The 100-ohm standard provides the optimal balance of low attenuation, manageable parasitic capacitance, and a physically flexible form factor suitable for consumer electronics.

Noise Immunity and Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC)

For differential signaling to reject external noise, the two conductors must be tightly coupled. External electromagnetic noise impacts both conductors equally; because the receiver only reads the difference between the two lines, this common-mode noise is cancelled out.

Holding a strict 100-ohm differential impedance requires precise control over the dielectric material, wire gauge, and twist rate along every millimeter of the cable. This physical precision ensures high Common-Mode Rejection Ratio (CMRR) and prevents the cable from radiating high-frequency energy that could interfere with nearby wireless signals like Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

Interoperability Across Devices

The HDMI Compliance Test Specification (CTS) mandates that cables maintain 100 ohms differential impedance within a narrow tolerance (typically ±15%, or ±10% for ultra-high-speed standards). By enforcing this electrical parameter, the standard guarantees that any compliant HDMI cable will seamlessly bridge any compliant source device with any compliant display, regardless of manufacturer.
