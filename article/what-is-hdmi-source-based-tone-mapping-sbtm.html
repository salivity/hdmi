What is HDMI Source-Based Tone Mapping (SBTM)?

HDMI Source-Based Tone Mapping (SBTM) is an advanced feature introduced in the HDMI 2.1a specification designed to optimize High Dynamic Range (HDR) performance by offloading tone mapping calculations from the display to the source device, such as a PC or gaming console. This article explains how SBTM works, why traditional HDR pipelines frequently suffer from degraded image quality caused by double tone mapping, and how SBTM eliminates this issue to deliver accurate, optimized visuals in gaming.

The Problem: Double Tone Mapping in HDR Gaming

Tone mapping is the process of adapting an HDR signal to match the brightness, contrast, and color capabilities of a specific screen. When an HDR signal contains brightness values that exceed a display’s hardware capabilities—such as a 4,000-nit highlight sent to an 800-nit television—the display compresses the dynamic range to avoid severe clipping.

In modern gaming, tone mapping often occurs twice:

  1. At the source level: The game engine or console maps the rendered frame based on internal settings or manual system-level calibration menus.
  2. At the display level: The television or monitor receives the already-adjusted signal and applies its own tone mapping algorithm over it.

This redundant process is called double tone mapping. Because the display does not know that the incoming signal has already been compressed to match its limits, it recompresses the signal. This results in washed-out colors, crushed shadow detail, diminished contrast, and clipped highlights, severely degrading the intended visual fidelity.

How HDMI SBTM Prevents Double Tone Mapping

SBTM addresses this conflict by enabling bidirectional communication between the source device and the display. Instead of forcing the display to guess how to handle incoming HDR data, the display shares its specific HDR performance parameters—such as peak luminance, black levels, and color volume—directly with the source.

Once the source receives this hardware profile, it tailors the HDR output directly to the display’s exact capabilities before transmission:

  • Single-Stage Mapping: The source applies tone mapping tailored to the screen's known parameters.
  • Bypassing Display Processing: Because the signal arriving at the television is already matched to the panel's physical limits, the display does not apply a secondary tone mapping curve. It simply outputs the image as received, eliminating double tone mapping entirely.

Managing Mixed HDR and SDR Content

Beyond standard gameplay, SBTM solves the challenge of mixed dynamic range content. In gaming, graphics engines frequently render standard dynamic range (SDR) user interface elements—such as mini-maps, crosshairs, and text overlays—on top of intense HDR environments.

Traditional displays apply a single, global tone mapping curve to the entire screen. If a bright explosion occurs, the display might dim the whole image to preserve highlights, unintentionally dimming the SDR user interface as well. With SBTM, the source device handles the tone mapping per window or visual layer: it optimizes the HDR background to match the display while keeping UI elements at their intended brightness levels.

Automatic Calibration and Consistency

SBTM eliminates the need for manual, trial-and-error HDR calibration sliders inside every individual game. Because the source device automatically recognizes the display's target brightness and color metrics over the HDMI connection, game engines can calibrate their output automatically. This ensures consistent, accurate HDR presentation across different games and displays without user intervention.
