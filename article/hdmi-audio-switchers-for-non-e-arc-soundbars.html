HDMI Audio Switchers for Non-eARC Soundbars

An external HDMI audio switcher allows users to route high-quality sound to non-eARC soundbars by physically separating the audio signal from the video signal before it reaches the television. Instead of relying on a modern TV to pass advanced audio downstream through an enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC) port, the switcher acts as an intermediary. It sends an untouched, direct audio stream straight to the soundbar's standard HDMI input while independently delivering the video feed to the display.

The Problem with Non-eARC Soundbars

Modern gaming consoles, media players, and Blu-ray players frequently output high-bandwidth, uncompressed audio formats like Dolby TrueHD, Dolby Atmos, DTS:X, and multi-channel LPCM. Older or budget soundbars that lack eARC typically rely on regular ARC (Audio Return Channel) or optical connections. Standard ARC has strict bandwidth limits and cannot carry uncompressed formats. Furthermore, many modern TVs downmix multi-channel audio or refuse to pass through specific formats (such as DTS) when connected to non-eARC gear.

How an HDMI Audio Switcher Solves the Issue

An external HDMI audio switcher with an integrated audio extractor bypasses the TV’s limitations through direct signal splitting and management:

  1. Direct Source Connection: Media devices plug directly into the switcher's HDMI input ports instead of the television.
  2. Signal Splitting (Audio De-embedding): Inside the switcher, the inbound HDMI signal is duplicated or separated into two distinct pipelines:
    • Main HDMI Output: Carries the full video signal (alongside standard stereo or muted audio) directly to any standard HDMI input on the TV or monitor.
    • Dedicated HDMI Audio Output: Carries a dedicated, high-bandwidth audio signal—often packaged inside a blank 720p or 1080p video carrier signal—directly to the standard HDMI input on the non-eARC soundbar.
  3. EDID Emulation: The switcher utilizes Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) management. It signals to the source device (such as an Apple TV or PlayStation 5) that it is connected to a high-end audio receiver, forcing the source to output full multi-channel surround sound even if the TV itself cannot process that format.

Why This Bypasses eARC

Because the soundbar receives the audio as a direct forward-facing input rather than an audio return feed from the television, neither device requires an eARC handshake. The soundbar treats the audio signal as if it were plugged directly into an uncompressed media player, while the TV receives its native resolution and refresh rate without the burden of processing or returning the audio stream.
