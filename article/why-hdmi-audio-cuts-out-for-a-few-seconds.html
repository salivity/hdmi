Why HDMI Audio Cuts Out for a Few Seconds

Experiencing brief audio dropouts lasting one to two seconds while streaming video, gaming, or watching TV is a common frustration with HDMI setups. This issue is typically caused by digital handshake interruptions, insufficient cable bandwidth, mismatched audio output formats, HDMI-CEC interference, or outdated device firmware. By understanding how HDMI transmits audio data alongside copy-protection signals, you can quickly identify the root cause and restore a stable, uninterrupted listening experience.

HDCP Handshake Interruptions

The most frequent cause of brief, recurring audio dropouts is a renegotiation of High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP). HDMI transmits encrypted content between your source device (such as a media player, console, or PC) and your display or audio receiver. If there is a momentary sync error or slight signal degradation, the devices will re-authenticate the encryption keys. During this sub-second handshake reset, the video may remain buffered on-screen, but the digital audio stream drops out momentarily while the audio decoder re-syncs.

Subpar or Damaged HDMI Cables

High-resolution audio formats—especially uncompressed multichannel PCM, Dolby TrueHD, or Dolby Atmos—require substantial bandwidth alongside modern 4K or 8K HDR video signals. If you are using an older cable (such as an older standard HDMI instead of a certified Premium High Speed or Ultra High Speed HDMI 2.1 cable), signal degradation can cause data packets to drop. Physical stress, excessive cable length (over 15 feet without an active amplifier), or loose connections can also cause minor data loss that manifests as recurring one-to-two-second audio silences.

Audio Format and Passthrough Incompatibilities

Periodic audio loss often happens when the source device is set to an audio format that the receiving device (such as a TV, AV receiver, or soundbar) struggles to decode efficiently. When a source transmits "Bitstream" (raw encoded data) to a device that has buffer latency or limited codec support, the decoder may fall out of sync, causing a pause while it catches up. Switching the audio output setting on your source device from Bitstream to "PCM" (or disabling specific spatial audio formats like Dolby Atmos/DTS:X if not strictly needed) frequently resolves this buffer delay.

HDMI-CEC and eARC/ARC Conflicts

HDMI Consumer Electronics Control (HDMI-CEC) allows connected devices to control each other’s power and volume, but it can also send erratic polling signals across the HDMI bus. When multiple devices communicate simultaneously over HDMI-CEC, minor bus collisions can interrupt the eARC/ARC audio return path for a second. Additionally, Enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC) requires flawless handshake compatibility; a minor mismatch in how the TV and soundbar implement eARC standards can trigger periodic dropouts. Disabling CEC (often labeled Anynet+, Bravia Sync, or Simplink) or toggling eARC to standard ARC can diagnose whether this is the cause.

Hardware Power-Saving and Audio Sleep States

If the issue occurs on a PC connected to a TV or receiver via HDMI, the graphics card’s digital audio driver may be entering an ultra-low-power sleep state whenever there is a micro-pause in sound, or it may drop sync during high GPU usage spikes. On dedicated streaming devices and consoles, eco-modes or quick-start features can occasionally corrupt the HDMI controller cache over time, leading to audio stutter until a full power cycle is performed.

How to Fix HDMI Audio Dropouts

  • Replace the cable: Upgrade to a certified Ultra High Speed (48 Gbps) HDMI cable to ensure sufficient bandwidth for both high-resolution video and audio.
  • Adjust audio output formats: Set your source device to output Stereo PCM or uncompressed LPCM instead of Auto or Bitstream to test decoder stability.
  • Perform a complete power cycle: Unplug the TV, receiver, and source device from the wall outlet for two minutes to clear the HDMI hardware cache and force a clean HDCP handshake.
  • Update all firmware: Check for software updates on your television, soundbar, AV receiver, and source device, as manufacturers regularly release patches for HDMI-eARC handshaking bugs.
  • Test HDMI-CEC: Temporarily turn off HDMI-CEC on all devices to verify if background control signals are disrupting the audio transmission.
