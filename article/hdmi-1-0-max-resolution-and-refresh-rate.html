HDMI 1.0 Max Resolution and Refresh Rate

Released in December 2002, the original HDMI 1.0 specification established the foundation for modern digital audio and video interfaces in consumer electronics. This article outlines the maximum video resolution, supported refresh rates, bandwidth limitations, and core technical specifications defined under the original HDMI 1.0 standard.

Maximum Resolution and Refresh Rate

The HDMI 1.0 specification defines a maximum video resolution of 1920 × 1080 (1080p) at a 60 Hz refresh rate.

For personal computer monitors using standard blanking intervals, the standard also supports a maximum resolution of 1920 × 1200 (WUXGA) at 60 Hz.

Standard lower-resolution formats are also fully supported, including:

  • 720p (1280 × 720) at 50 Hz / 60 Hz
  • 1080i (1920 × 1080 interlaced) at 50 Hz / 60 Hz
  • 480p / 576p (Standard Definition progressive) at 50 Hz / 60 Hz

Bandwidth and Transmission Limits

The video limitations of HDMI 1.0 are dictated by its physical transmission limits:

  • Maximum Pixel Clock Rate: 165 MHz
  • Total TMDS Bandwidth: 4.95 Gbps across three data channels
  • Maximum Video Data Throughput: 3.96 Gbps (after accounting for 8b/10b encoding overhead)

Because the pixel clock is capped at 165 MHz, higher resolutions like 1440p (QHD) or 2160p (4K), as well as refresh rates exceeding 60 Hz at 1080p, cannot be transmitted over an HDMI 1.0 link.

Color Depth and Audio Specifications

HDMI 1.0 was designed around standard digital video color spaces. It supports:

  • Color Depth: Standard 24-bit color (8 bits per channel) using standard sRGB or YCbCr (4:4:4 and 4:2:2). Higher color depths ("Deep Color") and High Dynamic Range (HDR) were not introduced until later HDMI revisions.
  • Audio Capabilities: Up to 8 channels of uncompressed LPCM digital audio at sample rates up to 192 kHz with up to 24-bit resolution, alongside support for standard compressed formats such as Dolby Digital and DTS.
