HDMI 2.1b Cable Power for Active Cables

The HDMI 2.1b specification addresses power delivery for active cables through a dedicated feature called HDMI Cable Power. This standard allows compatible source devices to deliver electrical power directly through the HDMI connector to power the active processing chips inside long-distance copper or optical hybrid cables. By formalizing power delivery mechanisms, the specification eliminates the clutter of external USB power leads while establishing clear hardware, safety, and labeling standards for reliable high-bandwidth transmission.

The Need for Cable Power

Higher-bandwidth formats like 4K at 120Hz and 8K at 60Hz require data rates up to 48 Gbps. Passive copper HDMI cables can typically only sustain these speeds over short distances (usually under 3 to 5 meters). For longer runs, active cables—which use integrated circuits to amplify signals or convert electrical signals to optical pulses—are mandatory.

Historically, active cables either siphoned unregulated power from the standard 5V rail (pin 18), which was only designed to supply minimal current (55 mA) to read EDID data, or relied on cumbersome external power dongles. HDMI 2.1b standardizes source-based power delivery to solve both issues.

How HDMI Cable Power Works

HDMI 2.1b defines a standardized handshake and electrical baseline between the source and the cable:

  • Increased Current Capability: Under the HDMI Cable Power specification, compliant source devices are capable of supplying up to 300 mA at 5V on the power line. This provides sufficient wattage to run low-power optical transceivers and active equalizers embedded directly inside the cable connectors.
  • Directional Design: Because power-drawing circuitry is embedded to boost the signal from transmitter to receiver, HDMI Cable Power cables are strictly directional. One connector is explicitly labeled for the "Source" (media player, console, or PC), while the other is labeled for the "Display" (TV, monitor, or projector). Power is pulled directly from the Source end.
  • Overcurrent and Fault Protection: The specification requires sources supporting HDMI Cable Power to include overcurrent protection. If an active cable attempts to draw more current than the source can safely supply, the source limits or cuts power to prevent hardware damage.

Backwards Compatibility and Fallback

Connecting an active HDMI 2.1b cable to a non-supporting legacy source will not damage the hardware. However, because legacy sources only guarantee 55 mA, the active components within the cable may not receive sufficient power to transmit high-speed signals.

To maintain compatibility with older devices, many HDMI Cable Power cables include a secondary, optional power port (such as a USB-C or Micro-USB pigtail) at the source end. If the connected source does not support HDMI Cable Power, the user can attach a standard external USB power source to supply the necessary voltage.

Identification and Labeling Requirements

To prevent consumer confusion, the HDMI 2.1b specification mandates clear labeling:

  • Cable Packaging: Packaging must indicate that the cable utilizes HDMI Cable Power and clearly state whether the cable requires a supporting source device or includes an auxiliary power connection.
  • Connector Ends: The physical ends of the cable must be permanently marked with "Source" and "Display" to ensure the cable is inserted in the correct signal direction, ensuring the active electronics receive power from the transmitting device.
