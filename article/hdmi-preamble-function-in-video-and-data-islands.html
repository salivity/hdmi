HDMI Preamble Function in Video and Data Islands

The HDMI preamble is a specialized signaling sequence transmitted during the Control Period that immediately precedes both Video Data Periods and Data Islands across Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) channels. Its primary function is to notify the receiving display (sink) of the exact type of data traffic arriving next, allowing the hardware to switch its internal decoding architecture in advance. By providing a predictable, 8-character transition window, the preamble ensures uninterrupted frame synchronization, accurate packet processing, and error-free rendering of audio and video content.

HDMI Transmission Periods

To understand the preamble's function, it is necessary to examine how an HDMI link categorizes transmission into three main operational states:

  1. Control Period: Used for synchronization signals (such as HSync and VSync) and preamble sequences.
  2. Video Data Period: Used to transmit active video pixels.
  3. Data Island Period: Used to transmit audio samples, auxiliary data, and InfoFrames.

Because these states share the same physical TMDS data lanes, the receiver must know precisely when one state ends and another begins.

Notifying the Receiver of Upcoming Payloads

The primary function of the HDMI preamble is state indication. It consists of eight identical, consecutive control characters sent across TMDS channels 1 and 2 during the final cycles of a Control Period.

The preamble uses four control bits—CTL0, CTL1, CTL2, and CTL3—to encode the identity of the incoming transmission:

  • Video Data Period Preamble: Configured with the bit pattern CTL0, CTL1, CTL2, CTL3 = 1, 0, 0, 0. This alerts the sink that the active video stream is about to commence.
  • Data Island Period Preamble: Configured with the bit pattern CTL0, CTL1, CTL2, CTL3 = 1, 0, 1, 0. This signals that the link is transitioning into a data packet transmission carrying audio or metadata.

Enabling Real-Time Decoder Switching

HDMI employs distinct encoding methods depending on the data type being transported:

  • Video Data is encoded using standard 8b/10b TMDS algorithms to maximize bandwidth and minimize electromagnetic interference.
  • Data Islands are encoded using TERC4 (TMDS Error Reduction Coding), a 4-bit to 10-bit mapping scheme designed to provide higher error tolerance for critical data such as audio streams and HDR metadata.
  • Control Data relies on a 2b/10b mapping scheme for high-reliability transmission of synchronization pulses.

Without the preamble, the sink device would be unable to anticipate the transition between 2b/10b, TERC4, and 8b/10b decoding logic. The preamble grants the receiver the required clock cycles to reconfigure its internal demultiplexers and decoders before the actual payload bytes arrive.

Preventing Signal Corruption and Misinterpretation

Because synchronization signals like HSync and VSync continue to be evaluated during control periods, sudden payload insertions without preparation could cause the sink to interpret active video or audio bits as false sync pulses. The preamble acts as an architectural boundary, guaranteeing that the receiver safely ceases sync-pulse parsing and locks into the correct data phase, thereby preventing screen flickering, signal dropouts, and audio pop artifacts.
