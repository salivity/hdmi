What Is an HDMI Self-Testing Certificate?

An HDMI Self-Testing Certificate is a formal compliance document that allows licensed manufacturers to verify in-house that their hardware meets HDMI standards without sending every iteration to an external test lab. This article explains what the certificate is, how it functions within the HDMI compliance framework, and the specific conditions a manufacturer must meet before being permitted to self-test and self-certify their products.

Understanding the HDMI Self-Testing Certificate

To legally manufacture and sell products bearing the HDMI logo and technology, companies must ensure their hardware complies with the official HDMI Compliance Test Specification (CTS). Compliance testing guarantees interoperability, signal integrity, and protocol compliance across all HDMI-enabled devices.

While third-party Authorized Test Centers (ATCs) handle baseline certification, requiring every single device revision or model to go through an ATC would cause significant delays and high costs. The HDMI Self-Testing Certificate is the official document created and retained by an HDMI Adopter affirming that a product was internally tested using approved equipment, strictly adheres to the CTS, and has passed all necessary functional and electrical tests.

When Is a Manufacturer Allowed to Use Self-Testing?

A manufacturer cannot simply build an HDMI device and immediately self-certify it. The HDMI Licensing Administrator (HDMI LA) enforces strict prerequisites before a manufacturer is authorized to use the self-testing procedure:

1. Active HDMI Adopter Status

The manufacturer must be an official HDMI Adopter in good standing. This requires having an active HDMI Adopter Agreement and staying current on all licensing fees and royalties. Non-adopters and third-party contract manufacturers without their own adopter agreements cannot issue HDMI Self-Testing Certificates.

2. The First-Product ATC Rule

Before a manufacturer can self-test a device, they must first submit the first product of that specific category to an official HDMI Authorized Test Center (ATC) for testing.

HDMI devices are grouped into distinct categories:

  • Source: Devices that output video and audio (e.g., Blu-ray players, PCs, set-top boxes).
  • Sink: Devices that receive and display signals (e.g., TVs, monitors, projectors).
  • Repeater: Devices that receive and re-transmit signals (e.g., AV receivers, switches).
  • Cable: Passive or active HDMI cable assemblies.

For example, if an Adopter passes compliance testing at an ATC for a Source device, they unlock the ability to self-test subsequent Source devices. However, this clearance does not apply to other categories; if that same manufacturer later builds an HDMI Sink or Repeater, the first model of that new category must still be submitted to an ATC.

3. Possession of Approved Testing Equipment

To validate a Self-Testing Certificate, the Adopter must perform all tests using specialized test fixtures, oscilloscopes, protocol analyzers, and signal generators that meet the specifications laid out in the current HDMI CTS. The test procedures must mirror the exact testing regimens used by official ATCs.

4. Record-Keeping and Submission Requirements

Once internal testing yields passing results:

  • The manufacturer completes the official HDMI Self-Testing Form/Certificate.
  • The manufacturer must keep comprehensive test logs, raw data, and the certificate on file.
  • Depending on the current guidelines from HDMI LA and the specific product class, the manufacturer may be required to submit the Self-Testing Certificate and test results to HDMI LA or the ATC before placing the product on the market.

Failure to follow these protocols, or issuing false self-testing documents, exposes manufacturers to the revocation of their Adopter status, loss of rights to use HDMI trademarks, and potential legal or customs actions against uncertified hardware.
