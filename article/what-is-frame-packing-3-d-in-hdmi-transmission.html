What Is Frame Packing 3D in HDMI Transmission?

Frame Packing is a standardized transmission format introduced in the HDMI 1.4 specification designed to deliver full-resolution, stereoscopic 3D video from a source device to a display. Unlike half-resolution 3D formats that compress images horizontally or vertically, Frame Packing combines two complete, uncompressed frames—one for the left eye and one for the right eye—into a single, vertically stacked transmission frame. This process roughly doubles the vertical pixel count of the signal, ensuring that viewers receive native Full HD clarity in both eyes without sacrificing image detail.

How Frame Packing Works

In traditional half-resolution 3D methods, such as Side-by-Side (SBS) or Top-and-Bottom (TAB), a standard video frame is divided in half. For example, a 1080p frame (1920x1080) in Side-by-Side mode squeezes both views horizontally into 960x1080 per eye, which degrades image sharpness.

Frame Packing avoids this compromise by avoiding compression altogether. It takes the full left-eye frame and the full right-eye frame and arranges them in a single tall container. When an HDMI source transmits a Frame Packed 3D signal, it sends the full-resolution left-eye frame, followed by a small blanking space, followed immediately by the full-resolution right-eye frame within a single video refresh cycle.

How Frame Packing Doubles the Vertical Pixel Count

The doubling of the vertical resolution occurs directly in the video signal's transport layer. Rather than sending a standard 1080p frame of 1920x1080 pixels, the HDMI transmitter structures the signal as follows:

  1. Left Eye Frame: 1920 pixels wide by 1080 pixels high.
  2. Active Blanking Buffer: An empty separator band—typically 45 vertical blanking lines—inserted between the two images to prevent cross-talk and give the receiving hardware time to synchronize.
  3. Right Eye Frame: 1920 pixels wide by 1080 pixels high.

When these components are stacked together vertically, the total resolution of the transmitted frame becomes 1920x2205 pixels (\(1080 + 45 + 1080 = 2205\)).

Because the horizontal resolution remains 1920 pixels while the vertical line count increases from 1080 to 2205, the vertical payload is slightly more than doubled compared to a standard 2D Full HD frame.

Display Decoding

Once the 1920x2205 signal reaches a compatible 3D television or projector via HDMI, the display's onboard processor unpacks the data:

  • It strips away the 45-line blanking buffer.
  • It separates the upper 1080 lines into the left-eye image and the lower 1080 lines into the right-eye image.
  • It renders the two distinct 1920x1080 frames sequentially (for active shutter glasses) or simultaneously via polarized lines (for passive glasses).

Because the transmission pipeline delivers two discrete, uncompressed 1080p frames, Frame Packing remains the default standard for 3D Blu-ray discs and dedicated 3D gaming consoles, offering true Full HD per eye.
