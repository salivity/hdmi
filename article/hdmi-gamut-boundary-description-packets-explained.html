HDMI Gamut Boundary Description Packets Explained

HDMI Gamut Boundary Description (GBD) data packets are specialized metadata structures transmitted across HDMI links to communicate the exact color boundaries of extended-gamut video content. This article explores what GBD packets are, why they are necessary for modern wide-gamut displays, the data they carry, and the precise timing and conditions under which an HDMI source transmits them to a display.

What Are Gamut Boundary Description Packets?

Introduced in the HDMI 1.3 specification to support extended color spaces like xvYCC (Extended-gamut YCC), Gamut Boundary Description packets allow a source device (such as an Ultra HD Blu-ray player, gaming console, or media streamer) to describe the precise color gamut of the incoming video signal.

Traditional standard-gamut signals (like standard BT.709 or sRGB) have rigid, predefined color boundaries that displays map automatically. However, extended color spaces can represent colors outside standard limits. Because not all displays can reproduce the full range of wide-gamut colors natively, the display needs to know the exact boundaries used by the source material.

GBD packets provide this boundary data. Using this information, the display's color management system can accurately remap (gamut-map) the colors to match the display panel's physical capabilities without causing distortion, clipping, or unnatural saturation.

Content and Structure of GBD Packets

GBD packets are transmitted as part of HDMI Data Island packets. A GBD structure typically includes:

  • Format Flags: Indicate whether the description uses a simple range format (specifying minimum and maximum coordinates) or a complex polygonal/faceted boundary format (specifying exact color vertices).
  • Color Space Identification: Defines the reference color standard being modified or described (e.g., xvYCC601, xvYCC709).
  • Gamut Boundary Vertices: Numerical values representing the limits of the color space in terms of luminance and chromaticity coordinates.

Depending on the complexity of the boundary, a complete GBD profile may span across multiple transmission packets.

When Are GBD Packets Transmitted?

GBD packets are transmitted under specific operational conditions and during designated intervals of the HDMI stream:

  • During Extended Gamut Transmission: GBD packets are only sent when the active video content uses a wide or extended color gamut that requires external mapping information (such as when xvYCC is enabled). If standard BT.601 or BT.709 color is being transmitted, GBD packets are unnecessary and not transmitted.
  • During the Vertical Blanking Interval (VBI): Like other HDMI InfoFrames and metadata packets, GBD packets are transmitted inside the Data Island periods, which occur primarily within the vertical and horizontal blanking intervals between active video frames.
  • Repetition Rate: To ensure proper synchronization and recovery from transmission errors or hot-plug events, GBD packets are typically transmitted at least once per video field or frame. They are also retransmitted immediately whenever the source content changes its color space or gamut boundary profile.
