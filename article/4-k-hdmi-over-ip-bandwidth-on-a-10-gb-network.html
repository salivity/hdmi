4K HDMI Over IP Bandwidth on a 10Gb Network

Transmitting 4K HDMI over an IP network requires substantial throughput, with an uncompressed or visually lossless stream typically consuming between 6 Gbps and 9 Gbps on a 10-Gigabit Ethernet (10GbE) network. Because true raw 4K at standard commercial refresh rates and color formats can push past the limits of a single 10Gb connection, the exact bandwidth utilized depends heavily on the resolution, frame rate, and chroma subsampling being transmitted.

Bandwidth Calculation Formula

To determine network throughput for uncompressed video, network engineers calculate the raw data rate using the following formula:

\[\text{Bandwidth (bps)} = \text{Horizontal Pixels} \times \text{Vertical Pixels} \times \text{Frame Rate (Hz)} \times \text{Color Depth (bits per pixel)}\]

Network packet overhead, audio streams, and control data (such as USB, IR, and RS-232) typically add an additional 5% to 10% to this base payload figure.

True Uncompressed 4K Formats (Under 10 Gbps)

True uncompressed video transmits pixel-for-pixel without any encoding algorithms or latency. On a 10GbE link, uncompressed 4K is achievable under specific configurations:

  • 4K @ 30Hz (4:4:4 Chroma, 8-bit color):
    • Resolution: 3840 × 2160
    • Data rate: Approximately 5.97 Gbps of active video data.
    • Network consumption: Roughly 6.5 Gbps to 7 Gbps after Ethernet packetization.
  • 4K @ 60Hz (4:2:0 Chroma, 8-bit color):
    • Resolution: 3840 × 2160
    • Data rate: Approximately 5.97 Gbps of active video data.
    • Network consumption: Roughly 6.5 Gbps to 7 Gbps including network overhead.

Both configurations fit comfortably within the practical throughput ceiling of a 10GbE port (which tops out around 9.4 Gbps after standard Ethernet framing and protocol overhead).

The 4K @ 60Hz 4:4:4 Limitation on 10Gb Networks

Full 4K resolution running at 60Hz with 4:4:4 chroma subsampling (or full RGB) and 8-bit color produces an active video payload of roughly 11.94 Gbps. When accounting for standard HDMI clock timing and blanking intervals, the native HDMI data rate reaches approximately 18 Gbps.

Because 11.94 Gbps exceeds the physical capacity of a 10GbE link:

  • True uncompressed 4K60 4:4:4 cannot travel over a single 10Gb network link.
  • Systems designed for 10Gb AV-over-IP (such as SDVoE) apply an ultra-lightweight, visually lossless compression ratio (roughly 1.3:1 to 1.4:1) with sub-millisecond latency.
  • Under this light compression, a 4K60 4:4:4 stream consumes between 8.0 Gbps and 9.0 Gbps of actual network switch bandwidth.

Summary of Network Utilization

On a managed 10Gb switch, an uncompressed 4K30 (4:4:4) or 4K60 (4:2:0) video stream consumes around 65% to 70% of a port's capacity. For 4K60 (4:4:4), light compression is applied to keep bandwidth consumption around 85% to 90% of the available 10Gb pipeline.
