HDMI Associate Category and Distributor Rights Explained

This article explores the HDMI Associate category, a specialized licensing designation established by HDMI Licensing Administrator, Inc. (HDMI LA) for businesses involved in the sale and distribution of HDMI technology. It details what the category entails, how it differs from a standard HDMI Adopter agreement, and the specific trademark and distribution permissions it grants to distributors and resellers.

The HDMI Associate category is a licensing tier tailored for companies that distribute, resell, or retail finished HDMI-compliant products but do not manufacture, design, or brand the products themselves. While manufacturers must become full HDMI Adopters to produce hardware and pay associated royalties, distributors can join the HDMI Associate program to legally market and sell those goods with official backing.

Key Permissions Granted to Distributors

Becoming an HDMI Associate provides distributors with defined rights and legal protections when handling HDMI products sourced from licensed HDMI Adopters:

  • Use of HDMI Trademarks: Associates are granted the legal right to use official HDMI trademarks, logos, and promotional assets in their marketing collateral, packaging displays, websites, and sales materials.
  • Proof of Authenticity: Associates receive official recognition and are listed on the HDMI LA public directory. This confirms to customers, partners, and customs authorities that the distributor sells verified, compliant products.
  • Access to Marketing and Educational Resources: Members gain access to official HDMI marketing tools, technical guidelines, and compliance updates to help educate downstream customers and ensure accurate labeling.
  • Reduced Compliance Burden: Because Associates only sell products made by licensed Adopters, they are exempt from paying product royalties or submitting devices to Authorized Test Centers (ATCs), significantly reducing operational overhead.

Restrictions and Limitations

While the HDMI Associate status grants marketing and distribution permissions, it carries specific boundaries:

  • No Private Labeling: Distributors cannot apply their own brand name to an HDMI product. Applying a private label classifies the company as a manufacturer, which requires a full HDMI Adopter agreement.
  • Source Restrictions: Associates must source all HDMI products and components exclusively from licensed HDMI Adopters in good standing. Selling products from unlicensed manufacturers violates the agreement.
