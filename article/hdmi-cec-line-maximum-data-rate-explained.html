HDMI CEC Line Maximum Data Rate Explained

This article explores the technical specifications of the HDMI Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) channel, focusing specifically on its transmission speed. HDMI CEC is a dedicated single-wire serial bus that allows interconnected audiovisual devices to communicate and be controlled using a single remote control. The following sections detail the exact maximum data rate of this communication line, the reasoning behind its low-bandwidth design, and how the protocol operates at the physical layer.

Maximum Data Rate of HDMI CEC

The maximum raw data rate supported by the single-wire HDMI CEC line is approximately 417 bits per second (bps), typically cited nominally as 400 bps.

Unlike the high-speed Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) channels in an HDMI cable that carry gigabits of audio and video per second, the CEC line operates at an extremely low bandwidth. It is designed to exchange small, discrete control packets rather than media streams.

Physical Layer and Bit Timing

The CEC protocol functions over Pin 13 in a standard Type-A HDMI connector. It utilizes a single-wire, shared-bus architecture that relies on an open-drain (wire-OR) configuration with a passive pull-up resistor.

The low data rate is a direct consequence of its pulse-width modulation (PWM) timing:

  • Nominal Bit Duration: A single data bit occupies a nominal time window of 2.4 milliseconds (ms).
  • Throughput Calculation: Dividing 1 second by 2.4 ms yields approximately 416.67 bits per second.
  • Logical Encoding: A logical '0' is represented by pulling the bus low for approximately 1.5 ms and releasing it for 0.9 ms. A logical '1' is represented by pulling the line low for approximately 0.6 ms and releasing it for 1.8 ms.
  • Header and Blocks: CEC transmissions are divided into 10-bit blocks (8 bits of data plus an EOM bit and an ACK bit), preceded by a distinctive start bit that lasts 4.5 ms.

Consequently, a typical standard CEC command frame (comprising a header block and one or two data blocks) takes between 25 ms and 80 ms to transmit.

Why the CEC Data Rate Is Intentionally Low

The ultra-low data rate of the CEC line is an intentional engineering choice that serves several functions:

  • Cable Length Tolerance: Slower signals have long rise and fall times, making them far less susceptible to degradation, capacitance issues, and signal loss across long or low-grade HDMI cables.
  • Cost Efficiency: Processing a 400 bps signal requires minimal microcontroller performance and inexpensive hardware transceivers, allowing manufacturers to integrate CEC into budget consumer displays and peripherals.
  • Low Noise and Crosstalk: High-frequency signaling on Pin 13 could introduce electromagnetic interference into the adjacent high-speed video lines or the Display Data Channel (DDC). A sub-kilobit transmission speed guarantees virtually zero signal interference.
  • Adequate for Purpose: CEC messages consist purely of human-interface commands—such as "One Touch Play," "System Standby," or volume adjustments—which require very few bytes to execute successfully.
