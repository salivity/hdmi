HDMI CEC Bus Bit Timing for Zero and One Bits

The HDMI Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) protocol operates over a single-wire, open-drain serial bus using pulse-width modulation to transmit data. Every data bit is framed within a nominal 2.4 ms period that begins with a falling edge (low transition), where the duration of the low state determines whether the bit is interpreted as a logical zero or a logical one. This article outlines the specific timing values, tolerances, and sampling intervals required to encode and decode zero and one bits according to the HDMI CEC specification.

Bus Electrical Characteristics

The CEC line uses passive pull-up resistors to maintain a default idle state of +3.3V (logic high). Transmitting devices communicate by actively pulling the line to ground (logic low). Because the bus is open-drain, a low level driven by any device overrides a high level, enabling collision detection and bit-level bus arbitration.

Logical '0' Bit Timing

A logical '0' represents the dominant state on the CEC bus and is characterized by a longer low-level duration:

  • Low Duration (Nominal): 1.5 ms (valid range: 1.3 ms to 1.7 ms)
  • High Duration (Nominal): 0.9 ms
  • Total Bit Period (Nominal): 2.4 ms (valid range: 2.05 ms to 2.75 ms)

The initiator drives the bus low for 1.5 ms before releasing it to allow the pull-up resistor to return the bus to a high state for the remainder of the bit period.

Logical '1' Bit Timing

A logical '1' represents the recessive state on the CEC bus and is characterized by a shorter low-level duration:

  • Low Duration (Nominal): 0.6 ms (valid range: 0.4 ms to 0.8 ms)
  • High Duration (Nominal): 1.8 ms
  • Total Bit Period (Nominal): 2.4 ms (valid range: 2.05 ms to 2.75 ms)

The initiator pulls the line low for only 0.6 ms before releasing the bus back to the high state for the remaining 1.8 ms.

Bit Sampling Point

Receiving devices do not wait for the complete bit period to determine a bit's value. Instead, they sample the state of the line at a specific window referenced from the initial falling edge:

  • Sample Time (Nominal): 1.05 ms after the falling edge (valid window: 0.85 ms to 1.25 ms)

Because the sample window occurs between the 0.6 ms transition of a logical '1' and the 1.5 ms transition of a logical '0':

  • If the line is high at 1.05 ms, the bit is read as a 1.
  • If the line is low at 1.05 ms, the bit is read as a 0.

This timing structure allows receiving devices to reliably sample bits despite jitter and propagation delay, while enabling transmitters to detect bus collisions during header and arbitration phases.
