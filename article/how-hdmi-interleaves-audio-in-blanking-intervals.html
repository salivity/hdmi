How HDMI Interleaves Audio in Blanking Intervals

HDMI transmits multi-channel digital audio and auxiliary data across the same physical Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) lines as video without requiring dedicated audio pins. It achieves this by time-division multiplexing: active video data is prioritized during visible scan lines, while audio data is packetized and interleaved into the transmission stream during horizontal blanking intervals (HBI) and vertical blanking intervals (VBI). This article explains the underlying mechanism, packet structures, and synchronization techniques HDMI sources use to inject audio data into these idle video periods.

The Three TMDS Operating Periods

An HDMI source switches between three primary operational states across its TMDS data channels:

  1. Video Data Period: Used to transmit active video pixel data using standard 8b/10b encoding.
  2. Control Period: Used between active video and data transmissions to convey synchronization signals (HSYNC, VSYNC) and preambles using 2b/10b encoding.
  3. Data Island Period: Used exclusively during horizontal and vertical blanking intervals to transmit audio samples, auxiliary info-frames, and clock synchronization packets.

Audio cannot interrupt active video pixels. Consequently, an HDMI source must wait for an active video line to conclude before it can transmit audio data.

Framing the Data Island Period

When the source reaches an HBI (the time between the end of one scan line and the start of the next) or a VBI (the time between the end of a field/frame and the start of the next), it executes a specific protocol to switch modes:

  • Preamble: The source outputs an 8-character control sequence indicating that a Data Island Period is about to begin.
  • Leading Guard Band: A 2-character sequence ensures the TMDS receivers can reliably detect the transition from control signals to data.
  • Packet Transmission: Audio packets are transmitted using TERC4 (TMDS Error Reduction Coding 4-bit) encoding, which maps 4-bit nibbles to 10-bit symbols. This encoding offers higher error tolerance than standard video encoding.
  • Trailing Guard Band: A 2-character sequence signals the end of the Data Island, allowing the source to transition back to control signals or prepare for the next Video Data Period.

Audio Packet Structure and Multiplexing

Inside a Data Island Period, audio data is formatted into discrete 32-byte packets, supported by an additional 4-byte header:

  • Header: A 24-bit field plus 8 bits of BCH Error Correction Code (ECC) parity data defining the packet type (e.g., Audio Sample Packet or Audio Clock Regeneration Packet).
  • Payload: Divided into four 7-byte subpackets, each protected by an 8-bit ECC parity code. Each subpacket holds up to one audio frame (L/R pairs) for uncompressed LPCM, or segments of a compressed IEC 61937 bitstream.

A source can fit multiple audio packets into a single blanking interval, provided the duration of the blanking interval exceeds the overhead required for the preambles, guard bands, and packet boundaries.

Audio Clock Regeneration (ACR)

Audio sample rates (such as 48 kHz or 96 kHz) are rarely integer multiples of video pixel clocks (such as 148.5 MHz for 1080p). To reconstruct the audio clock at the sink without introducing jitter or drift, the source interleaves Audio Clock Regeneration (ACR) packets alongside Audio Sample Packets.

ACR packets contain two values:

  • \(N\): A predefined integer value based on the audio sample frequency.
  • \(CTS\) (Cycle Time Stamp): A counter dynamically measured by the source indicating the number of video pixel clock cycles that elapsed during a specific number of audio clock cycles.

The receiving sink uses \(N\) and \(CTS\) in a Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) to synthesize the original audio clock independently of the pixel clock.

Buffer Management Across Blanking Intervals

Because audio input to the HDMI transmitter is continuous, but blanking intervals are periodic, the HDMI source relies on an internal First-In, First-Out (FIFO) buffer.

During the Video Data Period, incoming audio samples accumulate in the FIFO. As soon as the line ends and the HBI begins, the HDMI transmitter empties the FIFO by assembling and transmitting as many Audio Sample Packets as the horizontal blanking budget permits.

Because the HBI is relatively short, it handles small bursts of samples to prevent the source FIFO from overflowing. The VBI, which spans several complete line durations, provides a substantially larger window, allowing the transmitter to balance the buffer and transmit larger auxiliary payloads, such as multichannel Audio InfoFrames and high-bandwidth metadata.
