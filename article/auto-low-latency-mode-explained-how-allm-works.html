Auto Low Latency Mode Explained: How ALLM Works

Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM) is an HDMI feature designed to streamline the gaming experience by automatically switching a display into its lowest input lag setting. This article explains what ALLM is, how it communicates with an HDMI-connected television, the benefits it provides to gamers, and the basic hardware requirements needed to take advantage of it.

Auto Low Latency Mode is an automated handshake protocol introduced primarily with the HDMI 2.1 specification, though it can also be implemented on some HDMI 2.0 devices. Its primary function is to eliminate the need for users to manually navigate their television's settings menus to enable "Game Mode" whenever they launch a video game.

When a compatible source device—such as a PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, or a modern PC graphics card—launches a game, it sends a specific data signal over the HDMI cable to the television. This signal notifies the TV that gaming content is active. Upon receiving this instruction, the television immediately and automatically switches to its designated low-latency profile.

During standard viewing, modern televisions apply extensive post-processing to incoming video signals, including motion smoothing, dynamic contrast enhancement, edge sharpening, and noise reduction. While these features enhance the picture quality of movies and TV shows, processing them takes time—often adding 50 to 100 milliseconds of input lag. When the television receives an ALLM signal, it bypasses these image-processing pipelines. This reduces input lag down to single or low double-digit milliseconds, ensuring that controller inputs register almost instantly on the screen.

ALLM operates dynamically. When you close a game and switch to a movie, streaming application, or media disc on the same console, the source device drops the ALLM signal. The television immediately switches out of Game Mode and restores its full picture-processing suite without requiring any user intervention.

To use Auto Low Latency Mode, both the transmitting source device and the receiving display must explicitly support ALLM. The feature must also be enabled within the settings of both devices, typically found in the video output settings of the console and the external device or HDMI input settings of the television.
