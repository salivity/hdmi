Why PS4 VR Processor Box Blocks HDR Pass-Through

When Sony released the original PlayStation VR (model CUH-ZVR1), players discovered that routing their console through the required external processor unit completely disabled High Dynamic Range (HDR) on their televisions. This article breaks down the technical limitations responsible for this restriction, focusing on the processor unit's internal HDMI chipset, the missing metadata protocols, and how Sony ultimately addressed the issue in later hardware revisions.

The fundamental reason the original PSVR processor box blocks HDR is a hardware limitation within its internal HDMI pass-through circuitry. The first-generation breakout unit (CUH-ZVR1) was developed before the HDMI 2.0a standard became the established baseline for consumer 4K HDR displays. While the processor unit can pass a 4K resolution signal at 60Hz using a YUV 4:2:0 color format, its internal interface lacks the hardware capability to transmit the static metadata required by HDR10.

HDR content requires more than raw bandwidth; it depends on specific metadata packets defined under the HDMI 2.0a specification (specifically the CTA-861.3 standard). These packets inform the television how to adjust brightness, contrast, and color mapping on a frame-by-frame or scene-by-scene basis. Because the chipset inside the original processor unit is only rated for base-level HDMI 2.0 specifications without the "a" revision's metadata pipeline, it cannot recognize or forward this HDR information. Consequently, the console detects that the receiving device in the chain cannot handle HDR, prompting the PS4 or PS4 Pro to disable the feature and output a standard dynamic range (SDR) signal instead.

This limitation is exacerbated by the active role the processor unit plays in the setup. The breakout box is not a simple passive switch or HDMI splitter. It actively deconstructs and manages video and audio signals to generate the "Social Screen" output for the television while simultaneously feeding the VR headset and processing object-based 3D audio. Because all video passing to the TV must route through this active internal pipeline, any data standard unsupported by the box’s silicon is stripped out before the signal reaches the display.

Sony acknowledged this limitation and resolved it with the release of the revised PlayStation VR model (CUH-ZVR2) in late 2017. The updated version features a redesigned processor box equipped with an upgraded HDMI pass-through controller capable of forwarding HDR10 metadata directly to compatible displays, allowing users to play traditional HDR games without manually swapping HDMI cables.
