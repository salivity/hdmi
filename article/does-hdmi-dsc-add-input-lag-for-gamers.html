Does HDMI DSC Add Input Lag for Gamers?

Display Stream Compression (DSC) allows modern graphics cards and displays to reach high resolutions and ultra-fast refresh rates over HDMI without sacrificing visual fidelity. While any video processing technically introduces theoretical overhead, enabling DSC over HDMI does not produce a perceptible increase in overall input latency for gamers. The encoding and decoding process occurs in microseconds, making the added delay imperceptible and effectively zero in practical gaming scenarios.

How Display Stream Compression Works

Display Stream Compression is a VESA standard integrated into modern video interfaces, including HDMI 2.1 and DisplayPort 1.4/2.1. Unlike traditional video codecs like H.264 or HEVC—which analyze multiple full frames to compress data—DSC is a real-time, line-based compression standard.

It compresses the video signal line-by-line as it is transmitted from the GPU to the display's scaler. Because the display does not need to buffer entire frames to decompress the image, the processing happens on-the-fly inside dedicated hardware blocks on both the graphics card and the monitor's display controller.

The True Latency Added by DSC

The latency introduced by DSC's hardware encoder and decoder is measured in microseconds (millionths of a second), not milliseconds (thousandths of a second).

On average, DSC processing adds between 0.5 to 5 microseconds of total latency—equivalent to roughly one to two scanlines of image data. To put this in perspective:

  • 1 millisecond equals 1,000 microseconds.
  • At 144Hz, a single frame takes approximately 6.94 milliseconds to display.
  • At 240Hz, a single frame takes approximately 4.16 milliseconds to display.
  • A standard 1,000Hz gaming mouse has a polling delay of 1 millisecond.

The microsecond delay of DSC represents less than 0.1% of a single frame's display duration. It is completely drowned out by standard hardware variance, display panel response times, and peripheral input latency.

Why Enabling DSC Can Actually Reduce Overall Latency

Rather than increasing input lag, enabling DSC often leads to lower overall system latency.

High-resolution panels (such as 4K) require immense bandwidth. Without DSC, achieving high refresh rates over an HDMI connection might require dropping the color output to 4:2:2 or 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, or capping the refresh rate to a lower value (e.g., 60Hz or 120Hz instead of 144Hz or 240Hz).

Higher refresh rates drastically cut end-to-end latency:

  • Moving from 60Hz to 144Hz reduces display latency by roughly 10 milliseconds.
  • Moving from 120Hz to 240Hz reduces frame display time by over 4 milliseconds.

Because DSC provides the necessary bandwidth headroom to run panels at their native 10-bit or 12-bit RGB color alongside their maximum refresh rates, the net reduction in frame delivery time vastly outweighs the sub-millisecond overhead of the compression algorithm.

The Bottom Line

Enabling DSC over an HDMI connection does not increase overall input latency in any meaningful way. Competitive and casual gamers can safely enable DSC to take full advantage of high refresh rates, high dynamic range (HDR), and uncompromised color accuracy without worrying about input lag.
