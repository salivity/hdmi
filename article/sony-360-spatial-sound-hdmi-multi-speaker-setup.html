Sony 360 Spatial Sound: HDMI Multi-Speaker Setup

Sony’s 360 Spatial Sound Mapping technology leverages modern HDMI connections to transform living spaces into fully immersive acoustic environments, regardless of speaker layout. By using high-bandwidth HDMI eARC/ARC to transmit uncompressed object-based audio from source devices to a central processor, the system measures the physical positions of connected speakers and synthesizes multiple "phantom" speakers to fill in acoustic gaps. This article explains the technical role of HDMI audio in this ecosystem, how calibration compensates for asymmetrical speaker placement, and how signal processing renders a cohesive 360-degree soundstage.

High-Bandwidth HDMI Audio Delivery

The foundation of Sony’s 360 Spatial Sound Mapping (360 SSM) is the HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) connection. Immersive, object-based formats like Dolby Atmos, DTS:X, and Sony's proprietary 360 Reality Audio require significantly more bandwidth than traditional optical or standard ARC connections can support.

HDMI eARC provides up to 37 Mbps of bandwidth, enabling the transmission of:

  • Uncompressed 5.1 and 7.1 Linear PCM audio.
  • Lossless object-based bitstreams (such as Dolby TrueHD with Atmos and DTS-HD Master Audio with DTS:X).
  • Dynamic spatial metadata detailing where individual sounds should exist in three-dimensional space.

The HDMI connection routes this data directly to the central hub, A/V receiver (such as the Sony STR-AN series), or soundbar control box (such as the HT-A9 system), where the spatial decoding takes place.

Spatial Calibration and Non-Standard Placement

Traditional surround sound setups rely on rigid, symmetrical speaker placement to deliver accurate directional audio. Sony 360 Spatial Sound eliminates this requirement through built-in calibration microphones and Digital Cinema Auto Calibration (D.C.A.C. IX / Sound Field Optimization).

  1. Acoustic Measurement: The central unit sends test tones through each speaker connected via HDMI-routed channels or wireless links tied to the main HDMI hub.
  2. Triangulation: Dual microphones measure the distance, angle, and height of each physical speaker relative to the room and the listening position.
  3. Digital Compensation: The system calculates delay, volume balance, and frequency response to account for irregular distances—such as one surround speaker being closer to the couch or positioned higher on a shelf than another.

Generating Phantom Speakers

Once the processor receives the spatial metadata via HDMI and knows the precise layout of the physical speakers, it activates the 360 Spatial Sound Mapping algorithm.

Instead of routing specific channels strictly to physical speaker drivers, the processor splits and phases the audio waves among the physical speakers. By manipulating sound wave arrival times and frequency curves, it generates "phantom" speakers in empty spaces—such as directly above, between, or behind the physical units. For instance, a four-speaker setup can synthesize up to twelve virtual speakers, creating an expansive dome of sound that compensates entirely for uneven physical placement.

Acoustic Center Sync via HDMI

HDMI connections also facilitate Sony's "Acoustic Center Sync" feature. When an HDMI-connected BRAVIA television is paired with a compatible 360 Spatial Sound system, an auxiliary connection (often integrated alongside the HDMI handshake) allows the TV's internal speakers to act as the dedicated center channel.

The HDMI interface ensures lip-sync synchronization while the processor integrates the TV's screen-based sound directly into the 360-degree map. Dialogue appears to originate directly from the characters' mouths on screen, while ambient effects and soundtrack elements are delegated to the synthesized soundfield across the room.
