HDMI eARC Differential Audio Channel Data Rate

This article provides a technical breakdown of the data transmission capabilities of the HDMI Enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC). It details the exact raw bit rate of the single differential signaling pair, the coding mechanism used to transmit high-resolution digital audio, and how this raw bandwidth translates into support for modern, uncompressed surround sound formats.

The raw data rate of the differential audio channel inside an HDMI eARC connection is 98.304 Mbps (megabits per second).

Physical Layer and Signaling Architecture

Unlike traditional HDMI audio that rides alongside video across TMDS (Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling) or FRL (Fixed Rate Link) data lanes, eARC operates independently over a single, dedicated differential pair. This connection repurposes two specific pins on the HDMI connector: Pin 14 (Utility) and Pin 19 (Hot Plug Detect).

To transmit data across this single pair, eARC utilizes Biphase Mark Code (BMC) modulation. BMC is a self-clocking encoding scheme where each data bit contains at least one signal transition, embedding the clock directly into the bitstream. While BMC ensures synchronization and low jitter without requiring a separate clock line, it requires twice the frequency bandwidth of the underlying data signal.

Raw Bit Rate vs. Effective Audio Bandwidth

While the physical layer transmits at a raw rate of 98.304 Mbps, the usable audio payload capacity is approximately 37 Mbps to 38 Mbps. The difference between the raw bit rate and the usable throughput is consumed by:

  • BMC Encoding Overhead: The 1-to-2 symbol expansion fundamental to biphase modulation.
  • Protocol Framing: IEC 60958/IEC 61937 packet structures, preamble markers, parity bits, and channel status metadata.
  • eARC Common-Mode Data Channel: Bidirectional discovery, capability exchange, and heartbeat signaling that operate concurrently alongside the differential audio stream.

Audio Format Capabilities

The ~37 Mbps effective audio throughput enabled by the 98.304 Mbps raw signal represents a massive upgrade over the original HDMI ARC specification, which capped bandwidth at roughly 1 Mbps. This data rate easily handles:

  • Uncompressed LPCM: Up to 8 channels (7.1 surround) of 192 kHz, 24-bit uncompressed audio.
  • Lossless Object-Based Formats: Bitstreams including Dolby TrueHD (with Dolby Atmos metadata) and DTS-HD Master Audio (with DTS:X metadata).
  • High-Sample-Rate Stereo: 2-channel linear PCM up to 192 kHz at 24 bits.
