SBTM for HDR Gaming and SDR Overlays Explained

Source-Based Tone Mapping (SBTM) is an HDMI 2.1a feature that allows a source device, such as a PC or gaming console, to handle tone mapping instead of relying entirely on the display. When mixing High Dynamic Range (HDR) gaming graphics with Standard Dynamic Range (SDR) chat overlays, SBTM ensures that both types of content are rendered accurately without clipping, color distortion, or unnatural brightness shifts. This article explains how SBTM works and why it resolves the long-standing visual issues associated with combining HDR gameplay and SDR user interfaces.

The Problem with Mixed HDR and SDR Content

In standard HDR setups, the display handles tone mapping based on the overall signal it receives. When a system blends an SDR element—such as a Discord overlay, Twitch stream chat, or system notification—on top of an HDR game, the display cannot easily differentiate between the two.

Without SBTM, the display applies a single tone mapping curve across the entire frame. This typically leads to two major issues:

  • Blown-out SDR overlays: SDR text and icons, normally designed for a peak luminance of 100 to 200 nits, can be boosted to blinding HDR brightness levels, causing them to clip, lose detail, or distract the player.
  • Washed-out HDR graphics: If the source device artificially compresses the entire signal to accommodate the SDR overlay, the HDR game content can lose its dynamic range, resulting in dull highlights and muddy shadow details.

How SBTM Solves the Issue

SBTM shifts the tone mapping responsibility from the display back to the source device (the GPU or console). The display communicates its exact luminance and color volume capabilities to the source via HDMI metadata. With this information, the source device performs tone mapping directly before outputting the final video stream.

When rendering mixed content, the source device can process each visual layer independently:

  1. Targeted Luminance for Overlays: The GPU maps the SDR chat overlay strictly within standard SDR color and luminance targets. Text remains crisp, readable, and comfortable to view.
  2. Preserved Dynamic Range for Games: The GPU simultaneously maps the HDR game world to match the display’s maximum peak brightness and color gamut. Highlights like explosions and sunlight retain their intended impact without being muted.
  3. Unified Composition: The source combines these distinctly mapped elements into a single, fully optimized HDMI frame tailored specifically for that exact display.

Eliminating Display-Side Guesswork

Traditional tone mapping algorithms inside TVs and monitors rely on dynamic contrast adjustments that react to scene changes. A bright SDR chat window popping up on screen could trigger the display to dim the entire picture, including the HDR game underneath.

SBTM eliminates this display-side guesswork. Because the image arriving at the display is already fully tone-mapped to the panel's physical limits, the display does not need to apply aggressive, unpredictable dynamic tone mapping. The result is a stable, consistent viewing experience where chat overlays remain legible without compromising the immersion and visual fidelity of HDR gaming.
