Can HDMI 2.1 Drive 10K 120Hz with DSC Compression?

The HDMI 2.1 specification officially supports driving a 10K resolution display at 120Hz by utilizing Display Stream Compression (DSC 1.2a). While transmitting an uncompressed 10K signal at high refresh rates far exceeds the physical bandwidth limitations of the standard, the integration of visually lossless DSC enables the interface to deliver the necessary data throughput. This capability is primarily targeted at commercial, medical, and specialized industrial digital signage rather than standard consumer desktop monitors.

Bandwidth Constraints of HDMI 2.1

HDMI 2.1 introduces Fixed Rate Link (FRL) technology, which raises the physical transmission ceiling from the 18 Gbps found in HDMI 2.0 to a maximum raw bandwidth of 48 Gbps (with an effective usable data rate of approximately 42.6 Gbps after encoding overhead).

Driving high-resolution displays requires immense bandwidth:

  • Uncompressed 4K at 120Hz (10-bit color): ~40 Gbps (fits within the 48 Gbps limit).
  • Uncompressed 8K at 60Hz (10-bit color): ~80 Gbps (exceeds the 48 Gbps limit).
  • Uncompressed 10K at 120Hz (10-bit color): Approximately 140 to 175 Gbps, depending on blanking intervals and color formats.

Without compression, HDMI 2.1 cannot carry a 10K signal at 120Hz.

The Role of Display Stream Compression (DSC)

To overcome physical wire limits, the HDMI Forum integrated VESA Display Stream Compression (DSC) 1.2a into the HDMI 2.1 standard. DSC is an ultra-low-latency, visually lossless compression algorithm capable of compression ratios up to 3:1 or higher.

When DSC is applied:

  • A 10K signal requiring roughly 140–160 Gbps can be compressed down to approximately 40–48 Gbps.
  • The compressed data stream fits within the 48 Gbps FRL6 transmission pipeline of HDMI 2.1.
  • Decompression occurs in real-time inside the display hardware with virtually zero perceptible latency or visual artifacting.

Defining "10K" Under HDMI 2.1

In the context of the HDMI 2.1 specification, "10K" typically refers to an ultrawide aspect ratio rather than standard 16:9:

  • Resolution: 10240 × 4320 pixels (21:9 aspect ratio).
  • Pixel Count: Approximately 44.2 million pixels per frame.

While standard 16:9 10K (10240 × 5760) would exceed the data budget even with standard 3:1 DSC unless aggressive chroma subsampling (such as 4:2:0) or higher compression profiles are used, 10240 × 4320 at 120Hz fits within the specification parameters when utilizing DSC 1.2a with standard 10-bit color depths.

Practical Implementation

Although the HDMI 2.1 specification officially accounts for 10K at 120Hz with DSC, real-world deployment requires end-to-end hardware compliance:

  1. Source Hardware: The GPU or media player must support HDMI 2.1 FRL6 (48 Gbps) and have an encoder capable of handling DSC at 10K pixel clocks.
  2. Cables: Ultra High Speed HDMI cables certified for 48 Gbps are mandatory to maintain signal integrity.
  3. Display Hardware: The target display must house a scaler and timing controller (TCON) equipped with DSC decoders capable of rebuilding a 10K 120Hz image in real time.

In conclusion, an HDMI 2.1 connection can drive a 10K display at 120Hz, provided that Display Stream Compression is active and the resolution targets the specified 10240 × 4320 format.
