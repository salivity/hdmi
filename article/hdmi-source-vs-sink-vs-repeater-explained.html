HDMI Source vs Sink vs Repeater Explained

Understanding the roles of an HDMI source, an HDMI sink, and an HDMI repeater is essential for configuring and troubleshooting modern audio/video systems. In an HDMI ecosystem, the "source" generates the digital signal, the "sink" receives and displays it, and the "repeater" sits between them to process, split, or regenerate the data stream. Recognizing how these three components interact helps ensure proper signal transmission, correct resolution settings, and successful HDCP (High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection) handshakes across your devices.

What Is an HDMI Source?

An HDMI source is the originating point of an audio and video signal. It outputs digital content via an HDMI cable toward another device. The source reads capability data from connected devices to determine which resolutions, refresh rates, and audio formats it should output.

Common examples of HDMI sources include:

  • Gaming consoles (PlayStation, Xbox)
  • Blu-ray and DVD players
  • Streaming devices (Apple TV, Roku, Fire TV Stick)
  • Desktop computers and laptops
  • Cable and satellite set-top boxes

What Is an HDMI Sink?

An HDMI sink is the final destination for an audio and video signal. Its primary function is to receive the digital data transmitted by the source and render it into visible light and audible sound. The term "sink" originates from graph theory and electrical engineering, representing a node where signal or energy terminates.

The sink stores and provides EDID (Extended Display Identification Data) to the source. This data informs the source of the display’s native resolution, color space capabilities, refresh rates, and supported audio formats.

Common examples of HDMI sinks include:

  • Televisions
  • Computer monitors
  • Projectors

What Is an HDMI Repeater?

An HDMI repeater is an intermediary device that receives an HDMI signal, processes or retransmits it, and sends it along to one or more sinks or downstream repeaters.

Unlike a simple passive cable, an active repeater participates directly in the HDMI communication chain. It acts as a sink to the upstream source device (receiving the incoming signal and providing an EDID profile) and simultaneously acts as a source to the downstream sink device (transmitting the signal forward). Repeaters often extract audio, boost weak signals over long distances, switch between multiple inputs, or split a single input into multiple outputs.

Common examples of HDMI repeaters include:

  • Audio/Video (AV) receivers
  • Soundbars with HDMI passthrough
  • HDMI splitters and matrix switchers
  • Active HDMI signal boosters and extenders

Summary of Differences

  • Direction of Flow: The source only outputs data, the sink only receives data, and the repeater both receives and transmits data.
  • EDID Communication: The sink provides EDID; the source reads EDID; the repeater manages, modifies, or passes through EDID between both ends.
  • Content Protection (HDCP): The source encrypts data, the sink decrypts it for rendering, and the repeater decrypts incoming data to process it before re-encrypting it for downstream devices.
