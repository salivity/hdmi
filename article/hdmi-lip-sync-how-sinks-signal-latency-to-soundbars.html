HDMI Lip-Sync: How Sinks Signal Latency to Soundbars

This article explains how an HDMI sink device, such as a smart television, communicates its internal processing delays to a soundbar or audio receiver to eliminate lip-sync discrepancies. You will learn about the signaling pathways defined by the HDMI specification, including static Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) declarations, dynamic eARC communication channels, and HDMI-CEC timing commands that enable audio hardware to delay playback in exact harmony with the display's video pipeline.

The Source of Lip-Sync Discrepancies

Modern televisions incorporate intensive image processing pipelines—such as motion smoothing, upscaling, tone mapping, and local dimming—that introduce significant processing delay (often between 30 and 150 milliseconds). Audio decoding and digital-to-analog conversion require far less time. Without explicit coordination, the soundbar plays audio immediately, resulting in the audio leading the video. To correct this, the TV (the HDMI sink) must accurately measure its own video latency and convey that parameter to the soundbar so the soundbar can buffer and delay the audio accordingly.

Static Latency Signaling via EDID

The foundational mechanism for automatic synchronization was introduced in HDMI 1.3 as Auto Lip-Sync. Under this specification, the television exposes its fixed latency values through its Extended Display Identification Data (EDID), specifically within the HDMI Vendor-Specific Data Block (VSDB).

The sink populates dedicated fields within the VSDB:

  • Video Latency: The processing time required for progressive video formats.
  • Audio Latency: The internal audio processing time for the TV's built-in speakers.
  • Interlaced Video Latency: Processing delays specific to interlaced video signals.
  • Interlaced Audio Latency: Audio delays applied during interlaced playback.

These values are expressed as single-byte values representing delay in milliseconds according to a standardized formula:

\[\text{Delay (ms)} = (\text{Value} - 1) \times 2\]

When a soundbar or source reads the TV's EDID over the Display Data Channel (DDC), it extracts these static offsets and applies an identical delay to the outbound audio stream.

Dynamic Lip-Sync over HDMI eARC

Static EDID fields cannot account for modern displays whose processing time fluctuates depending on the active picture preset (such as switching from a high-latency Cinema Mode to a low-latency Game Mode) or varying frame rates (Variable Refresh Rate/VRR).

HDMI 2.1 solves this through Enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC) Dynamic Lip-Sync. Rather than relying solely on static EDID tables, eARC establishes an independent, out-of-band communication link known as the eARC Data Channel (eADC).

  1. Capabilities Exchange: When connected, the television and soundbar exchange their Capabilities Data Structure (CDS) over the eADC.
  2. Continuous Latency Updates: The sink actively monitors its internal rendering pipeline. If the picture mode changes or input lag fluctuates, the sink sends an updated latency message through the eADC.
  3. Adaptive Buffering: The soundbar continuously receives these microsecond-accurate timing offsets and dynamically increases or decreases its internal FIFO (First-In, First-Out) audio buffer without causing audible pitch shifts or audio dropouts.

HDMI-CEC Latency Coordination

In standard Audio Return Channel (ARC) connections or setups utilizing older HDMI versions, the Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) bus provides a secondary protocol for synchronization.

Through the CEC channel, devices utilize the following operational commands:

  • <Request Audio Latency>: Sent by an audio renderer (soundbar) or upstream source to query the sink's current delay parameters.
  • <Report Audio Latency>: Transmitted by the sink back to the audio device, carrying a payload that specifies the total rendering delay currently experienced by the video engine.

By combining physical-layer discovery through EDID, bus-level polling via HDMI-CEC, and continuous low-latency data channel packets across eARC, HDMI sink devices ensure soundbars maintain an aligned audio buffer that matches display latency in real time.
