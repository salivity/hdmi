HDMI Compliance Testing Electrical Parameters

HDMI compliance testing at an Authorized Test Center (ATC) verifies that devices meet the strict physical-layer and signal-integrity standards defined in the HDMI Compliance Test Specification (CTS). This article outlines the specific electrical parameters measured across HDMI sources, sinks, and cables—focusing on high-speed signaling, impedance, jitter, and low-speed interface characteristics required for official certification.

TMDS and FRL Differential Voltage Levels

HDMI relies on Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) for legacy modes and Fixed Rate Link (FRL) for higher bandwidths like HDMI 2.1. ATCs evaluate the differential voltage swings to ensure transmitters do not overdrive or underdrive the transmission line:

  • Differential Output Voltage (\(V_{swing}\)): Measures the peak-to-peak amplitude of the differential signal lines (Clock, D0, D1, D2, and additional FRL lanes).
  • Single-Ended Output High/Low Levels (\(V_H, V_L\)): Validates the absolute DC levels relative to the \(3.3\text{V}\) nominal termination voltage (\(V_{term}\)).
  • Overshoot and Undershoot: Evaluates signal ringing beyond steady-state levels to prevent long-term component degradation and electromagnetic interference (EMI).

Rise and Fall Times

Edge rates must be fast enough to support target data rates while maintaining control over high-frequency radiation. Testing centers measure:

  • 20% to 80% Transition Durations: Evaluates the transition times between the 20% and 80% voltage thresholds on rising and falling edges.
  • Edge Rate Matching: Ensures symmetric transitions between rising and falling edges to prevent duty cycle distortion.

Eye Diagram and Jitter Analysis

Eye diagram compliance tests evaluate overall signal integrity under dynamic operating conditions:

  • Eye Mask Margins: Transmitted signals are sampled using high-bandwidth oscilloscopes to ensure the waveform does not breach standard eye mask boundaries at specific reference points (e.g., TP1 or through cable emulation filters at TP2).
  • Total Jitter (\(TJ\)): Evaluated at a defined Bit Error Ratio (BER), typically \(10^{-9}\) or \(10^{-12}\).
  • Deterministic Jitter (\(DJ\)) and Random Jitter (\(RJ\)): Separates data-dependent jitter (DDJ) and duty cycle distortion from unbounded Gaussian noise.
  • Clock Jitter: For TMDS modes, clock channel period jitter and cycle-to-cycle jitter are measured.

Skew Measurements

Timing synchronization between differential lines and channels is critical for proper clock recovery and symbol decoding:

  • Intra-Pair Skew: The time delay difference between the positive and negative traces of a single differential pair (e.g., \(D0+\) and \(D0-\)).
  • Inter-Pair Skew: The propagation delay variance across different data pairs and between data pairs and the clock pair.

Impedance and Return Loss (TDR/ENA)

ATCs employ Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) and Vector Network Analyzers (VNA/ENA) to assess the passive transmission path:

  • Differential Characteristic Impedance: The target nominal impedance is \(100\,\Omega \pm 15\%\), with allowable limits widening across connector interfaces for brief durations.
  • Single-Ended Impedance: Nominal \(50\,\Omega\) characteristic impedance per line.
  • Differential Return Loss (\(SDD11 / SDD22\)): Measures the reflected signal power caused by impedance discontinuities.
  • Far-End Crosstalk (FEXT): Evaluates noise coupling between adjacent differential pairs inside cables and connector assemblies.

Receiver (Sink) Sensitivity and Tolerance

Sink testing evaluates how well an HDMI receiver detects degraded signals:

  • Minimum Differential Sensitivity: Tests the receiver’s ability to decode signals with minimum allowable voltage amplitudes.
  • Jitter Tolerance: Injects controlled amounts of random, sinusoidal, and deterministic jitter into the data stream to ensure the receiver maintains the specified BER.

Auxiliary Lines and DC Power Verification

In addition to high-speed lines, control and power circuits are verified:

  • +5V Power Line: Measures voltage output under load (typically \(4.8\text{V}\) to \(5.3\text{V}\) delivering at least \(55\text{mA}\)) and verifies overcurrent protection limits.
  • Hot Plug Detect (HPD): Assesses high and low voltage thresholds and input impedance.
  • DDC and CEC Electrical Characteristics: Tests I2C-based Display Data Channel (DDC) and Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) lines for bus capacitance, pull-up resistance values, output drive capability, and voltage rise/fall times.
