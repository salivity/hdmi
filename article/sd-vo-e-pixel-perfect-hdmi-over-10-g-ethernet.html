SDVoE: Pixel-Perfect HDMI Over 10G Ethernet

The Software Defined Video Over Ethernet (SDVoE) standard delivers pixel-for-pixel video transmission by leveraging standard 10 Gigabit Ethernet (10GbE) network switches and advanced PHY technology. This article examines the technical architecture behind SDVoE, explaining how it transmits uncompressed HDMI signals within a 10 Gbps envelope, utilizes zero-frame-latency line-based processing, and manages high-bandwidth formats without introducing visual artifacts or latency.

The 10Gbps Bandwidth Envelope

HDMI 2.0 supports total data rates up to 18 Gbps, but many common video resolutions operate well within the capacity of a standard 10GbE network. SDVoE takes advantage of the fact that uncompressed 1080p60, 4K30 (4:4:4 8-bit), and 4K60 (4:2:0 8-bit) require bandwidths between roughly 3 Gbps and 9 Gbps.

Because standard 10G Ethernet provides an effective raw data throughput of 10.3125 Gbps (with line coding), SDVoE transports these formats completely uncompressed. The raw digital pixel data from the HDMI receiver passes directly to the network payload without discarding color information or applying mathematical transform algorithms.

Direct Ethernet Packetization via Dedicated ASICs

At the core of SDVoE hardware is dedicated silicon (most notably Semtech’s BlueRiver technology). Unlike traditional AV-over-IP architectures that encode video using H.264, H.265, or heavy JPEG codecs, the SDVoE chipset does not employ a traditional frame buffer.

Instead, it converts the native HDMI Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or DisplayPort packets directly into standard Ethernet frames in real time:

  1. Clock Synchronization: SDVoE incorporates Precision Time Protocol (IEEE 1588 PTP) to establish sub-microsecond synchronization across all transmitters and receivers. This eliminates frame dropping, tearing, or the need for multi-frame buffer delays.
  2. Audio and Control Decoupling: Video data, multichannel audio, USB, and control signals (IR, RS-232) are packetized into independent network streams. This architecture ensures audio and metadata routing do not interfere with raw video throughput.

Handling Full-Bandwidth 4K60 4:4:4

When a source outputs 4K60 at 4:4:4 8-bit or HDR formats (which demand roughly 12 to 18 Gbps), the signal exceeds the 10 Gbps boundary of the physical Ethernet interface. In these scenarios, SDVoE maintains pixel-level fidelity using a proprietary, ultra-lightweight line-based compression engine.

  • Line-Based Processing: Rather than buffering entire frames to calculate inter-frame motion (temporal compression), SDVoE compresses video on a line-by-line basis. The latency introduced by this process is measured in microseconds (less than 100 microseconds), which is less than a single video line and imperceptible in real-time applications.
  • Low Compression Ratio: The compression ratio required to fit an 18 Gbps stream into a 10G pipe is tiny—roughly 1.3:1 to 1.5:1. Because the compression is so light, the mathematical reconstruction on the receiver side preserves native color edges and high-frequency details (such as single-pixel CAD lines and fine text) without ringing, macroblocking, or color smearing.

Standardization on Commodity 10G Switching Fabrics

SDVoE avoids proprietary matrix hardware by utilizing standard, commercially available 10GbE enterprise network switches. By pairing standard Layer 2 and Layer 3 IP networking protocols (specifically IGMP snooping for efficient multicast distribution) with dedicated hardware-level packet conversion, SDVoE bridges consumer and professional HDMI specifications directly onto IT networks, achieving true pixel parity with standard HDMI cables.
