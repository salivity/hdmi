HDMI 5V Pin Maximum Current Draw Explained

This article examines the power delivery specifications of the HDMI interface, focusing specifically on the Pin 18 (+5V Power) line. It details the exact current limits established by the HDMI specification for both source and sink devices, outlines the mandatory overcurrent protection thresholds, and explains the operational risks associated with powering active accessories directly through an HDMI port.

The Standard Specification: 55 mA

According to the official HDMI specification, a source device (such as a PC, Blu-ray player, or game console) must supply a nominal +5V on Pin 18 with an output voltage between 4.7V and 5.3V. The specification explicitly states that a source must deliver a minimum of 55 mA to the bus.

Consequently, standard sink devices (such as monitors, televisions, and projectors) are permitted to draw a maximum of 55 mA during normal operation. This current is specifically allocated to power the Display Data Channel (DDC) circuitry and read the Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) EEPROM inside the display, allowing the source to detect display capabilities even when the display is unplugged from AC mains or in standby mode.

Overcurrent Protection Limit: 500 mA (0.5 A)

While the baseline requirement allows sink devices to draw up to 55 mA, the HDMI specification also mandates that source devices incorporate overcurrent protection to guard against short circuits and excessive load.

Under the specification, this protection mechanism must limit the maximum allowable current draw to no more than 0.5 A (500 mA). Therefore, 500 mA represents the absolute ceiling of current that can physically be extracted from an HDMI port before an internal fuse, positive temperature coefficient (PTC) thermistor, or active current limiter disconnects or throttles the power line.

Practical Considerations for Active Accessories

Many modern peripherals—such as HDMI-to-VGA converters, active optical cables, and wireless display dongles—attempt to draw operational power entirely from Pin 18 to avoid requiring an external power brick.

Relying on Pin 18 for high-power devices presents several risks:

  • Voltage Drop: Drawing more than the guaranteed 55 mA frequently causes the voltage on Pin 18 to fall below the minimum required 4.7V, leading to intermittent handshake failures, black screens, or total signal loss.
  • Inconsistent Source Designs: While some desktop graphics cards can supply up to the 500 mA limit, many laptops, set-top boxes, and energy-efficient media players strictly cap current output at or near 55 mA to preserve battery life and component longevity.
  • Component Damage: Sustained current draws approaching or exceeding 500 mA can permanently damage passive protection fuses on cheaper motherboards and source equipment.

Any accessory requiring more than 55 mA should utilize an external USB or dedicated DC power connection rather than drawing power exclusively through the HDMI interface.
