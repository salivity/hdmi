Penalties for Selling Non-Certified HDMI Products

The High-Definition Multimedia Interface (HDMI) standard is a proprietary technology governed strictly by HDMI Licensing Administrator, Inc. (HDMI LA). Manufacturers that sell non-certified, unapproved, or counterfeit HDMI cables, adaptors, and devices violate intellectual property rights and compliance agreements. Companies engaging in these unauthorized practices face severe operational, financial, and legal repercussions, including border seizures, substantial monetary damages, immediate product delisting across retail platforms, and permanent bans from the HDMI Adopter program.

Customs Seizures and Product Destruction

HDMI LA works directly with border and customs agencies worldwide, including U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and the European Union customs authorities, to identify and seize unauthorized imports. HDMI is a registered trademark; therefore, goods bearing the HDMI logo or marketing terms without proper certification are classified as counterfeit contraband. When flagged:

  • Shipments are confiscated at the port of entry at the importer's or manufacturer's expense.
  • The infringing goods are routinely destroyed.
  • Importers and manufacturers forfeit the entire value of the shipment and can be fined by customs agencies.

Trademark Infringement and Legal Damages

Producing or marketing hardware using HDMI trademarks, ports, or protocols without a valid Adopter Agreement constitutes trademark and patent infringement. HDMI LA and its founding members (such as Sony, Panasonic, and Philips) actively pursue legal remedies in federal and international courts. Consequences include:

  • Statutory Damages: In jurisdictions like the United States, statutory damages under the Lanham Act can reach up to $2,000,000 per counterfeit mark per type of goods sold if the infringement is deemed willful.
  • Actual Damages and Disgorgement of Profits: Courts can order the infringing company to surrender all profits generated from the sale of the uncertified goods.
  • Legal Fees: Infringing manufacturers are frequently ordered to cover the plaintiff’s attorney fees and court costs.

Termination of HDMI Adopter Status

For existing HDMI Adopters that bypass mandatory compliance testing at an Authorized Test Center (ATC) or self-certification guidelines:

  • Revocation of Rights: HDMI LA can immediately revoke the company's Adopter status, permanently barring them from licensing the technology.
  • Retroactive Royalties and Audits: Non-compliant adopters are subjected to forensic accounting audits. HDMI LA can demand unpaid royalties, retroactive licensing fees, and non-compliance penalties with interest.

Marketplace Delisting and Retail Bans

Major global e-commerce platforms—including Amazon, Newegg, Walmart Marketplace, and eBay—maintain strict anti-counterfeit policies and collaborate directly with HDMI LA. When uncertified products are identified:

  • Product listings are permanently removed (ASIN suspensions).
  • Seller accounts can be terminated without the ability to appeal.
  • Merchant funds may be frozen indefinitely to satisfy intellectual property claims.
  • Physical brick-and-mortar retailers will terminate vendor contracts to protect themselves from contributory infringement liability.

Breach of Contract with Downstream Partners

Manufacturers supplying uncertified components to downstream brands or original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) expose themselves to third-party civil liability. When an OEM discovers that a component manufacturer supplied non-compliant HDMI ports or cables, the supplier is liable for breach of contract, recall costs, and commercial losses associated with the compromised end-user products.
