Maximum Bus Capacitance for HDMI CEC

The Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) line in the HDMI standard is a single-wire, bidirectional bus operating on an open-drain architecture that allows interconnected multimedia devices to control one another. To maintain proper signal integrity and prevent communication timeouts across multiple connected devices, the HDMI specification strictly regulates electrical parameters. This article explains the maximum permissible electrical capacitance on the HDMI CEC bus and control traces, detailing the exact limits for both full network topologies and individual device designs.

Maximum Bus Capacitance Limits

Under the official HDMI specification, the maximum allowable total capacitance for the entire interconnected CEC bus is 7.2 nF (7,200 pF).

This 7.2 nF maximum represents the combined electrical capacitance of the entire CEC network, including:

  • The capacitance of all connected source, sink, and repeater devices.
  • The intrinsic capacitance of the HDMI cables connecting the devices.
  • The parasitic capacitance introduced by connectors and printed circuit board (PCB) traces.

Individual Device and Trace Capacitance

To ensure that a multi-device setup does not exceed the 7.2 nF system-wide threshold, strict constraints apply to each individual device connected to the bus:

  • Active Device Capacitance: A single HDMI device must present an electrical capacitance of no more than 700 pF at its CEC pin.
  • Unpowered / Standby Capacitance: When powered off or in standby mode, a device's CEC line capacitance must remain extremely low—typically specified to not exceed 100 pF to 150 pF—to prevent an idle device from loading down the active bus.
  • PCB Trace Budgeting: When routing the CEC control trace on a PCB, designers must account for parasitic capacitance introduced by the trace geometry, ESD protection diodes, and EMI filtering. In practice, the board-level trace capacitance is kept as low as possible (often under 50 pF) to leave sufficient margin for external cables and protection components while staying below the 700 pF device limit.

Why the Capacitance Limit Exists

The CEC bus relies on a passive pull-up resistor—nominally 27 kΩ (typically between 24 kΩ and 27.3 kΩ depending on tolerance and implementation)—to pull the signal line high to 3.3 V when no device is actively pulling it to ground.

Because the transition from a logic low to a logic high relies entirely on this resistor charging the line, the total RC time constant determines the signal's rise time.

  • Maximum Permitted Rise Time (10% to 90%): 250 µs
  • Maximum Permitted Fall Time (90% to 10%): 50 µs

If the total bus capacitance exceeds 7.2 nF, the RC time constant becomes too large, causing the rise time to exceed 250 µs. This results in signal distortion, bit-timing errors, and dropped CEC command frames across the connected network.
