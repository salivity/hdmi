How HDMI eARC Delivers 37 Mbps Audio Bandwidth

HDMI Enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC) achieves its remarkable 37 Mbps throughput by replacing the single-ended signaling of legacy ARC with a dedicated high-speed differential transmission pair. Introduced as part of the HDMI 2.1 specification, this architecture bypasses standard transmission bottlenecks, allowing modern displays to pass lossless, uncompressed audio formats—including 8-channel 24-bit/192kHz LPCM, Dolby TrueHD, and DTS-HD Master Audio—directly to external sound systems without downsampling or dynamic range compression.

The Shift to Differential Signaling

Legacy HDMI ARC relies on a single wire within the HDMI cable (pin 14) running an unbalanced, single-ended signal. This design is highly susceptible to electromagnetic interference (EMI) and crosstalk from neighboring video lines, capping ARC's functional bandwidth at roughly 1 Mbps—just enough for stereo PCM or lossy, compressed 5.1 surround sound (such as standard Dolby Digital or DTS).

To achieve up to 37 Mbps (specifically 36.864 Mbps of audio data payload), eARC utilizes a balanced differential signaling pair over two physical pins:

  • Pin 14 (Utility / HEAC+)
  • Pin 19 (Hot Plug Detect / HEAC-)

These pins were originally designated for the HDMI Ethernet and Audio Return Channel (HEAC) in HDMI 1.4. By sending two inverted versions of the same signal across this twisted pair, the receiving audio processor can cancel out common-mode noise through differential subtraction. This noise immunity permits significantly higher clock rates and cleaner data integrity across distances up to several meters.

The Audio Payload Architecture

The theoretical maximum bandwidth for uncompressed studio-master audio across 8 channels is calculated directly from industry-standard framing:

\[\text{8 channels} \times 192\text{ kHz sampling rate} \times 24\text{ bits per sample} = 36.864\text{ Mbps}\]

eARC transmits this high-resolution audio using an IEC 60958 framing structure. The data stream is modulated using Biphase Mark Code (BMC) on the differential pair, enabling the clock and data signals to be recovered simultaneously by the receiver's Phase-Locked Loop (PLL). Because the physical layer handles approximately 75 to 98 MHz of raw differential transition speed, it reliably sustains the net 36.864 Mbps payload required for uncompressed studio-quality tracks.

Common-Mode Discovery and Lip-Sync Control

In addition to the high-speed differential audio link, eARC simultaneously transmits low-speed bidirectional communication across the same two wires using common-mode signaling. Known as the eARC Data Channel (eADC), this lower-frequency channel operates independently of Consumer Electronics Control (CEC).

The eADC continually handles:

  • Device capability negotiation and link training
  • Format detection to verify bandwidth compatibility before transmission begins
  • Mandatory lip-sync correction data, compensating for video processing delays in the television without requiring user calibration

Physical Cable Implementation

Because eARC utilizes the dedicated HEAC physical traces, it does not require an Ultra High Speed (48 Gbps) HDMI cable to function for audio alone. Standard High Speed or Premium High Speed HDMI cables with Ethernet possess the required internal wiring on pins 14 and 19 to establish the differential pair, allowing the 37 Mbps audio pipe to operate independently of the primary High-Speed Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) video lanes.
