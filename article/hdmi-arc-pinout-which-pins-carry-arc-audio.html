HDMI ARC Pinout: Which Pins Carry ARC Audio?

HDMI Audio Return Channel (ARC) enables a display to transmit audio back upstream to an AV receiver or soundbar over a standard HDMI cable, eliminating the need for a separate S/PDIF optical audio connection. Introduced in the HDMI 1.4 specification, ARC reuses specific non-video pins rather than the primary Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) channels. Specifically, ARC audio relies on Pin 14 and Pin 19, backed by Pin 17 as their ground reference.

The Physical ARC Pins

Standard Type-A HDMI connectors feature 19 pins. ARC repurposes existing utility and detection wiring to transmit digital audio data:

  • Pin 14 (Utility / HEAC-): In HDMI specifications prior to version 1.4, Pin 14 was designated as "Reserved." With HDMI 1.4, it was repurposed as the "Utility" pin and forms one half of the HDMI Ethernet and Audio Return Channel (HEAC) differential pair, serving as HEAC-.
  • Pin 19 (Hot Plug Detect / HEAC+): Traditionally dedicated to the Hot Plug Detect (HPD) function—allowing source devices to detect when a display is connected—this pin was upgraded to serve a dual role. It continues to handle HPD detection via DC signaling while simultaneously transmitting high-frequency AC audio data as the HEAC+ line.
  • Pin 17 (DDC/CEC/HEAC Ground): This pin provides the dedicated ground return and shielding for the Consumer Electronics Control (CEC), Display Data Channel (DDC), and HEAC circuits, ensuring a stable reference and noise immunity for the ARC signal.

How ARC Uses These Pins

The HDMI specification defines two transmission methods across these physical pins:

  1. Single-Mode ARC: Uses only Pin 14 (Utility) as an unbalanced signal line, referenced against Pin 17 (Ground). This mode allows basic S/PDIF-style digital audio transmission without interfering with the standard Hot Plug Detect voltage on Pin 19.
  2. Common-Mode ARC: Utilizes both Pin 14 and Pin 19 simultaneously as a balanced, differential pair (HEAC- and HEAC+). By isolating high-frequency AC audio data from the DC level of the Hot Plug Detect line, both ARC audio and HDMI Ethernet can operate concurrently across the pair without signal corruption.

Relation to eARC (HDMI 2.1)

Enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC), introduced in HDMI 2.1, utilizes the exact same physical pins: Pin 14 and Pin 19, with Pin 17 as the ground reference. Unlike legacy ARC, eARC strictly uses the differential HEAC pair at higher data rates (up to ~37 Mbps compared to ARC's ~1 Mbps limit), enabling the transport of uncompressed 5.1 and 7.1 surround sound formats, as well as object-based formats such as Dolby Atmos and DTS:X.
