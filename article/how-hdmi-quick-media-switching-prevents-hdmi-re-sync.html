How HDMI Quick Media Switching Prevents HDMI Re-Sync

HDMI Quick Media Switching (QMS) is an HDMI 2.1a feature designed to eliminate the brief blackout—often called the HDMI "bonk" or re-sync delay—that occurs when a video source switches frame rates. By leveraging the mechanics of Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) technology, QMS allows displays to transition instantly between different video cadences (such as moving from 24 Hz film to 60 Hz streaming content) without interrupting the video signal or forcing a hardware-level handshake.

The Cause of the HDMI Re-Sync Delay

Under standard HDMI operation, changing video refresh rates requires a complete reconfiguration of the physical transmission stream. When a media player switches output from 60 Hz to 24 Hz to match source material, the clock frequency and timing parameters of the Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) architecture change.

Because the display's internal timing controller loses lock on the incoming stream, the display mutes the screen to prevent visual artifacts. The source and sink (display) must then re-synchronize, re-establish clock locks, and occasionally re-verify HDCP (High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection). This process takes anywhere from one to three seconds, resulting in a disruptive black screen.

How QMS Eliminates the Interruption

Quick Media Switching prevents this delay by utilizing the underlying framework of HDMI Variable Refresh Rate (VRR). Instead of rebuilding the video clock signal for every frame rate adjustment, QMS maintains a single, fixed transmission clock across different frame rates.

  1. Static Link Frequency: Under QMS, the HDMI connection establishes a baseline transmission link capable of handling the highest required refresh rate (such as 60 Hz or 120 Hz) at the chosen resolution and color format. This physical clock never resets during frame rate transitions.
  2. Modulating the Vertical Blanking Interval (VBLANK): Instead of changing the clock frequency to lower the frame rate, QMS expands the Vertical Blanking Interval—the period between the end of one video frame and the start of the next. By extending the duration of the VBLANK period, the source holds an existing frame on screen longer before transmitting the next one, effectively matching 24 Hz, 25 Hz, 30 Hz, or 50 Hz cadences seamlessly.
  3. Continuous Clock Lock: Because the base link rate and pixel clock remain steady, the display’s timing controller never loses sync with the incoming signal. The audio and video pipelines remain active, eliminating the need for signal re-acquisition, mode changes, or HDCP renegotiations.

Scope and Operating Boundaries

While QMS completely removes the blackout when switching between frame rates, it only functions when other video parameters remain identical. If a media transition requires a change in resolution (such as scaling from 1080p to 4K) or a change in color format/dynamic range (such as transitioning from Standard Dynamic Range to HDR10 or Dolby Vision), the HDMI connection must perform a full mode reset. In those scenarios, the traditional re-sync delay will still occur.
