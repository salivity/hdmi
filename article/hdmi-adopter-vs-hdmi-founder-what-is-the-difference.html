HDMI Adopter vs HDMI Founder: What Is the Difference?

The primary difference between an HDMI Founder and an HDMI Adopter lies in the creation versus the implementation of the HDMI standard. The HDMI Founders are the original technology companies that developed the High-Definition Multimedia Interface specification and established its intellectual property pool. In contrast, HDMI Adopters are third-party manufacturers and developers who pay licensing fees and royalties to legally incorporate HDMI technology and branding into their commercial products.

What Is an HDMI Founder?

The HDMI Founders comprise seven major consumer electronics and semiconductor companies: Hitachi, Panasonic, Philips, Silicon Image (now Lattice Semiconductor), Sony, Technicolor (formerly Thomson), and Toshiba. In 2002, these companies formed a working group to create a universal, uncompressed digital audio/video interface to replace legacy analog connections.

As the architects of the technology, the Founders:

  • Authored the original HDMI specifications (HDMI 1.0 through 1.4).
  • Established HDMI Licensing, LLC (now HDMI Licensing Administrator, Inc., or HDMI LA) to act as the official licensing agent for the standard.
  • Hold the foundational patents governing HDMI technology and receive a share of the licensing and royalty revenues generated from global use.

What Is an HDMI Adopter?

An HDMI Adopter is any commercial entity that signs the HDMI Adopter Agreement with HDMI Licensing Administrator, Inc. Adopters range from television and monitor manufacturers to graphics card developers, cable makers, and source-device manufacturers (such as gaming console or streaming box companies).

To use HDMI technology, an Adopter must:

  • Pay an annual administrative fee to maintain active status.
  • Pay per-unit royalties on every finished product sold that features HDMI ports or cabling.
  • Submit products to Authorized Test Centers (ATCs) or perform certified self-testing to guarantee specification compliance.
  • Adhere strictly to the HDMI Trademark and Logo Usage Guidelines to market their devices legally.

Key Differences Between Founders and Adopters

  • Intellectual Property and Governance: Founders created and own the foundational HDMI intellectual property. Adopters are simply licensees with permission to build hardware based on those specifications.
  • Financial Flow: Adopters pay into the HDMI licensing ecosystem through annual membership fees and per-unit royalties. Founders, along with participating patent holders, receive revenue distributions from that licensing pool.
  • Standard Evolution: While Founders defined the original architecture, future updates beyond HDMI 2.0 transitioned toward the HDMI Forum—a broader consortium where active Adopters can join working groups to vote on and shape future iterations (such as HDMI 2.1). However, the original Founder status remains a distinct historical and intellectual property designation.
  • Brand Rights: Founders defined the trademark framework, whereas Adopters receive limited legal rights to print HDMI logos on packaging, cables, and hardware, provided they pass strict compliance tests.
