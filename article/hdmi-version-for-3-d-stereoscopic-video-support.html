HDMI Version for 3D Stereoscopic Video Support

HDMI 1.4 is the specific version that first introduced standardized support for 3D stereoscopic video transmission. Released in May 2009 by the HDMI Licensing Administrator, this specification established common 3D formats and resolutions for home theater devices, paving the way for the wave of 3D televisions, Blu-ray players, and gaming consoles that followed. This article explains the capabilities introduced in HDMI 1.4, the 3D formats it standardized, and the hardware requirements necessary to transmit stereoscopic video.

The Introduction of HDMI 1.4

Before HDMI 1.4, there was no unified transmission standard for consumer 3D content over a single digital cable. While some proprietary and dual-link systems could transmit stereoscopic signals, cross-brand compatibility did not exist. HDMI 1.4 solved this issue by defining input/output protocols directly within the specification, standardizing how 3D video signals were packaged and transported from source devices to displays.

Supported 3D Formats and Resolutions

HDMI 1.4 required compliant displays to support mandatory 3D formats to ensure interoperability. The specification introduced support for stereoscopic resolutions up to:

  • 1080p at 24 Hz for movies and pre-recorded content (using the Frame Packing format).
  • 720p at 50 Hz or 60 Hz for high-frame-rate content such as gaming.
  • 1080i at 50 Hz or 60 Hz for broadcast television.

The standard established several transmission structures, most notably Frame Packing (which stacks full-resolution left-eye and right-eye frames into a single oversized transmission), as well as Side-by-Side (Half) and Top-and-Bottom formats, which divide standard bandwidth between the two perspectives for broadcast efficiency.

Cable Requirements for 3D Video

Transmitting 3D stereoscopic signals doubled the data payload compared to standard 2D signals at equivalent refresh rates. To handle this throughput, HDMI 1.4 designated the High Speed HDMI Cable (Category 2) as the required cable standard for 3D video. High Speed cables supported the 10.2 Gbps bandwidth ceiling required to deliver uncompressed, dual-stream full HD video.

HDMI 1.4a and 1.4b Updates

Shortly after the release of HDMI 1.4, the HDMI Consortium released two minor revisions to refine 3D support:

  • HDMI 1.4a (March 2010): Added mandatory 3D formats specifically for broadcast content, ensuring that set-top boxes, cable providers, and satellite distributors used standardized Top-and-Bottom and Side-by-Side formats.
  • HDMI 1.4b (October 2011): Clarified existing 3D specifications and enabled support for 1080p 3D video at 120 Hz total (60 Hz per eye), providing a smoother experience for 3D gaming.

Every HDMI standard released since—including HDMI 2.0 and HDMI 2.1—retains backward compatibility with the stereoscopic 3D structures first introduced in HDMI 1.4.
