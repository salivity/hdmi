How DSC Enables 8K and 10K Video Over HDMI 2.1

Display Stream Compression (DSC) is the critical technology that allows HDMI 2.1 to transmit ultra-high-resolution video formats, such as 8K at high refresh rates and 10K resolution, without exceeding physical bandwidth limits. While the HDMI 2.1 specification provides a substantial raw bandwidth capacity of 48 Gigabits per second (Gbps), uncompressed 8K and 10K streams with high dynamic range (HDR) demand data rates that far surpass this ceiling. By employing a standardized, visually lossless compression algorithm that operates in real time with virtually zero latency, DSC bridges the gap between hardware bandwidth limits and next-generation display demands.

The Bandwidth Problem in Next-Gen Resolutions

HDMI 2.1 introduced Fixed Rate Link (FRL) technology, expanding maximum raw throughput from HDMI 2.0's 18 Gbps up to 48 Gbps. However, video bandwidth requirements scale rapidly as resolution, refresh rate, and color depth increase.

An uncompressed 8K stream (7680 × 4320) running at 60Hz with 10-bit color (standard for HDR) requires roughly 64 to 80 Gbps of bandwidth, depending on timing margins and color subsampling. Pushing the refresh rate to 120Hz or moving up to 10K resolutions (10240 × 4320, often used in ultra-wide displays and commercial installations) requires data rates that exceed 100 Gbps. Because HDMI 2.1 is physically capped at 48 Gbps, transmitting these signals in an uncompressed state is technically impossible.

How Display Stream Compression (DSC) Works

Developed by the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA) and integrated into the HDMI 2.1 specification as DSC 1.2a, Display Stream Compression solves this bottleneck by applying a real-time, lightweight compression algorithm.

  • Up to a 3:1 Compression Ratio: DSC typically compresses the video data stream by up to a 3:1 ratio. For example, a heavy 80 Gbps stream can be reduced to approximately 27 to 30 Gbps, placing it safely beneath the 48 Gbps limit of an HDMI 2.1 connection.
  • Visually Lossless Quality: Unlike consumer video codecs like HEVC or AV1, which are designed for storage or streaming and introduce visible artifacts at low bitrates, DSC is classified as "visually lossless." It has undergone rigorous human visual testing to ensure that the compressed image cannot be distinguished from an uncompressed original under typical viewing conditions.
  • Sub-Millisecond Latency: Traditional compression uses temporal frame buffering (comparing previous and future frames), which introduces significant display lag unsuitable for gaming and real-time interaction. DSC uses a line-buffer-based system that processes images on a slice-by-slice basis. This architecture limits processing latency to fractions of a single microsecond, making it imperceptible to end users.
  • Predictive Coding and Adaptive Quantization: DSC divides each display frame into small blocks and applies predictive coding to determine color values. If a section of the image is visually simple, fewer bits are used. More complex, high-detail sections receive a larger share of the bit budget, preserving sharp text, fine textures, and accurate color gradients.

The End-to-End Pipeline for 8K and 10K Output

For DSC to function, both the source device (such as a modern graphics card, gaming console, or media player) and the sink device (such as an 8K television or high-end monitor) must support the standard.

  1. Handshake: When connected via an Ultra High Speed HDMI cable, the display and source communicate via Extended Display Identification Data (EDID). The display advertises its supported resolutions and DSC capabilities.
  2. Encoding: When an 8K 120Hz or 10K mode is selected, the source GPU automatically encodes the uncompressed rendering pipeline into a DSC-compliant stream.
  3. Transmission: The compressed data is transmitted over the HDMI 2.1 link at a bit rate well within the 48 Gbps physical threshold.
  4. Decoding: The display's onboard scaler or timing controller (TCON) decodes the DSC stream in real time and renders the uncompressed image to the screen.

Without Display Stream Compression, HDMI 2.1 would be restricted to uncompressed 8K video capped at 30Hz (or 60Hz with heavy 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, which degrades text clarity and color accuracy). By maintaining full 4:4:4 chroma, HDR color depth, and high refresh rates within the 48 Gbps envelope, DSC is the foundational technology that makes practical 8K and 10K video output possible over a single HDMI 2.1 cable.
