What Happens When HDMI 5V Drops Below 4.7V?

The HDMI specification defines a dedicated +5V power rail on Pin 18, requiring source devices to maintain a voltage between 4.7V and 5.3V to guarantee reliable communication with connected displays. When the voltage on this line drops below the 4.7-volt minimum threshold, it disrupts fundamental negotiation mechanisms, including Hot Plug Detect (HPD), EDID data retrieval, and HDCP authentication. This article details the specific hardware and functional failures that occur when an HDMI source fails to meet the 4.7-volt standard.

Failure to Read EDID and Display Recognition Issues

The 5V line primarily powers the display's internal EEPROM (Electrically Erasable Programmable Read-Only Memory) via the Display Data Channel (DDC), an I²C-based communication bus. This ensures the source can read the display's Extended Display Identification Data (EDID)—which specifies native resolutions, refresh rates, and audio capabilities—even if the display is turned off or in standby.

If the voltage drops below 4.7V, the EEPROM often browns out or fails to receive sufficient power to drive logic-level responses over the DDC. Consequently, the source cannot read the EDID, leading to:

  • The source defaulting to basic fallback resolutions (such as 640x480 or 1024x768).
  • Inability to negotiate advanced features such as HDR, variable refresh rates (VRR), or specific color spaces.
  • Total failure of the source operating system to recognize that a display is connected.

Hot Plug Detect (HPD) Disruption

Pin 19 of an HDMI connection serves as the Hot Plug Detect line. When a display is plugged in, it asserts a high logic level (typically 2.4V to 5.3V) back to the source, signaling that it is ready to receive a video stream. Most displays generate this HPD signal by pulling up the voltage provided directly by the source's 5V rail on Pin 18.

A voltage drop below 4.7V can pull the HPD line below the logic-high threshold required by the source’s transmitter chipset. If the source does not register the HPD signal as stable and high:

  • The source will shut off the Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) lines.
  • The display will show a "No Signal" or "Cable Disconnected" error.
  • Marginal voltages cause the HPD circuit to float near the threshold, triggering rapid connect/disconnect loops where the screen continuously blinks on and off.

Malfunction of Active Accessories and Converters

Passive HDMI cables rely on the 5V line only for signaling, but active accessories draw operational current directly from Pin 18. Devices such as active optical cables (AOC), HDMI-to-VGA/DVI converters, inline signal boosters, and passive-powered HDMI switches require the full 4.7V to 5.3V range to power their internal chips.

When the rail sags below 4.7V:

  • Active Optical Cables (AOC): The internal electrical-to-optical transceivers fail to initialize or transmit data, resulting in total link failure over long runs.
  • Format Converters: Digital-to-analog converters (such as HDMI to VGA) crash, produce distorted output, or fail entirely.
  • Switches and Splitters: Inline unpowered switches fail to switch inputs or experience unstable power resets.

HDCP Handshake Failures

High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP) requires continuous, bidirectional cryptographic communication between the source and the sink over the DDC lines. Because HDCP relies on the same power-dependent bus as EDID, voltage drops create communication timeouts and corrupted key exchanges. This leads to HDCP handshake failures, manifesting as snow/static on the display, blank screens when launching protected content (such as streaming apps or Blu-rays), or explicit HDCP error codes.

Common Causes and Mitigation

A voltage drop below 4.7V is usually caused by:

  • Long, thin copper HDMI cables exhibiting excessive resistive loss (IR drop).
  • High-draw active accessories pulling more current than the source's nominal 55mA specification allows.
  • Weak, degrading, or out-of-spec power supplies inside compact streaming devices, laptops, or graphics cards.

To resolve a sub-4.7V condition, install an inline 5V power injector (also known as an HDMI voltage inserter) near the source, use a port-powered HDMI splitter with an external DC adapter, or replace long passive cables with externally powered active solutions.
