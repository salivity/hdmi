Diagnostic LEDs on a Basic Field HDMI Tester

A basic field HDMI continuity tester relies on a series of diagnostic light-emitting diodes (LEDs) to verify pin-to-pin continuity and physical integrity across all conductors within an HDMI cable. During a functional test, the unit sequences or simultaneously lights individual LEDs corresponding to the 19 standard HDMI Type-A pins plus the outer chassis shield. This guide breaks down exactly which LEDs illuminate during a healthy continuity sweep and what functional channels those indicators represent.

The Standard 19-Pin LED Array

Most field HDMI testers feature 19 individually numbered LEDs (labeled 1 through 19) on both the master transmitter and the remote receiver unit. In a fully functional, non-damaged cable, all 19 of these LEDs illuminate in consecutive sequence (in sweep mode) or concurrently (in static mode). These 19 lights correspond directly to the standard HDMI pinout:

  • LEDs 1 to 12 (TMDS Video and Audio Channels): These pins handle Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS). They carry the uncompressed digital video and audio signals.
    • LEDs 1, 2, 3: TMDS Data 2 (Data 2+, Data 2 Shield, Data 2-)
    • LEDs 4, 5, 6: TMDS Data 1 (Data 1+, Data 1 Shield, Data 1-)
    • LEDs 7, 8, 9: TMDS Data 0 (Data 0+, Data 0 Shield, Data 0-)
    • LEDs 10, 11, 12: TMDS Clock (Clock+, Clock Shield, Clock-)
  • LED 13 (CEC): Consumer Electronics Control pin, enabling connected hardware to exchange commands using a single remote control.
  • LED 14 (Utility / HEC / ARC): Carries Ethernet (HEC) and Audio Return Channel (ARC/eARC) signals.
  • LEDs 15 and 16 (DDC Bus): Display Data Channel lines (Pin 15: SCL/Clock, Pin 16: SDA/Data), essential for EDID handshakes and HDCP copy-protection negotiation.
  • LED 17 (DDC/CEC Ground): The dedicated ground reference for the DDC and CEC lines.
  • LED 18 (+5V Power): Verifies the carrying wire for the low-voltage power rail used by the display to read EDID data from the source when the source is off or in standby.
  • LED 19 (Hot Plug Detect): Senses the physical connection to initiate the communication handshake between the source and display.

The Shield / Ground LED

In addition to the 19 numbered pin lights, nearly every field tester features an extra LED labeled "G", "S", or "Shield".

  • During a successful test, the Shield LED illuminates alongside the 19 numbered pins.
  • This indicator confirms electrical continuity through the cable's external metal braided shield, foil layer, and the metal connector housings on each end, which protect against electromagnetic interference (EMI).

Reading Diagnostic Results

  • Pass / Good Cable: LEDs 1 through 19, plus the Shield LED, illuminate bright and solid in a sequential 1-to-1 pattern across both the transmitter and receiver.
  • Open Circuit (Broken Wire): A specific LED fails to light on the receiver while illuminating on the transmitter, pinpointing an internal conductor break.
  • Short Circuit: Two or more LEDs illuminate simultaneously or abnormally bright on the receiver during what should be an individual step in the test cycle.
  • Crossed / Miswired Conductor: The LEDs light out of numerical sequence, indicating improper pin termination during custom field terminations.
