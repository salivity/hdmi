Does HDMI eARC Require a Special Cable?

HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) does require a specific type of HDMI cable to function properly, though it may not always require purchasing the newest standard on the market. Unlike traditional ARC, eARC relies on specific data pins within the cable to transmit high-bandwidth, uncompressed audio formats like Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio. To ensure seamless operation, users must use either a High Speed HDMI Cable with Ethernet or an Ultra High Speed HDMI Cable.

The Direct Answer: Cable Requirements for eARC

eARC cannot function on standard, basic HDMI cables. It requires an HDMI cable that includes dedicated wiring for Ethernet functionality. The two cable categories certified to support eARC are:

  1. Ultra High Speed HDMI Cables (HDMI 2.1): These cables support up to 48 Gbps bandwidth and are fully certified for all eARC features, including uncompressed 5.1, 7.1, and object-based audio like Dolby Atmos and DTS:X.
  2. High Speed HDMI Cables with Ethernet (HDMI 2.0): These cables support up to 18 Gbps bandwidth and contain the physical wiring necessary to carry eARC signals.

Cables labeled simply as "Standard HDMI" or "High Speed HDMI" without the "with Ethernet" designation lack the internal wiring required for eARC communication.

Why Ethernet Wiring Is Required

Traditional ARC used a single pin in the HDMI connector to transmit compressed stereo or 5.1 audio back to an AV receiver or soundbar.

eARC dramatically increases audio bandwidth from roughly 1–3 Mbps (on ARC) up to 37 Mbps. To transmit this much data without compressing the signal, eARC re-purposes the two physical pins inside the cable originally designated for the HDMI Ethernet Channel (HEC). If a cable lacks these internal Ethernet data lines, the eARC handshake between the television and sound system will fail, causing the system to either produce no sound or fall back to standard, lossy ARC mode.

Audio Capabilities: ARC vs. eARC Cables

Using the correct cable unlocks significantly higher audio fidelity:

  • Standard High Speed Cable (Standard ARC): Limited to lossy formats, including stereo PCM, Dolby Digital, and Dolby Digital Plus (which can carry compressed Dolby Atmos).
  • High Speed with Ethernet or Ultra High Speed Cable (eARC): Supports uncompressed 24-bit/192kHz audio, 8-channel uncompressed LPCM, Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD Master Audio, and full uncompressed Dolby Atmos and DTS:X.

How to Check Your Existing Cables

If you already own HDMI cables, check the physical jacket of the wire. Manufacturers print the cable specifications directly onto the insulation. Look for the phrases "High Speed with Ethernet" or "Ultra High Speed."

If purchasing a new cable specifically for an eARC sound system, choose an officially certified Ultra High Speed HDMI Cable. These cables feature an official HDMI Forum QR code label on the packaging, ensuring full compatibility with both current eARC audio requirements and modern video features like 4K at 120Hz or 8K resolution.
