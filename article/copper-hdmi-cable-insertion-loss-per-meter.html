Copper HDMI Cable Insertion Loss Per Meter

High-grade copper HDMI cables experience signal attenuation, measured as insertion loss, which increases alongside transmission frequency and distance. For premium passive copper cables utilizing thick conductors (typically 24 to 28 AWG), typical insertion loss ranges from roughly 0.8 dB to 1.5 dB per meter at HDMI 2.0 frequencies (around 3 GHz) and can reach 2.5 dB to 4.0 dB per meter at HDMI 2.1 frequencies (up to 6 GHz). Understanding these loss values is critical for determining maximum passive cable runs before signal degradation causes dropouts or complete transmission failure.

Typical Loss Values by Frequency and Gauge

Insertion loss in a copper transmission line is not a single static number; it scales with the frequency of the electrical signal due to the skin effect and dielectric losses.

  • At 1.0 GHz to 1.5 GHz (HDMI 1.4 / 1080p): High-grade 24–26 AWG copper cable typically exhibits an insertion loss between 0.5 dB and 1.0 dB per meter.
  • At 3.0 GHz (HDMI 2.0 / 4K at 60Hz): Insertion loss typically measures between 1.0 dB and 2.0 dB per meter.
  • At 6.0 GHz (HDMI 2.1 / 4K at 120Hz, 8K at 60Hz): Insertion loss increases significantly, ranging between 2.5 dB and 4.0 dB per meter.

Factors Influencing Insertion Loss

  1. Wire Gauge (AWG): Lower AWG ratings indicate thicker copper conductors. A 24 AWG wire has significantly lower DC resistance and less high-frequency skin effect attenuation than a slimmer 28 AWG or 30 AWG wire, yielding lower loss per meter.
  2. Dielectric Material: The insulation surrounding the twisted pairs (typically foamed polyethylene or polypropylene) absorbs high-frequency energy. High-grade cables use precision-foamed dielectrics with low dielectric constants to reduce signal absorption.
  3. Shielding and Geometry: Precision twisting of the transition-minimized differential signaling (TMDS) or fixed-rate link (FRL) pairs maintains a strict 100-ohm differential impedance. Variations in impedance cause signal reflections, compounding overall insertion loss.

Practical Distance Limits for Passive Copper

HDMI compliance specifications define maximum allowable total channel loss rather than strict physical length limits. Most HDMI sink devices (such as TVs or AV receivers) feature equalization circuits capable of recovering signals with up to 15 dB to 20 dB of total insertion loss.

Because premium 24 AWG copper yields roughly 1.5 dB to 2 dB per meter under 18 Gbps (HDMI 2.0) transmission, passive runs can reliably reach up to 7 to 10 meters. For Ultra High Speed HDMI 2.1 (48 Gbps), where loss frequently exceeds 3 dB per meter at peak frequencies, reliable passive copper links are typically limited to 3 to 5 meters. Beyond these lengths, insertion loss exceeds receiver equalization capabilities, requiring active copper chipsets or active optical cables (AOC).
