How 1:1 Pixel Mapping Fixes HDMI Overscan Issues

This article explains how 1:1 pixel mapping over an HDMI connection resolves the persistent problem of display overscan. Overscan occurs when a screen crops the outer edges of an image and artificially zooms in, causing lost visual content and blurred rendering. By enforcing 1:1 pixel mapping, a display matches every pixel transmitted from a source device directly to its own physical hardware pixels, completely eliminating edge cropping, scaling blur, and digital distortion.

Understanding Overscan and Its Modern Problems

Overscan is a legacy behavior dating back to cathode-ray tube (CRT) televisions. Broadcasters originally added extra image data beyond the visible screen edges to account for variations in CRT manufacturing and to hide broadcast signal artifacts. Televisions were designed to crop 2% to 5% of the outer boundary and stretch the remaining picture to fill the screen.

When modern digital sources—such as computers, media streamers, or gaming consoles—connect to a TV via HDMI, overscan causes significant usability issues. Operating system interfaces lose their taskbars, menu items, and window borders off-screen. Furthermore, because the display must scale a digital signal to fit the artificially enlarged area, text becomes blurry and graphics lose their intended sharpness.

What 1:1 Pixel Mapping Means

Pixel mapping refers to the relationship between the digital pixels generated by a source device's graphics processing unit (GPU) and the physical sub-pixels embedded in a display panel.

A 1:1 pixel mapping configuration (often labeled in TV settings as "Just Scan," "Dot by Dot," "Fit to Screen," or "Full Pixel") forces the display to disable all internal scaling algorithms. If a computer outputs a 3840x2160 resolution signal over HDMI, a 4K display operating in 1:1 mapping mode assigns each of those 8,294,400 input pixels to exactly one physical pixel on the panel.

How 1:1 Mapping Eliminates Overscan over HDMI

Connecting via HDMI allows the source and the display to exchange metadata through Extended Display Identification Data (EDID). While standard television presets often apply automatic zoom filters even over digital HDMI, enabling 1:1 pixel mapping overrides these presets in the following ways:

  • Disabling the Internal Scaler: The television's image processor halts any horizontal or vertical expansion. The full coordinate space sent by the source device remains visible from edge to edge without any outer border cutoff.
  • Preventing Interpolation Blur: When an image is enlarged during overscan, the display must create non-existent pixels via interpolation, which smears crisp edges and text. With 1:1 pixel mapping, each source pixel is rendered directly by the corresponding hardware pixel, restoring native sharpness and exact geometry.
  • Accurate Desktop Boundaries: Because the aspect ratio and frame dimensions are preserved exactly as output by the source, operating system taskbars, full-screen gaming menus, and critical status bars sit precisely inside the visible viewing area.

Enabling the Setting

To resolve overscan via HDMI, locate your display's picture or aspect ratio settings. Change the mode from presets like "16:9" or "Standard" to "Just Scan" (LG), "Screen Fit" (Samsung), "Dot by Dot" (Sony), or "Full" (Panasonic). Once applied, the HDMI signal renders without clipping, delivering a complete and pixel-perfect image.
