Using Clean HDMI with External ProRes Recorders

This article explains how cinema and mirrorless cameras utilize clean HDMI outputs to deliver pristine video signals to external recorders like the Atomos Ninja. You will learn what a clean HDMI feed is, how uncompressed video data travels from the camera sensor to an external unit, how recorders encode that data into Apple ProRes, and the workflow benefits of bypassing internal camera compression.

Understanding Clean HDMI Output

A clean HDMI output refers to an uncluttered video signal sent through a camera’s HDMI port that contains purely the visual data captured by the sensor, free of any on-screen display (OSD) elements. In standard operation, a camera displays diagnostic overlays such as battery levels, shutter speed, aperture, focus peaking, and histogram indicators on its screen. When clean HDMI is enabled in the camera's system settings, these graphical overlays are stripped from the output pipeline, ensuring the external recorder receives only raw video frames without burned-in telemetry.

The Signal Path: Sensor to HDMI

To understand how an external recorder receives this feed, it helps to track the camera's internal processing pipeline:

  1. Sensor Readout: The camera sensor captures incoming light, and the internal image processor debayers the raw photosite data.
  2. Signal Routing: Before the internal processor compresses the image into high-efficiency, lossy formats (such as H.264 or H.265) and writes it to internal SD or CFexpress cards, a tap of the signal is routed to the HDMI transmitter.
  3. Transmission: The uncompressed digital video data—often structured as a 10-bit 4:2:2 or 8-bit 4:2:2 YCbCr signal, or in select setups, 12-bit RAW data—is sent across high-speed HDMI 2.0 or 2.1 lanes to the receiving device.

How External Recorders Capture ProRes

External recorders, such as the Atomos Ninja series, serve as dedicated encoding computers mounted directly to the camera rig.

Once the clean video signal enters the recorder's HDMI input:

  • Hardware Ingestion: The recorder’s internal processing chip accepts the digital video stream.
  • ProRes Encoding: Rather than relying on the camera's internal processor, the recorder uses dedicated hardware to compress the incoming frame data in real-time into intermediate production codecs, typically Apple ProRes 422, ProRes HQ, or ProRes LT. Because ProRes uses intra-frame compression (where every frame is compressed independently rather than across a group of pictures), it places significantly less demand on editing workstations later.
  • Storage Offload: The encoded ProRes stream is written directly to high-capacity, removable 2.5-inch SATA SSDs or custom compact drives housed within the recorder.

Timecode and Camera Triggering

A seamless workflow requires the camera and external recorder to operate in sync. Clean HDMI carries more than just image data; it also transmits auxiliary data packets defined by consumer electronics and broadcast standards.

Modern cameras embed timecode and record trigger flags into the HDMI data stream. When the operator presses the camera's physical record button, a trigger packet signals the Atomos Ninja to begin recording simultaneously. The external file is saved with the exact timecode generated by the camera, ensuring synchronized audio and video in multi-camera workflows.

Key Advantages of Clean HDMI External Recording

  • Higher Bit Depths and Color Sampling: Cameras that only record 8-bit 4:2:0 internally often output 10-bit 4:2:2 through HDMI, allowing for better color grading flexibility and reduced banding in gradients.
  • Thermal Management: Encoding highly compressed codecs internally generates significant heat. Offloading encoding to an external recorder reduces camera sensor temperatures, eliminating recording time limits and thermal shutdowns.
  • Cost-Effective Media: Standard SATA SSDs used in external recorders generally provide a lower cost-per-gigabyte compared to proprietary camera memory cards.
