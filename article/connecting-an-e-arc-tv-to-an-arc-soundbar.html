Connecting an eARC TV to an ARC Soundbar

Connecting an eARC-capable television to a legacy ARC-only soundbar via HDMI works seamlessly due to built-in backward compatibility, but the connection will automatically downgrade to standard ARC performance. While you will still receive audio and retain basic control features like TV remote volume syncing, the system will be restricted to the lower bandwidth limits of standard ARC, preventing the playback of uncompressed, high-bitrate audio formats like Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio.

Automatic Downgrade to Standard ARC

HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) is designed to detect the capabilities of the receiving audio device. When the eARC port on your TV detects that the soundbar only supports legacy ARC, the TV automatically negotiates the connection down to standard ARC protocols. No special converters or adapters are needed; you simply connect a standard High-Speed HDMI cable between the TV's eARC port and the soundbar's ARC port.

Impact on Audio Quality and Formats

The primary difference between ARC and eARC is bandwidth. Standard ARC caps bandwidth at approximately 1 Mbps, whereas eARC supports up to 37 Mbps. Because the connection defaults to legacy ARC, your audio output will be subject to the following limitations:

  • Supported Formats: You can still transmit 2-channel uncompressed PCM, standard Dolby Digital (AC-3), DTS Digital Surround, and Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3).
  • Dolby Atmos Support: You can still receive lossy Dolby Atmos if your soundbar supports it, provided it is delivered via the Dolby Digital Plus container (commonly used by streaming services like Netflix, Disney+, and Prime Video).
  • Unsupported Formats: You will lose the ability to transmit lossless audio formats commonly found on 4K Blu-ray discs and next-gen gaming consoles, including Dolby TrueHD (lossless Dolby Atmos), DTS:X, DTS-HD Master Audio, and uncompressed 5.1 or 7.1 multichannel PCM.

If you play a source with a lossless audio track, your TV will typically transcode the signal down to standard Dolby Digital or stereo PCM so the soundbar can decode it.

Control and Usability (HDMI-CEC)

Basic system control functions will remain intact. HDMI-CEC (Consumer Electronics Control) operates independently of eARC. As long as CEC is enabled in your TV's settings menu (often branded as Anynet+, Bravia Sync, or Simplink), your TV remote will still control the soundbar's volume, mute function, and synchronized power on/off states. However, you will miss out on the mandatory automated lip-sync compensation that eARC provides, relying instead on manual audio delay settings if video and audio fall out of sync.
