HDMI Digital Pulse Rise and Fall Time Limits

This article provides a technical overview of the maximum and minimum allowed rise and fall times for high-speed HDMI digital pulse transitions. It covers the specific timing constraints defined across HDMI specifications—including Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) and Fixed Rate Link (FRL)—how these transition limits are measured, and their impact on signal integrity and compliance testing.

HDMI Digital Transition Requirements

Digital video data across HDMI relies on high-speed differential signaling. To maintain signal integrity and ensure receivers correctly decode symbols, the HDMI specification defines strict boundary conditions for transition speeds (edge rates) between the low and high logic states.

Rise and fall times are standardly measured across the 20% to 80% amplitude thresholds of the differential voltage swing.

Maximum Allowed Rise and Fall Time

Under the HDMI TMDS (Transition Minimized Differential Signaling) architecture used in HDMI 1.4 and HDMI 2.0:

  • Relative Maximum Limit: The maximum transition time is bound to the bit period (Unit Interval, or \(T_{bit}\)), specified at \(0.4 \times T_{bit}\). Transitions cannot exceed 40% of the total bit duration.
  • Eye-Diagram Compliance: The absolute upper limit is constrained by the HDMI transmitter compliance eye-diagram mask. Any edge that takes too long to transition infringes upon the horizontal and vertical margins of the eye mask, resulting in compliance failure.

For practical application across TMDS operating speeds:

  • At 1.65 Gbps (\(T_{bit} \approx 606\text{ ps}\)), the theoretical maximum rise/fall time is approximately 242 ps.
  • At 3.4 Gbps (\(T_{bit} \approx 294\text{ ps}\), the ceiling for HDMI 1.4), the maximum transition time is roughly 118 ps.
  • At 6.0 Gbps (HDMI 2.0), the Unit Interval is roughly \(167\text{ ps}\), requiring edge transitions typically faster than 67 ps to clear receiver eye-opening requirements.

Minimum Allowed Rise and Fall Time

HDMI specifications also enforce a minimum transition time to suppress electromagnetic interference (EMI) and prevent high-frequency ringing:

  • TMDS Minimum Limit: The minimum allowable 20% to 80% differential rise/fall time at the transmitter connector is 75 ps.

Slew rates that transition faster than 75 ps introduce excessive high-frequency spectral content, causing radiation compliance failures and cross-talk between adjacent differential lanes.

HDMI 2.1 Fixed Rate Link (FRL)

For HDMI 2.1 operating in FRL mode (supporting lane rates of 3, 6, 8, 10, and 12 Gbps):

  • Pulse transitions are evaluated using standardized transmitter equalization (FFE) and reference receiver equalizer models rather than static picosecond ceilings.
  • At 12 Gbps, a single bit duration is only 83.3 ps. Consequently, transitions must occur within fractions of this window—typically sub-30 ps—while managed by feed-forward equalization to prevent severe Inter-Symbol Interference (ISI).

Summary

The maximum allowed transition time for HDMI TMDS signals is \(0.4 \times T_{bit}\) (not to exceed the limits dictated by the compliance eye mask), while the minimum allowed transition time is fixed at 75 ps. Together, these thresholds ensure that the pulse edges are fast enough to keep the eye open without being so abrupt that they generate unmanageable electromagnetic noise.
