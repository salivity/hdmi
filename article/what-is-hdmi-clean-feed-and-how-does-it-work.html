What Is HDMI Clean Feed and How Does It Work?

An HDMI clean feed is a raw, unencumbered video signal sent from a camera through its HDMI port without any on-screen display (OSD) elements like shutter speed, battery life, or focus boxes. This article explains what clean HDMI output is, how modern camera hardware and firmware technically isolate the pure video feed from the user interface, and why it is an essential feature for live streamers, broadcasters, and professional video creators.

Understanding Clean HDMI Output

When operating a digital camera, the built-in LCD screen and electronic viewfinder (EVF) typically display crucial shooting data. This includes exposure settings (ISO, aperture, shutter speed), audio meters, framing grids, battery levels, and autofocus tracking boxes.

A standard HDMI output mirrors everything shown on that internal display. In contrast, an HDMI clean feed isolates the footage recorded by the camera sensor, transmitting only the pure video signal to external devices such as capture cards, monitors, or dedicated video recorders.

How Cameras Strip On-Screen Menus

The removal of menus and status icons is not done by "erasing" graphics after they are drawn; instead, it is handled at the hardware and firmware processing levels through layered rendering:

  1. Separation of Video and Graphic Layers: Modern digital cameras utilize system-on-chip (SoC) image processors that maintain separate memory buffers. One buffer processes the raw sensor data into the video frame, while a secondary graphics processor generates the On-Screen Display (OSD) overlay.
  2. The Compositing Stage: For the camera's built-in LCD or EVF, the processor combines these two layers into a single visual feed using an internal compositor.
  3. Bypassing the Compositor for HDMI: When a clean HDMI output mode is enabled in the camera firmware, the video pipeline reroutes the output. Instead of pulling from the combined composite buffer, the camera directs the untouched video buffer straight to the HDMI transmitter chip. The OSD layer remains visible on the camera's physical display (on supported models) or is disabled entirely, leaving the external HDMI feed pristine.

Why HDMI Clean Feed Is Essential

  • Live Streaming: Platforms like Twitch, YouTube, and Zoom require an unobscured view of the subject. A clean feed allows cameras to function as high-end webcams without cluttering the broadcast with technical readouts.
  • External Recording: Devices like external monitors and SSD recorders capture higher bitrates and formats (such as Apple ProRes) directly from the HDMI port. A clean feed guarantees the recorded master files are free of interface overlays.
  • Software-Driven Control: By sending a clean signal into production software like OBS Studio or vMix, creators can add their own professional overlays, lower thirds, and branding without clashing with the camera's built-in graphics.

Enabling Clean Feed on Your Camera

Most modern mirrorless and DSLR cameras include an option for clean output within their setup or display menus. Look for settings labeled "HDMI Info Display," "HDMI Output Settings," or "Clean Out." Setting these options to "Off" or selecting an external recording preset ensures the camera suppresses interface elements across the HDMI pipeline.
