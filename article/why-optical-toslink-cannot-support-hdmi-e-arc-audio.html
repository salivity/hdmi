Why Optical Toslink Cannot Support HDMI eARC Audio

Optical Toslink cables cannot support modern, high-bitrate audio formats like Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD Master Audio, and uncompressed 7.1 LPCM because of severe physical and protocol-level bandwidth constraints. While HDMI eARC provides up to 37 Mbps of bandwidth to carry object-based formats like lossless Dolby Atmos, Toslink is limited to a fraction of that capacity, restricting it to uncompressed two-channel stereo or legacy, lossy 5.1 surround sound.

Severe Bandwidth Limitations

The primary reason Toslink fails to match HDMI eARC is data throughput. Toslink relies on the S/PDIF (Sony/Philips Digital Interface) standard, developed in the 1980s. This protocol caps data transfer rates at roughly 1.5 Mbps to 3.1 Mbps.

In contrast, HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel), introduced with HDMI 2.1, offers a dedicated bandwidth of roughly 37 Mbps. Modern lossless formats require this expanded pipeline:

  • Dolby TrueHD / Lossless Atmos: Requires up to 18 Mbps.
  • DTS-HD Master Audio / DTS:X: Requires up to 24.5 Mbps.
  • Uncompressed 5.1/7.1 LPCM: Requires up to 27.6 Mbps.

Because a standard Toslink cable simply does not have the capacity to transmit these high-bitrate data streams, it cannot transmit lossless surround sound.

Hardware and Optical Transceiver Constraints

Although fiber-optic technology in enterprise telecommunications can transmit gigabits of data per second, consumer Toslink cables and ports use low-cost components designed to keep manufacturing inexpensive:

  • Plastic Optical Fiber (POF): Toslink typically uses 1mm plastic optical core instead of high-grade glass. This plastic disperses light quickly, limiting high-frequency signal integrity over distance.
  • Inexpensive LED Transmitters: The optical transmitters in consumer electronics are slow, inexpensive red LEDs rather than the high-speed laser diodes used in data centers.

Upgrading these components would require redesigning consumer hardware ports, which manufacturers opted not to do given the universal adoption of HDMI.

Legacy Protocols and Handshaking

Toslink is a one-way, unidirectional connection governed by legacy protocols. It lacks the modern bidirectional communication channels required by today’s advanced home theater systems.

HDMI eARC features a dedicated eARC Data Channel that runs over a pair of wires in the HDMI cable. This channel allows the television and the audio receiver or soundbar to negotiate formats dynamically, automatically discover audio capabilities, manage mandatory lip-sync correction, and handle copy protection (HDCP). Toslink lacks the architecture to perform this communication, meaning it cannot negotiate or process modern audio metadata.
