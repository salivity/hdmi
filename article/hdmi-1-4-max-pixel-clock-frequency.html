HDMI 1.4 Max Pixel Clock Frequency

This article examines the maximum pixel clock frequency supported by the legacy HDMI 1.4 specification. It breaks down the technical transmission limits of the standard, details how the clock rate translates into total video bandwidth, and outlines the practical display resolutions and refresh rates that this limit allows.

Under the HDMI 1.4 specification, the maximum supported pixel clock frequency—formally designated as the TMDS (Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling) clock rate—is 340 MHz (340 Mcsc).

Bandwidth and Signaling

HDMI uses three differential data lanes to transmit video data, accompanied by a dedicated TMDS clock lane. With the 8b/10b encoding scheme utilized by HDMI 1.4, each clock cycle transfers 10 bits per lane. At a 340 MHz clock frequency:

  • Per-Lane Data Rate: 3.40 Gbps (Gigabits per second)
  • Total Bandwidth: 10.2 Gbps across all three channels
  • Effective Video Throughput: 8.16 Gbps (after subtracting the 20% overhead from 8b/10b encoding)

While HDMI 1.3 originally introduced the jump to a 340 MHz TMDS clock from HDMI 1.2's 165 MHz cap, HDMI 1.4 retained this exact 340 MHz physical signaling limit while adding features such as HDMI Ethernet Channel (HEC), Audio Return Channel (ARC), and formal 4K format support.

Supported Resolutions and Frame Rates

The 340 MHz limit dictates the maximum amount of pixel data sent per second, directly capping supported resolution and refresh rate combinations at standard 8-bit (24-bit per pixel) color:

  • 4K (3840×2160): Supported up to 30 Hz in RGB or 4:4:4 color.
  • 4K DCI (4096×2160): Supported up to 24 Hz.
  • 1080p (1920×1080): Supported up to 120 Hz natively, and up to 144 Hz using reduced blanking (CVT-RB) timings.
  • 1440p (2560×1440): Supported up to 60 Hz.
  • Stereoscopic 3D: Up to 1080p at 24 Hz or 720p at 60 Hz per eye.

Because of the 340 MHz pixel clock ceiling, HDMI 1.4 cannot support 4K resolution at 60 Hz with full 8-bit 4:4:4 color, a capability that required the later HDMI 2.0 standard's 600 MHz clock rate.
