Parade and MegaChips HDMI Dock Protocol Converters

This article provides an overview of protocol converters manufactured by Parade Technologies and MegaChips (now part of Kinetic Technologies) that are commonly embedded within modern USB-C and Thunderbolt HDMI docks. It explores the technical function of these integrated circuits, why they are essential for translating native DisplayPort streams into HDMI video signals, and how popular chipsets such as the Parade PS176/PS186 and MegaChips MCDP28xx/MCDP29xx series govern display resolutions, refresh rates, and copy protection.

The Purpose of a Protocol Converter in an HDMI Dock

Most modern computing devices output video over USB-C or Thunderbolt ports using DisplayPort Alternate Mode (DP Alt Mode). However, consumer displays, televisions, and projectors predominantly rely on HDMI interfaces. Because DisplayPort and HDMI use fundamentally different signaling methods—packet-based low-voltage differential signaling for DisplayPort and Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) for HDMI—a host device cannot simply reroute electrical pins to output HDMI video.

A protocol converter (also referred to as an active bridge or retiming converter) acts as a hardware bridge. Embedded directly onto the dock's printed circuit board (PCB), it demultiplexes incoming DisplayPort video, audio, and auxiliary data streams and re-encodes them into standard HDMI output formats in real time with minimal latency.

Common Parade Technologies Protocol Converters

Parade Technologies is one of the most widespread silicon providers for display adapters and docking stations. Their converters are renowned for integrated jitter cleaners and robust signal integrity management.

  • Parade PS176: A widely used DP 1.2a to HDMI 2.0b protocol converter. It supports data rates up to 5.4 Gbps per lane across 4 lanes, enabling 4K UHD resolutions at 60Hz. It handles HDCP 1.4 to HDCP 2.2 cross-conversion and includes an on-chip microprocessor for firmware updates.
  • Parade PS186: An upgraded version designed for DP 1.4a to HDMI 2.0b conversion. It introduces support for High Dynamic Range (HDR) static metadata pass-through and lower power consumption profiles suitable for compact, bus-powered travel docks.
  • Parade PS196: A newer-generation converter engineered for HDMI 2.1 docks, capable of converting DP 1.4 or DP 2.0 signals into HDMI 2.1 Fixed Rate Link (FRL), allowing docks to deliver resolutions up to 8K at 60Hz or 4K at 120Hz.

Common MegaChips (Kinetic Technologies) Protocol Converters

MegaChips developed high-performance display processing chips, an intellectual property portfolio that later transitioned under Kinetic Technologies. Their converters are commonly found in commercial docking hubs from brands like Dell, Lenovo, and HP.

  • MegaChips MCDP2800 / MCDP2850: These chips convert DisplayPort 1.2 to HDMI 1.4b or HDMI 2.0. They are engineered with deep-color processing and audio stream format conversions (such as multi-channel LPCM, Dolby Digital, and DTS).
  • MegaChips MCDP2900: A ubiquitous DP 1.4 to HDMI 2.0b active converter. It features an integrated ARM Cortex-M0 processor to manage secure firmware execution, Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) tunneling, and conversions between DisplayPort HDCP 2.2/1.3 and HDMI HDCP 2.2/1.4. The MCDP2900 supports full 4K UHD at 60Hz with 24-bit RGB/YCbCr 4:4:4 or 12-bit HDR formats.

Core Functions Managed by These Converters

Beyond basic signal translation, these chips oversee several essential operations within a dock:

  • HDCP Handshaking: They negotiate High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection between the host OS and the display, decrypting incoming DisplayPort content streams and re-encrypting them via HDMI-compliant HDCP keys.
  • Clock and Data Recovery (CDR): They clean degraded high-speed signals over long cable runs, reducing jitter before transmitting the signal to the external monitor.
  • Audio Protocol Translation: They map DisplayPort audio packets into the standard HDMI audio sample packets, maintaining sync with video frames.
  • Firmware Adaptability: Most include integrated flash memory or SPI interfaces, enabling dock manufacturers to update the converter's firmware to resolve display compatibility quirks with specific monitors or operating systems.
