How HDMI CEC One Touch Play Powers on AV Receivers

HDMI Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) features a core automation capability called One Touch Play, which allows a single playback device to power on and configure an entire home theater setup with one command. Acting as the central switching hub, the Audio/Video (AV) receiver intercepts these control signals, awakens itself from standby, sets the appropriate audio and video inputs, and passes the power commands downstream to the display. This article explains the underlying mechanism, signal flow, and device coordination that enable an AV receiver to automate the power state of an entire media stack.

The CEC Physical Layer and Standby Communication

HDMI cables contain a dedicated electrical conductor on Pin 13 designated for CEC signaling. This single-wire, bidirectional serial bus operates independently of the high-speed audio and video data channels.

Even when your media devices appear off, they maintain a low-power standby state that keeps their internal HDMI CEC microcontrollers active. Because the Pin 13 bus remains powered and receptive to serial data, devices can send and receive control packets without needing the full video processing pipeline active.

Initiation: The Source Device Signal

The sequence begins at the source device—such as a streaming box, Blu-ray player, or gaming console. When you press the power or home button on the source's remote:

  1. The source generates a standardized CEC opcode known as <Image View On> (or <Text View On>).
  2. The source broadcasts an <Active Source> message containing its unique physical HDMI address (for example, 1.1.0.0).
  3. These messages travel down the HDMI cable toward the AV receiver.

The AV Receiver as the Central Coordinator

Because modern AV receivers sit inline between source components and the display, the receiver functions as a signal router and interpreter:

  • Signal Interception: The AV receiver detects the incoming <Active Source> message on its designated input port.
  • Power State Transition: The receiver’s internal microcontroller triggers the main power relay, waking the unit from standby.
  • Input Switching: By reading the physical address attached to the packet, the receiver automatically routes internal video switching to the corresponding HDMI port and activates its internal digital signal processors (DSP) and amplifiers.
  • System Audio Routing: If configured, the receiver issues a <System Audio Mode Request> across the bus, notifying the ecosystem that the AVR will handle all audio decoding, which prompts connected televisions to mute internal speakers.

Passing the Signal to the Display

Once the receiver processes the message, it repeats the <Image View On> and <Active Source> commands through its HDMI output (eARC/ARC port) to the television or projector.

Upon receiving <Image View On>, the display powers on from standby. When it detects the <Active Source> payload indicating the AV receiver's output port, the display immediately switches its active input to match the AVR, bypassing manual source selection entirely.

Resulting Automated Chain Reaction

Through this structured cascade of commands over Pin 13, a single button press on a streaming remote initiates a three-step chain: the source boots up, the AV receiver powers on and locks onto the correct audio/video stream, and the display turns on to present the image. The entire media stack synchronizes into an active state in a matter of seconds without requiring infrared line-of-sight or complex universal remotes.
