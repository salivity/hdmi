HDMI Transmitter Eye Diagram Compliance Mask

The HDMI transmitter eye-diagram compliance mask defines the geometric boundary that Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) signals must avoid during physical-layer compliance testing. This mask guarantees that the transmitter delivers adequate differential voltage levels and maintains minimal timing jitter across high-speed data lines before sending the signal across an HDMI cable to a receiver.

Purpose of the Compliance Mask

In high-speed digital transmission, the eye diagram overlays hundreds or thousands of signal transitions across single-bit periods (Unit Intervals, or UIs). The transmitter compliance mask represents a "keep-out" zone positioned within the center of the open eye. If any measured differential waveform trace crosses into this inner mask boundary, the transmitter fails compliance because the transmitted signal lacks the necessary amplitude, rise/fall performance, or jitter margin required for error-free recovery by a sink device.

Differential Voltage Limits and Mask Geometry

Transmitter compliance testing is conducted at Test Point 1 (TP1), representing the output pins of the HDMI connector terminated into an ideal reference load (typically 50-ohm pull-up resistors to a 3.3 V termination voltage, \(AV_{CC}\)).

The compliance mask features a diamond or hexagonal profile centered on the Unit Interval, defining limits on both the vertical (voltage) and horizontal (time) axes:

  • Differential Voltage Swing (\(V_{swing}\)): For standard HDMI TMDS transmitters, the typical differential peak-to-peak output voltage swing ranges between 800 mV and 1200 mV. The nominal single-ended voltage swing on each differential leg ranges between 400 mV and 600 mV.
  • Minimum Eye Opening (Vertical Height): The mask specifies a minimum eye-opening height at the center of the UI. For legacy TMDS testing up to 3.4 Gbps, the inner mask height guarantees a minimum differential voltage amplitude—typically requiring an inner boundary clearance of at least 150 mV to 200 mV differential (depending on the exact HDMI specification revision and data rate) to prevent signal degradation over distance.
  • Maximum Allowable Jitter (Horizontal Width): The horizontal boundaries establish the maximum permissible total jitter (\(T_J\)). In standard HDMI specifications, the allowable transmitter jitter generally restricts edge transitions so that the inner mask maintains a clear width typically equivalent to 0.70 UI to 0.75 UI, allowing no more than 0.25 UI to 0.30 UI of aggregate jitter.

HDMI 2.0 and HDMI 2.1 Considerations

As data rates scale to 6 Gbps per channel under HDMI 2.0 and up to 12 Gbps per lane under HDMI 2.1 FRL, transmission line attenuation and skin effect close the physical eye directly at the transmitter pins.

To address this, modern specifications alter the compliance approach:

  • HDMI 2.0 (TMDS at 6 Gbps): The compliance mask is evaluated after applying a standardized reference equalizer model (defined in the specification) to the captured signal to reconstruct the eye diagram.
  • HDMI 2.1 (FRL): The architecture transitions to embedded clock schemes and multi-level signaling modes. FRL transmitter testing replaces static optical-style masks with post-equalization masks that account for programmable transmitter de-emphasis, pre-shoot, and reference receiver continuous-time linear equalization (CTLE).
