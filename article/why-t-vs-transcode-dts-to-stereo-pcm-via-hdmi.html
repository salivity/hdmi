Why TVs Transcode DTS to Stereo PCM via HDMI

Many home theater users discover that high-definition DTS surround sound tracks are unexpectedly downmixed to two-channel stereo PCM when routed from a source device through a television to a soundbar or AV receiver. This behavior typically stems from a combination of omitted audio licensing, HDMI handshake limitations, and fallback protocols designed to prevent silent playback. Understanding why televisions alter this audio signal helps identify the right settings or hardware adjustments needed to restore full surround sound.

Lack of DTS Licensing and Codec Support

The most common reason a television downmixes DTS (Digital Theater Systems) to stereo PCM is that the manufacturer chose not to license DTS decoding or passthrough capabilities. Audio licensing incurs royalty fees for every unit sold. Because mainstream streaming services (such as Netflix, Disney+, and Amazon Prime Video) almost exclusively use Dolby audio formats (Dolby Digital Plus and Dolby Atmos), many TV manufacturers—including Samsung and certain generations of LG—dropped native DTS support entirely to cut production costs.

When a TV lacks a DTS license, its internal hardware cannot decode, process, or bitstream the raw DTS data. Rather than producing complete silence or digital noise, the TV automatically falls back to uncompressed two-channel PCM, which is the baseline audio standard that all HDMI-compliant devices can decode.

The HDMI EDID Handshake

Audio capabilities across an HDMI chain are determined by an Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) handshake. When a media player or gaming console connects directly to a television, the TV sends its EDID profile to the source device, listing the audio formats it supports.

If the TV does not support DTS, the EDID communicates that it cannot accept multi-channel DTS streams. Depending on how the source device and TV are configured:

  • The source device may be forced to downmix the audio to stereo before sending it to the TV.
  • The TV may accept a multi-channel stream it cannot output, stripping the surround metadata and outputting a basic stereo PCM stream over HDMI ARC or eARC.

HDMI ARC vs. HDMI eARC Bandwidth Constraints

Standard HDMI ARC (Audio Return Channel) has limited bandwidth. While it can carry compressed 5.1 bitstream audio (such as standard Dolby Digital or legacy DTS) and uncompressed 2.0 PCM, it lacks the bandwidth to transmit uncompressed multi-channel LPCM (5.1 or 7.1).

If a TV receives a DTS signal, lacks the ability to pass the raw bitstream through ARC, and attempts to convert it to PCM, the bandwidth limit of standard ARC forces that PCM signal down to two channels. HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel) solves the bandwidth issue and supports multi-channel PCM, but only if both the TV and the sound system support eARC and the TV's firmware permits multi-channel PCM passthrough.

Incorrect TV Audio Output Settings

Even on televisions that technically support DTS passthrough, incorrect software settings can cause transcoding to stereo PCM. Modern TVs typically offer three main audio output settings:

  • Auto: The TV determines the format automatically, which frequently defaults to stereo PCM for maximum compatibility.
  • PCM: Forces the TV to decode all incoming audio internally and output it as uncompressed PCM. Without a DTS decoder, this results in two-channel stereo.
  • Passthrough / Bitstream: Instructs the TV to ignore the audio encoding entirely and pass the untouched digital bitstream directly to the receiver or soundbar.

If a TV supports DTS pass-through but is set to "PCM" or "Auto," it will actively transcode the multi-channel signal into stereo.

How to Bypass the Stereo Transcoding Issue

To avoid having DTS audio downmixed into stereo PCM, users have three primary solutions:

  1. Direct Connection: Connect the source device (Blu-ray player, media streamer, or console) directly into an HDMI input on the AV receiver or soundbar, routing only the video out to the TV. This bypasses the TV's audio limitations completely.
  2. Source-Level LPCM Decoding: If using an HDMI eARC setup, configure the playback device to decode the DTS track internally into multi-channel LPCM before sending it to the TV. The TV can pass multi-channel LPCM over eARC without needing a dedicated DTS license.
  3. HDMI Audio Extractor: Use an external HDMI audio splitter to separate the audio and video signals at the source, sending high-resolution audio directly to the sound system and video directly to the display.
