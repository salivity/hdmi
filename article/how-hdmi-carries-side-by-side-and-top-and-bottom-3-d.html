How HDMI Carries Side-by-Side and Top-and-Bottom 3D

This article provides an overview of how High-Definition Multimedia Interface (HDMI) delivers stereoscopic 3D content using Side-by-Side (SbS) and Top-and-Bottom (TaB) multiplexing techniques. It explores how these layouts fit two distinct visual perspectives into standard 2D video frames, how HDMI signaling informs the display of the formatting, and the technical trade-offs between horizontal and vertical compression over the transmission link.

The Principles of 3D Transmission Over HDMI

Stereoscopic 3D requires two separate video feeds: one for the left eye and one for the right eye. While the HDMI specification (introduced formally in HDMI 1.4) supports "Frame Packing"—a method that transmits both full-resolution frames sequentially with an active blanking space between them—bandwidth constraints in broadcasting and legacy playback hardware popularized sub-sampled multiplexing formats.

Side-by-Side and Top-and-Bottom formats solve the bandwidth problem by squeezing both the left and right perspectives into the physical payload of a single conventional 2D video frame (such as 1080p, 1080i, or 720p). To standard HDMI transmitter and receiver hardware, the signal behaves electrically as a normal 2D frame buffer over the Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) channels.

Side-by-Side (SbS) 3D Architecture

In a Side-by-Side layout, the source device takes two full-resolution frames and reduces their horizontal resolution by half using an anamorphic squeeze.

  • Pixel Geometry: In a standard 1080p frame (1920×1080 pixels), the left-eye view is compressed into a 960×1080 box on the left half of the raster, and the right-eye view is compressed into a 960×1080 box on the right half.
  • Scan Order: The HDMI transmission serializes pixels row by row from top to bottom. Because the images are placed horizontally next to each other, a single scanline carries pixel data for the left eye first, immediately followed by the pixel data for the right eye across the horizontal axis.
  • Resolution Trade-off: Horizontal spatial resolution is halved, but full vertical resolution (1080 lines) is maintained. This makes SbS particularly suitable for content with fine horizontal details or for broadcast over interlaced signals (1080i), where vertical resolution cannot afford further decimation.

Top-and-Bottom (TaB) 3D Architecture

In a Top-and-Bottom layout (often referred to as Over-Under), the source compresses the two stereoscopic images vertically rather than horizontally.

  • Pixel Geometry: Within a 1920×1080 frame, the top half (1920×540 pixels) contains the complete horizontally intact image for the left eye. The bottom half (1920×540 pixels) contains the image for the right eye.
  • Scan Order: During HDMI transmission, the source clocks out the entire left-eye image line by line across the top 540 active scanlines. Once the midpoint of the frame is reached, the subsequent 540 active scanlines deliver the right-eye image.
  • Resolution Trade-off: Full horizontal resolution (1920 pixels) is preserved, while vertical resolution is cut by 50%. TaB is preferred for progressive video feeds, such as 720p at 50Hz or 60Hz, where vertical lines are stable and maintaining full horizontal sharpness is prioritized for motion clarity.

HDMI Signaling and Metadata Delivery

Because both SbS and TaB fit completely inside a standard video raster, an HDMI cable transfers the raw pixel data without needing higher clock frequencies or modified physical pinouts. However, the display device must be informed that the frame contains two multiplexed views rather than a normal 2D picture.

  1. HDMI Vendor-Specific InfoFrame (VSIF): The source device embeds a dedicated 3D metadata packet inside the data island periods of the HDMI signal (the blanking intervals between active video lines).
  2. 3D_Structure Field: Within the VSIF defined by the HDMI 1.4a specification, a specific byte designated as 3D_Structure tells the display how to parse the payload:
    • A value indicating Side-by-Side (Half) instructs the sink (display) to split each horizontal line in half.
    • A value indicating Top-and-Bottom instructs the sink to split the vertical frame at the halfway point.
  3. Display Reconstruction: Upon reading the InfoFrame, the display’s video processor demultiplexes the incoming frame into two individual buffers, scales each sub-frame back to the native panel resolution (stretching horizontally for SbS or vertically for TaB), and syncs the output to active shutter glasses, a polarized screen filter, or an autostereoscopic panel.

If the source device fails to send the HDMI 3D InfoFrame, the display receives the feed as a standard 2D image, requiring the user to manually activate the display's internal SbS or TaB 3D decoding modes via the menu interface.
