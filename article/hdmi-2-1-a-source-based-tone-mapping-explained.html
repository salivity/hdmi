HDMI 2.1a Source-Based Tone Mapping Explained

The HDMI 2.1a specification introduces Source-Based Tone Mapping (SBTM), a feature designed to optimize High Dynamic Range (HDR) content delivery by offloading tone mapping processes from the display to the source device. This article explores how HDMI 2.1a defines SBTM, how the technology improves upon traditional tone mapping methods, and how it handles complex viewing environments, such as simultaneous HDR and Standard Dynamic Range (SDR) content on a single screen.

What Is Source-Based Tone Mapping?

Source-Based Tone Mapping is an optional feature introduced in the HDMI 2.1a update. In standard HDR implementations, the source device (such as a gaming console, streaming box, or PC) transmits a fixed HDR signal, leaving the display (such as a television or monitor) entirely responsible for mapping that signal to match its specific brightness, contrast, and color volume capabilities.

Under the HDMI 2.1a standard, SBTM allows the source device to actively participate in or entirely manage the tone mapping process. Instead of leaving the interpretation exclusively to the target display, the source device adapts its output to match the precise capabilities of the connected screen.

How SBTM Works

The operation of SBTM relies on two-way communication between the source and the sink (display):

  1. Capability Reporting: The display sends detailed metadata regarding its specific color and luminance limits to the source device via the HDMI interface.
  2. Source-Side Adjustment: Armed with the display's performance parameters, the source device processes and adapts the video signal to fit within those exact limits before transmitting the signal over the cable.
  3. Optimized Signal Delivery: The display receives a pre-adjusted signal, eliminating the need for aggressive, broad-brush tone mapping on the display side, which often leads to clipped highlights or crushed shadows.

SBTM does not necessarily replace the display's native tone mapping capabilities; instead, it works in tandem with them. A display can still apply tone mapping, but SBTM ensures that the signal it receives is already optimized for its native panel performance.

Solving the Mixed-Content Problem

The primary problem SBTM aims to solve is the presentation of mixed HDR and SDR content on a single screen.

Without SBTM, a system outputting mixed content—such as a PC displaying an HDR game alongside an SDR chat window, or a set-top box showing an SDR electronic program guide (EPG) over an HDR broadcast—must choose a single tone mapping approach for the entire display. This frequently causes SDR graphics to appear washed out or overly bright, or HDR elements to lose dynamic range.

Because the source device understands the structure of its own operating system and software windows, SBTM enables the source to tone-map individual elements independently. The source adjusts the HDR window, properly maps the SDR interface, and outputs a composite, unified video stream where every element maintains its intended brightness and color accuracy.

Implementation and Compatibility

HDMI 2.1a specifies SBTM as an optional feature for manufacturers. Because it is largely a software- and protocol-level communication layer, it can often be added via firmware updates to existing HDMI 2.1 hardware, provided the source and sink manufacturers choose to support it. To take advantage of SBTM, both the source device and the connected display must support the feature.
