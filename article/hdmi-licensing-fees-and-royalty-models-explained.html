HDMI Licensing Fees and Royalty Models Explained

This article outlines the licensing costs, royalty models, and testing requirements hardware manufacturers must fulfill to incorporate HDMI technology into their products. Manufacturers must become official HDMI Adopters, pay mandatory annual fees, submit per-unit royalties based on specific implementation criteria, and undergo compliance testing, alongside potential third-party content protection licensing fees.

The HDMI Adopter Agreement and Annual Fees

To legally manufacture and sell products utilizing HDMI technology, a company must sign the HDMI Adopter Agreement administered by HDMI Licensing Administrator, Inc. (HDMI LA).

Becoming an Adopter requires an annual membership fee:

  • Standard Annual Fee: $10,000 per year, paid annually on the anniversary of the agreement.
  • Low-Volume Alternative: Historically, a lower annual fee ($5,000) existed for manufacturers producing fewer than 10,000 units annually, though standard mass-market hardware manufacturers operate under the standard $10,000 annual fee.

Per-Unit Royalty Rates

In addition to the annual fee, manufacturers must pay a royalty on every end-user licensed product sold. The royalty rate depends on whether the manufacturer meets specific marketing and technical criteria:

  • Base Royalty Rate: $0.15 per unit if the product does not display the HDMI logo.
  • Logo Incentive Rate: $0.05 per unit if the manufacturer places the official HDMI logo on the product and all marketing materials.
  • HDCP and Logo Incentive Rate: $0.04 per unit if the product includes the HDMI logo and implements High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP).

Royalties are paid quarterly and apply only to finished end products (such as televisions, graphics cards, monitors, or Blu-ray players). Component manufacturers selling intermediate parts, such as silicon chips or connectors, do not pay per-unit royalties, provided their buyers are licensed HDMI Adopters.

Compliance Testing and Certification Costs

Before commercially releasing an HDMI product, manufacturers must verify compliance:

  • Authorized Test Centers (ATCs): A manufacturer must submit the first product of each product category (e.g., source, sink, cable, or repeater) to an official ATC for compliance testing. Fees for ATC testing are paid directly to the testing facility and typically range from $1,000 to several thousand dollars per device, depending on complexity.
  • Self-Testing: Once the first product in a category passes ATC testing, manufacturers are permitted to self-test subsequent products within that same category, eliminating ongoing third-party lab fees for minor product variations.

HDCP Licensing Requirements

While technically separate from HDMI LA, almost all commercial HDMI implementations require HDCP to transmit protected commercial video content. HDCP is governed by Digital Content Protection LLC (DCP):

  • Annual HDCP Fee: $15,000 per year.
  • Key Purchase Costs: Manufacturers must purchase cryptographic key sets from DCP LLC, which cost fractions of a cent per key set depending on order volumes.
