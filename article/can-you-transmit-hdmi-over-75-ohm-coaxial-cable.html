Can You Transmit HDMI Over 75-Ohm Coaxial Cable?

HDMI video signals can be successfully transmitted over standard 75-ohm coaxial cable systems, but not through a simple passive wire adapter. Because HDMI relies on multi-channel, high-bandwidth digital data, converting it to travel over a single-conductor coaxial cable requires active electronic conversion. By utilizing dedicated HDMI-over-coax extenders, digital RF modulators, or SDI converters, you can repurpose existing RG6 or RG59 coaxial infrastructure to carry high-definition audio and video over distances that far exceed standard HDMI cable limitations.

Why Direct Passive Adapters Do Not Work

A standard HDMI cable contains 19 individual pins carrying high-speed Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) data lanes, clock signals, consumer electronics control (CEC), and High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP) handshakes. A 75-ohm coaxial cable (such as RG6 or RG59) consists of a single center conductor, a dielectric insulator, and outer shielding. Because the signal architectures are entirely incompatible, you cannot simply splice an HDMI cable into a coaxial connector; you must use an active powered device to encode the signal at the source and decode it at the display.

Methods for Transmitting HDMI Over Coax

There are three primary ways to send HDMI over standard coaxial cabling:

  1. Point-to-Point HDMI-over-Coax Extenders These systems consist of a dedicated transmitter and receiver pair. The transmitter converts the HDMI signal into a proprietary high-frequency digital signal, transmits it down the coaxial line, and the receiver reconverts it into an HDMI output.

    • Best For: Connecting a single video source directly to a single display.
    • Performance: Very low to zero latency, support for up to 1080p or 4K resolutions depending on the model, and reach of up to 300 to 500 feet over quality RG6 cable.

  2. Digital RF Modulators Digital RF modulators convert an incoming HDMI signal into a standard digital broadcast channel (such as QAM, ATSC, or DVB-T). The output is injected directly into an existing coaxial television distribution network. Any television connected to the coax network can tune to the selected channel to watch the video source.

    • Best For: Distributing a single video source (such as a cable box, digital signage player, or security NVR) to dozens or hundreds of televisions simultaneously using standard RF splitters.
    • Performance: Introduces noticeable latency (often 200–1000 milliseconds, making it unsuitable for gaming or PC interaction), with resolutions typically capped at 1080p.

  3. HDMI-to-SDI Converters In professional broadcast environments, HDMI signals are frequently converted to Serial Digital Interface (SDI) formats (like 3G-SDI, 6G-SDI, or 12G-SDI). SDI is natively designed to operate across standard 75-ohm coaxial lines using BNC connectors.

    • Best For: Professional video production, live events, and commercial installations requiring robust locking connectors and zero latency.
    • Performance: Exceptional stability, zero latency, and cable runs up to 300 feet for 1080p (longer with repeaters), though it typically strips or fails to process HDCP-protected consumer content.

Cable Quality and Performance Considerations

  • RG6 vs. RG59: RG6 cable features a larger center conductor and thicker shielding, making it the preferred choice for long-distance digital signal transmission. While older RG59 cable will work for shorter runs, it suffers from higher signal attenuation at high frequencies, which can cause dropouts or limit maximum resolution.
  • Solid Copper vs. Copper-Clad Steel (CCS): For active extender sets that power the receiver unit via the coaxial line (Power over Coax or PoC), solid bare copper center conductors are essential. Copper-clad steel has higher DC resistance and may cause power delivery failures.
  • HDCP Compatibility: When distributing commercial media (such as Blu-ray discs or streaming devices), ensure the chosen converter hardware supports HDCP compliance; otherwise, the display will show a black screen due to digital copyright handshaking failures.
