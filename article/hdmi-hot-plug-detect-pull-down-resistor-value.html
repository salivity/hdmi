HDMI Hot Plug Detect Pull-Down Resistor Value

The HDMI Hot Plug Detect (HPD) line relies on voltage-level transitions to notify a Source device when a Sink (display) is connected or disconnected. To reliably detect a disconnection, the HDMI Source must implement a pull-down resistor that drains residual voltage and pulls the floating signal to ground. According to standard HDMI design practices and compliance specifications, this pull-down resistor typically requires a nominal value of 100 kΩ (with an acceptable functional range between 10 kΩ and 100 kΩ).

How HPD Disconnection Signaling Works

The HPD mechanism operates on Pin 19 of the standard HDMI Type-A connector.

  • Connected State: The Sink asserts a logical high signal by asserting a voltage between 2.4 V and 5.0 V (typically outputting +5 V through an internal resistor, often 1 kΩ).
  • Disconnected State: When the HDMI cable is unplugged, the Sink stops driving the line. Without an active pull-down on the Source, Pin 19 would float, causing unpredictable behavior, signal bouncing, or false assertions of an active link.

The Source-side pull-down resistor ties Pin 19 to ground (GND), ensuring that when the Sink disconnects, the line rapidly drops below the logical low threshold (\(V_{IL} \le 0.4\text{ V}\) to \(0.8\text{ V}\), depending on the receiver IC logic).

Recommended Resistor Value and Design Tolerances

While the HDMI specification mandates that a Source device must pull the HPD line to ground, hardware implementations must balance signal decay speed and current consumption:

  • Nominal Standard Value: 100 kΩ is the industry-standard recommendation across most HDMI receiver and transmitter datasheets.
  • Acceptable Range: 10 kΩ to 100 kΩ is commonly utilized in production hardware.
  • Alternative Value (Fast Discharges): 47 kΩ is frequently used when routing high-capacitance traces or when ESD protection diodes add extra capacitance that must be discharged quickly to meet strict timing windows.

Why Values Outside 10 kΩ to 100 kΩ Are Problematic

Choosing a resistor outside the recommended range causes signaling failures:

  • Values lower than 10 kΩ: A pull-down that is too strong creates a voltage divider with the Sink's internal pull-up (often ~1 kΩ). If the pull-down resistance is too low, the voltage at Pin 19 may drop below the required 2.4 V threshold even when connected, preventing the display from being detected.
  • Values higher than 100 kΩ (or floating): High values (such as > 1 MΩ) slow down the discharge of parasitic trace capacitance and ESD diodes. This delay prolongs the transition below the low threshold (\(V_{IL}\)), leading to delayed disconnect recognition or floating logic errors caused by electromagnetic interference (EMI).
