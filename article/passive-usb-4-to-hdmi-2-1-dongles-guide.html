Passive USB4 to HDMI 2.1 Dongles Guide

Connecting a USB4 port to an external HDMI 2.1 display requires converting high-bandwidth video streams between two distinct transmission standards. While many users search for passive dongles to achieve this connection, purely passive adapters cannot bridge USB4 to HDMI 2.1. This article explains the technical limitations of USB4 display output, why passive conversion is impossible for HDMI 2.1 features, and what hardware is actually required.

The Problem with Passive Adapters

USB4 handles external displays by tunneling native DisplayPort (DP) signals or utilizing DisplayPort Alternate Mode over the USB Type-C connector. Historically, passive DisplayPort-to-HDMI adapters relied on Dual-Mode DisplayPort (DP++). In a DP++ setup, the host GPU detects a passive adapter and changes its physical output to emit HDMI-compatible TMDS (Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling) signals at standard HDMI voltages.

However, DP++ does not support HDMI 2.1. HDMI 2.1 relies on Fixed Rate Link (FRL) signaling to achieve the bandwidth necessary for resolutions such as 4K at 120Hz or 8K at 60Hz. Because modern GPUs and USB4 host controllers do not output native HDMI 2.1 FRL signals over USB-C, a passive wire-to-wire pin adapter cannot deliver an HDMI 2.1 signal.

Why Active Conversion Is Mandatory

To output video from a USB4 port to an HDMI 2.1 screen, the adapter must be an active protocol converter. An active converter receives the DisplayPort stream (typically DisplayPort 1.4 with Display Stream Compression or DisplayPort 2.1) tunneled through USB4, decodes it, and re-encodes the data into an HDMI 2.1 FRL signal.

These adapters rely on specialized internal chipsets, such as the Synaptics VMM6100/VMM7100 series or the Realtek RTD2173. These integrated circuits perform the complex signal translation, clock recovery, and packet management required by the HDMI 2.1 specification.

Identifying the Right Adapter

Any dongle on the market that successfully outputs full HDMI 2.1 bandwidth (supporting features like 4K @ 120Hz, 8K @ 60Hz, Variable Refresh Rate, and HDR) from a USB4 or Thunderbolt port is an active adapter, even if it looks like a compact, single-piece cable or dongle. These devices draw the small amount of power needed to run their internal conversion chips directly from the USB4 port's VBUS line, eliminating the need for an external power brick while remaining active hardware.
