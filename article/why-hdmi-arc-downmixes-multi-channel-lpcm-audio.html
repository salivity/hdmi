Why HDMI ARC Downmixes Multi-Channel LPCM Audio

Connecting a PC to a television via standard HDMI Audio Return Channel (ARC) often results in multi-channel uncompressed Linear PCM (LPCM) 5.1 or 7.1 audio being downmixed to 2.0 stereo or muted entirely. This issue occurs primarily because standard HDMI ARC is bound by legacy protocol bandwidth constraints inherited from S/PDIF, which physically cannot carry more than two channels of uncompressed digital audio. Consequently, the display communicates this limitation back to the operating system, forcing the computer to drop multi-channel playback or downmix surround audio into stereo.

The S/PDIF Heritage and Bandwidth Limits

Standard HDMI ARC was introduced with HDMI 1.4 to eliminate the need for dedicated optical audio cables between a TV and an audio receiver or soundbar. To maintain backward compatibility and low implementation costs, ARC was built directly on the S/PDIF (Sony/Philips Digital Interface) protocol standard.

Because of this architectural design, standard ARC has a maximum transmission bandwidth of roughly 1 to 1.5 Megabits per second (Mbps). While this bandwidth is sufficient for a 2-channel, 16-bit/48kHz uncompressed LPCM audio track, uncompressed multi-channel audio requires significantly more throughput:

  • 2.0 LPCM (16-bit/48kHz): Requires ~1.5 Mbps (fits within ARC limits).
  • 5.1 LPCM (16-bit/48kHz): Requires ~4.6 Mbps (exceeds ARC limits).
  • 7.1 LPCM (24-bit/48kHz): Requires ~9.2 Mbps (greatly exceeds ARC limits).

Because the physical data link for standard ARC cannot support the 4.6 Mbps or higher needed for 5.1 or 7.1 LPCM, sending raw multi-channel audio across the link without downmixing is impossible.

EDID Handshake and Windows Audio Behavior

When you connect a PC to a television via HDMI, the two devices perform an Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) handshake. The TV reports its display capabilities and supported audio formats back to the graphics card and operating system.

When standard ARC is in the signal path:

  1. The TV recognizes that its upstream ARC port only supports 2-channel LPCM.
  2. The TV adjusts its EDID table to declare a maximum of two channels for uncompressed PCM to the PC.
  3. Windows reads the EDID and locks the audio output device configuration to "Stereo," graying out 5.1 or 7.1 surround options.
  4. If a PC application tries to send multi-channel audio, Windows or the media player automatically downmixes the surround channels into the left and right speakers to prevent a complete loss of sound. If downmixing fails or is unsupported by the software, the audio stream is refused, resulting in complete silence.

Why Movies Work but PC Games Fail

Standard ARC can pass multi-channel 5.1 surround sound if the stream is pre-compressed into lossy formats like standard Dolby Digital (AC-3) or DTS. These compressed bitstreams take up less than 640 kbps, easily fitting within ARC's 1.5 Mbps limit.

However, PC audio behaves differently than a streaming media box or a Blu-ray player:

  • Pre-rendered media (Movies/TV): Audio is already compressed into Dolby Digital or DTS files. The PC can simply pass the encoded bitstream directly through the TV to the receiver.
  • Real-time audio (PC Games/Desktop): PC games render sound effects in real time and output them as uncompressed, multi-channel LPCM. Because games do not natively encode this audio into a compressed Dolby Digital bitstream on the fly, the audio hits the ARC bandwidth bottleneck and gets downmixed to stereo.

Unless your sound setup supports a real-time encoder (such as Dolby Digital Live or DTS Interactive) to compress the PC's uncompressed game audio into a bitstream before output, standard ARC cannot transport the surround channels.

Overcoming the Limitation

To receive true, multi-channel uncompressed LPCM audio from a PC without downmixing, you must eliminate the standard ARC bottleneck using one of two methods:

  • HDMI eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel): Introduced with HDMI 2.1, eARC increases audio bandwidth up to 37 Mbps. This is more than enough bandwidth to transmit full 5.1 and 7.1 uncompressed 24-bit/192kHz LPCM audio from the PC through the TV to the sound system. Both the TV and the soundbar/receiver must support eARC.
  • Direct Connection: Route the PC’s HDMI output directly into an HDMI input on the AV receiver or soundbar first, and then send a second HDMI cable from the receiver's output to the TV for video. This completely bypasses the ARC return path and allows the receiver to process the PC's multi-channel LPCM stream directly.
