HDMI TMDS Voltage Swing Levels Explained

Transition Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) is the core physical layer technology used in HDMI to transmit high-speed digital audio and video data. To maintain signal integrity and ensure interoperability across various transmitters, cables, and sinks, the HDMI TMDS electrical specification sets strict Current Mode Logic (CML) amplitude boundaries. This article details the four discrete voltage swing thresholds defined within the HDMI TMDS electrical specification, covering both single-ended and differential domains.

The Four Discrete Voltage Swing Levels

The HDMI TMDS electrical specification defines the transmitter output requirements using four discrete voltage swing boundaries that govern single-ended and differential peak-to-peak amplitudes:

  1. 400 mV (Minimum Single-Ended Output Swing, \(V_{SWING, min}\))
    This is the lowest allowable single-ended voltage transition on either individual line (\(D+\) or \(D-\)) of a differential pair. When a TMDS driver transitions from high to low, the signal must drop by at least 400 mV below the high-level reference voltage (\(AV_{CC}\)).

  2. 600 mV (Maximum Single-Ended Output Swing, \(V_{SWING, max}\))
    This represents the maximum permissible single-ended voltage drop on an individual line. Keeping the single-ended drop between 400 mV and 600 mV ensures a nominal single-ended swing of 500 mV, preventing excessive electromagnetic interference (EMI) and signal overshoot.

  3. 800 mV (Minimum Differential Peak-to-Peak Swing, \(V_{DIFF, min}\))
    Calculated as twice the minimum single-ended swing (\(2 \times 400\text{ mV}\)), 800 mV peak-to-peak is the minimum differential amplitude required across the differential pair. This ensures that the HDMI sink receiver can reliably resolve logic states even after high-frequency cable attenuation.

  4. 1200 mV (Maximum Differential Peak-to-Peak Swing, \(V_{DIFF, max}\))
    Calculated as twice the maximum single-ended swing (\(2 \times 600\text{ mV}\)), 1200 mV peak-to-peak is the upper limit for the differential voltage across the pair. With a nominal differential swing of 1000 mV, this ceiling prevents receiver saturation and cross-talk between adjacent TMDS channels.

Corresponding DC Voltage Rails

These four swing limits operate around fixed DC voltage levels established by the 3.3V termination rail (\(AV_{CC}\)):

  • Single-Ended High (\(V_{OH}\)): Nominally \(3.3\text{ V}\) (\(AV_{CC} \pm 10\text{ mV}\)).
  • Single-Ended Low (\(V_{OL}\)): Nominally \(2.8\text{ V}\) (\(AV_{CC} - 500\text{ mV}\)), bounded strictly between \(2.7\text{ V}\) (\(AV_{CC} - 600\text{ mV}\)) and \(2.9\text{ V}\) (\(AV_{CC} - 400\text{ mV}\)).
  • Differential Logic High: Driven when \(D+\) is at \(V_{OH}\) and \(D-\) is at \(V_{OL}\), creating a positive differential voltage (\(+400\text{ mV}\) to \(+600\text{ mV}\)).
  • Differential Logic Low: Driven when \(D+\) is at \(V_{OL}\) and \(D-\) is at \(V_{OH}\), creating an inverted differential voltage (\(-400\text{ mV}\) to \(-600\text{ mV}\)).
