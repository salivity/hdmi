HDMI Type A TMDS Pinout Configuration

This article provides an overview and technical breakdown of the Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) pinout within a standard 19-pin HDMI Type A receptacle. The TMDS architecture carries high-speed digital video, audio, and auxiliary data across four differential pairs. Below, you will find the precise pin assignments, polarities, and shielding arrangements for pins 1 through 12, which encompass all primary TMDS channels.

TMDS Architecture in HDMI Type A

HDMI Type A connectors utilize four discrete TMDS communication lanes: three data channels (Data 0, Data 1, and Data 2) and one independent clock channel. Each lane is implemented as a differential pair comprising a positive line (+), a negative line (-), and a dedicated ground shield to reduce electromagnetic interference (EMI) and signal crosstalk.

These channels occupy the first 12 pins of the 19-pin receptacle layout.

TMDS Pinout Breakdown (Pins 1–12)

The 12 TMDS pins are organized sequentially into alternating groups of positive signal, ground shield, and negative signal:

TMDS Data Channel 2

  • Pin 1: TMDS Data2+ (Carries the positive differential signal for red video components and auxiliary data)
  • Pin 2: TMDS Data2 Shield (Dedicated ground/shield for Data Channel 2)
  • Pin 3: TMDS Data2- (Carries the negative differential signal for red video components and auxiliary data)

TMDS Data Channel 1

  • Pin 4: TMDS Data1+ (Carries the positive differential signal for green video components and auxiliary data)
  • Pin 5: TMDS Data1 Shield (Dedicated ground/shield for Data Channel 1)
  • Pin 6: TMDS Data1- (Carries the negative differential signal for green video components and auxiliary data)

TMDS Data Channel 0

  • Pin 7: TMDS Data0+ (Carries the positive differential signal for blue video components, sync pulses, and auxiliary data)
  • Pin 8: TMDS Data0 Shield (Dedicated ground/shield for Data Channel 0)
  • Pin 9: TMDS Data0- (Carries the negative differential signal for blue video components, sync pulses, and auxiliary data)

TMDS Clock Channel

  • Pin 10: TMDS Clock+ (Carries the positive differential clock signal used for pixel synchronization)
  • Pin 11: TMDS Clock Shield (Dedicated ground/shield for the Clock Channel)
  • Pin 12: TMDS Clock- (Carries the negative differential clock signal used for pixel synchronization)

Electrical Design Considerations

The physical geometry of the HDMI Type A receptacle places the shield pin directly between the positive and negative conductors of adjacent channels rather than between the complementary pairs themselves. This interleaving pattern ensures impedance matching (typically 100 ohms differential) and minimizes skew across high-frequency transmissions up to the maximum data rates defined by the HDMI specification. Pins 13 through 19 handle low-speed control functions (CEC, DDC, HPD, and power) and do not carry TMDS data.
