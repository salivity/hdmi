Closed Captioning and Teletext in HDMI Signals

This article provides an overview of how High-Definition Multimedia Interface (HDMI) signals transport closed captioning and teletext data. While consumer HDMI sources commonly decode and overlay caption text directly onto the video raster, the HDMI specification also supports carrying raw caption and teletext data within auxiliary data structures known as Data Island periods. Below is a detailed look at the signal architecture, packet structures, and CTA-861 standards that make this transmission possible.

HDMI Transmission Modes and the Data Island

An HDMI link operates across transition-minimized differential signaling (TMDS) channels or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) lanes using three primary transmission periods:

  1. Video Data Period: Transmits the active uncompressed video pixels.
  2. Control Period: Used for synchronization signals (such as HSync and VSync) and preamble patterns.
  3. Data Island Period: Occurs during the horizontal and vertical blanking intervals when active video pixels are not being transmitted.

Auxiliary data, including multi-channel audio, InfoFrames, and ancillary vertical blanking data, is strictly transported during the Data Island period.

Auxiliary Packet Structure

Data sent during the Data Island period is organized into discrete 32-byte units called Data Island Packets. Each packet consists of:

  • Header (4 bytes): Contains packet type identification and payload-specific flags, protected by a BCH(32, 24) error-correcting code.
  • Payload (28 bytes): Divided into four subpackets of 7 bytes each, protected by parity bits and a BCH(64, 56) code.

To survive transmission across high-frequency TMDS lines, these packets use specialized error reduction coding (TERC4), ensuring robust delivery of non-video data.

Encapsulating Closed Captioning and Teletext

When raw closed captioning (such as CEA-608 or CEA-708) or teletext (World Standard Teletext / WST) is preserved as data rather than rendered into pixels, it is encapsulated using the CTA-861 standard extensions referenced by the HDMI specification:

  • VBI Data Packets: The Consumer Technology Association (CTA-861) defines methods to carry digitized Vertical Blanking Interval (VBI) services. Line 21 data (CEA-608 closed captions) and teletext waveforms can be digitized into byte representations and placed into dedicated VBI Data InfoFrames or Ancillary Data Packets.
  • SMPTE 291M / VANC Mapping: In broadcast and professional AV workflows, HDMI transmitters encapsulate Vertical Ancillary (VANC) data packets. Caption structures (such as SMPTE 334M carrying CEA-708) are placed directly into HDMI auxiliary packets, allowing downstream devices (like professional monitors or recording units) to parse the raw metadata.

Passthrough vs. Video Rendering

In practice, HDMI handles closed captions and teletext through two distinct methods depending on the hardware implementation:

  • Source-Rendered Bitmaps (Consumer Standard): In typical consumer configurations (streaming devices, Blu-ray players, set-top boxes), the source hardware decodes the closed captioning or teletext stream internally. It renders the characters into the active pixel raster before the HDMI output stage. In this case, the captions are part of the Video Data Period rather than auxiliary structures.
  • Auxiliary Passthrough (Sink Decoding): When the source acts strictly as a transport pipeline, the raw text bytes are packaged into Data Island packets during the blanking intervals. The receiving display (sink device) parses the auxiliary packets, extracts the CEA-608/708 or teletext payload, and uses its internal character generator to render the captions on screen.
