HDMI eARC Discovery and Capability Exchange Explained

HDMI Enhanced Audio Return Channel (eARC) provides the bandwidth necessary for uncompressed, object-based audio formats like Dolby TrueHD and DTS:X between a display and an audio receiver. Central to this process is the eARC Data Channel (eADC), a dedicated bidirectional communication path that automates device discovery and negotiates audio capabilities. This article details how connected devices detect each other, establish the link via hardware-level heartbeats, and exchange capability structures using the eADC before any audio data flows across the cable.

The Physical Medium: HEAC Lines

Unlike legacy HDMI ARC, which relies on consumer electronics control (CEC) over a single-ended line, eARC operates over the HDMI Ethernet and Audio Return Channel (HEAC) differential pair:

  • Pin 14 (Utility / HEAC+)
  • Pin 19 (Hot Plug Detect / HEAC-)

These two pins serve a dual purpose. They carry the high-speed differential audio signal from the eARC Transmitter (TX, typically a TV) to the eARC Receiver (RX, typically an AVR or soundbar), while concurrently supporting the low-speed differential signaling used for the eARC Data Channel (eADC).

Step 1: Device Discovery and Heartbeat Detection

The discovery process ensures that eARC activates only when both connected devices explicitly support it, preventing signal corruption on legacy HDMI ports.

  1. Idle State and Differential Biasing: By default, standard HDMI Hot Plug Detect (HPD) signaling operates on Pin 19. When an eARC-capable connection is initialized, the eARC TX applies a specific common-mode voltage to the HEAC lines while monitoring for differential responses.
  2. Heartbeat Generation: Discovery is governed by a low-frequency, bidirectional "Heartbeat" signal. The eARC RX and TX transmit alternating pulses across the HEAC pair at predefined intervals.
  3. Link Verification: Once both the TX and RX detect valid heartbeat patterns from each other, they confirm the presence of an active eARC peer. The hardware transitions out of the idle/discovery state into the connected state, enabling the eADC protocol without requiring HDMI CEC to broker the connection.

Step 2: The eARC Data Channel (eADC) Setup

Once discovery is confirmed, communication shifts to the eADC. The eADC is a half-duplex, packet-based, bidirectional link operating over the same differential lines at a lower carrier frequency than the incoming high-speed audio stream.

The eADC handles all link management tasks:

  • Device state synchronization
  • Capability verification
  • Audio latency (lip-sync) reporting
  • Real-time link status monitoring

Because the eADC operates independently of the HDMI CEC line, it eliminates the manufacturer-specific handshake incompatibilities that frequently affected legacy ARC.

Step 3: Capability Exchange via the Capability Data Structure (CDS)

With the eADC active, the TX must determine what audio formats the RX can decode. This exchange relies on the Capability Data Structure (CDS), an eARC-specific equivalent to EDID (Extended Display Identification Data).

  1. Read Request: The eARC TX initiates an eADC read transaction targeting the RX's internal registers.
  2. Payload Delivery: The RX compiles its CDS and sends it to the TX over the eADC. This data structure contains:
    • Supported audio coding types (e.g., LPCM, Dolby Digital Plus, Dolby TrueHD/Atmos, DTS-HD Master Audio, DTS:X).
    • Maximum channel counts (up to 8 channels of uncompressed audio).
    • Supported sampling frequencies (ranging from 32 kHz up to 192 kHz) and bit depths (16-bit, 20-bit, 24-bit).
    • Audio latency parameters, allowing the TV to dynamically adjust video delay for precise lip-sync correction.
  3. Parsing and Configuration: The TX parses the CDS, matches it against the audio stream coming from the broadcast, built-in apps, or external HDMI inputs, and configures the audio encoder/pass-through engine accordingly.

Step 4: Audio Transmission and Continuous Monitoring

After the TX validates the CDS, it enables the high-bandwidth audio differential transmitter, sending audio down the HEAC lines to the RX.

Throughout playback, the eADC remains active in the background. The devices continuously exchange status packets and heartbeats. If the RX changes its input mode, reports a change in audio latency, or if the cable is disconnected, the eADC immediately alerts the TX to mute the stream, renegotiate the CDS, or safely revert the pins to standard HDMI HPD behavior.
