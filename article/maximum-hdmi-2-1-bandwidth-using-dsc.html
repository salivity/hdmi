Maximum HDMI 2.1 Bandwidth Using DSC

This article provides a technical overview of HDMI 2.1 bandwidth capabilities when combined with Display Stream Compression (DSC). While the physical connection remains capped at 48 Gbps, DSC allows the transmission of video feeds that would typically require significantly higher data rates. Below, we examine the physical limits of HDMI 2.1, how DSC multiplies effective throughput, and the maximum equivalent bandwidth achievable.

Physical Bandwidth vs. Effective Bandwidth

The physical architecture of HDMI 2.1 utilizes Fixed Rate Link (FRL) signaling, delivering a maximum raw transmission rate of 48 Gbps across four data lanes (12 Gbps per lane). Accounting for 16b/18b encoding overhead, the maximum usable uncompressed data payload is approximately 42.66 Gbps. Utilizing DSC does not increase the physical signaling rate over the cable; instead, it compresses the video data prior to transmission so that higher-resolution signals can traverse the fixed 48 Gbps pipeline.

The Role of Display Stream Compression (DSC 1.2a)

HDMI 2.1 incorporates the VESA Display Stream Compression (DSC) 1.2a standard. DSC is a visually lossless, low-latency compression algorithm specifically designed for real-time display interfaces. The standard defines a target compression ratio of up to 3:1 (and up to 3.75:1 depending on color format and slice configurations), reducing the required bit rate for high-resolution, high-refresh-rate video streams without introducing perceptible image artifacts or input lag.

Maximum Supported Compressed Bandwidth

When operating at the maximum 3:1 compression ratio supported by the standard:

  • Equivalent Raw Bandwidth: Multiplying the physical 48 Gbps link by the 3:1 ratio yields an effective equivalent throughput of 144 Gbps.
  • Equivalent Usable Data Payload: Multiplying the effective data payload of 42.66 Gbps by the 3:1 ratio delivers an effective uncompressed payload capacity of approximately 128 Gbps.

Practical Capabilities

This expanded effective throughput allows HDMI 2.1 to transport extreme formats that exceed the uncompressed 48 Gbps boundary. With DSC active, HDMI 2.1 can deliver:

  • 8K (7680 × 4320) at 60 Hz or 120 Hz with 10-bit or 12-bit 4:4:4 HDR color.
  • 10K (10240 × 4320) ultra-wide video at up to 120 Hz.
  • 4K (3840 × 2160) at refresh rates up to 240 Hz with uncompromised chroma and bit depth.

Both the video source (such as a modern GPU or console) and the sink device (such as a TV or monitor) must support HDMI 2.1 and DSC 1.2a to negotiate this compressed pipeline.
