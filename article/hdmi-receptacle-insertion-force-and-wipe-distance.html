HDMI Receptacle Insertion Force and Wipe Distance

This article provides a technical overview of the mechanical mating specifications for certified standard HDMI (Type A) receptacles. It details the precise insertion force limits and contact wipe distance tolerances defined by the HDMI Compliance Test Specification (CTS). Adhering to these mechanical parameters ensures interoperability, connector longevity, and reliable electrical contact across all compliant HDMI consumer electronics and commercial hardware.

Insertion Force Specifications

For a standard certified HDMI Type A receptacle, the insertion force is defined by an absolute maximum limit rather than a loose bidirectional tolerance:

  • Maximum Insertion Force: 44.1 N (4.5 kgf / 9.91 lbf)

During mechanical compliance testing, the plug must engage with the receptacle at a controlled speed—typically 25 mm (±3 mm) per minute. The peak force measured throughout full engagement cannot exceed 44.1 N.

To maintain proper retention, the specification also mandates withdrawal (unmating) force limits:

  • Initial Withdrawal Force: 9.8 N (1.0 kgf) minimum to 39.2 N (4.0 kgf) maximum.
  • Post-Durability Withdrawal Force: 4.9 N (0.5 kgf) minimum to 39.2 N (4.0 kgf) maximum (after undergoing the standard 10,000 mating cycle test).

Contact Wipe Distance

The contact wipe distance—the distance over which the spring contact of the receptacle slides along the mating pad of the plug during insertion—is critical for cutting through surface oxidation, debris, and contaminants:

  • Minimum Contact Wipe: 1.00 mm

While mechanical drawings define individual contact beam geometries, lead-in chamfers, and shell depths, the CTS requires that the primary signal pins (TMDS/FRL lines) and power/ground pins achieve at least 1.00 mm of sliding contact engagement before the connector is fully seated. This minimum wipe guarantees a self-cleaning action that preserves low contact resistance (maximum 30 mΩ initial change per contact) over the connector’s operational lifespan.
