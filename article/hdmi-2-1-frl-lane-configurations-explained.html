HDMI 2.1 FRL Lane Configurations Explained

Fixed Rate Link (FRL) is the signaling technology introduced in HDMI 2.1 to replace legacy TMDS (Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling), enabling bandwidths up to 48 Gbps. While the HDMI 2.1 specification supports both 3-lane and 4-lane structures, the system defines four selectable 4-lane FRL configurations designed to deliver higher data rates by utilizing all four differential pairs for serialized data with embedded clocking. Below is an overview of these four selectable 4-lane configurations, their throughput, and their target display capabilities.

1. 4 Lanes at 6 Gbps (24 Gbps Total Bandwidth)

In this configuration, each of the four data lanes operates at a fixed rate of 6 Gbps, yielding an aggregate raw bandwidth of 24 Gbps. Using the 16b/18b encoding scheme of FRL, this delivers an effective payload rate of approximately 21.33 Gbps. This level provides sufficient throughput for uncompressed 4K video at 60 Hz with 10-bit or 12-bit color, as well as 4K at 120 Hz when paired with VESA Display Stream Compression (DSC).

2. 4 Lanes at 8 Gbps (32 Gbps Total Bandwidth)

Operating at 8 Gbps per lane, this setup achieves an aggregate bandwidth of 32 Gbps, resulting in an effective data throughput of approximately 28.44 Gbps after encoding overhead. This configuration natively supports uncompressed 4K video at 120 Hz using 8-bit color depth (4:2:0 or 4:2:2 chroma subsampling), making it suitable for mid-tier high-refresh-rate gaming setups and high-bandwidth multimedia playback.

3. 4 Lanes at 10 Gbps (40 Gbps Total Bandwidth)

Each lane in this configuration transfers data at 10 Gbps, providing a cumulative raw throughput of 40 Gbps (approximately 35.56 Gbps effective). Widely implemented on modern home gaming consoles and premium consumer televisions, this mode supports uncompressed 4K at 120 Hz with 10-bit HDR and 4:4:4 uncompressed chroma, as well as baseline 8K resolutions at 60 Hz when using DSC.

4. 4 Lanes at 12 Gbps (48 Gbps Total Bandwidth)

Representing the maximum transmission capability of the HDMI 2.1 specification, this configuration operates all four lanes at 12 Gbps to reach a peak raw bandwidth of 48 Gbps (approximately 42.67 Gbps effective). This tier enables uncompressed 4K at 120 Hz with full 12-bit color, uncompressed 8K at 30 Hz, and up to 8K at 60 Hz or 10K at 120 Hz with DSC.

Negotiation via Link Training

The selection of these lane configurations occurs dynamically during the HDMI Link Training status and control protocol. The source device and sink (display) negotiate link capabilities via the Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) and Display Data Channel (DDC). If the cable or sink cannot reliably sustain the signal integrity required for higher configurations like 48 Gbps, the system steps down to a lower stable FRL configuration (such as 40 Gbps, 32 Gbps, or 24 Gbps) to ensure an uninterrupted audiovisual signal.
