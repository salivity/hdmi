What Is HDMI 2.1b? Features and Specs Explained

HDMI 2.1b is the latest maintenance update to the HDMI 2.1 standard, designed to refine technical specifications, resolve implementation ambiguities, and improve interoperability across modern audiovisual hardware. Rather than increasing maximum bandwidth or introducing radical new physical hardware changes, HDMI 2.1b provides essential clarifications for manufacturers regarding Source-Based Tone Mapping (SBTM), high-refresh-rate signaling, and compliance testing protocols.

Understanding the HDMI 2.1b Revision

Released by the HDMI Forum, HDMI 2.1b serves as an incremental refinement of HDMI 2.1a. It maintains the core bandwidth capabilities established by HDMI 2.1—up to 48 Gbps over Fixed Rate Link (FRL)—which supports uncompressed resolutions up to 8K at 60Hz and 4K at 120Hz, as well as up to 10K with Display Stream Compression (DSC).

Because HDMI 2.1 introduced a wide array of advanced features that led to inconsistent implementations across displays, consoles, and AV receivers, HDMI 2.1b was published primarily to tighten compliance specifications and eliminate technical gray areas.

Key Technical Clarifications Introduced in HDMI 2.1b

1. Source-Based Tone Mapping (SBTM) Optimization

First introduced in HDMI 2.1a, Source-Based Tone Mapping allows the source device (such as a PC or gaming console) to handle a portion of the HDR processing instead of relying exclusively on the display. HDMI 2.1b introduces technical clarifications on how tone-mapping metadata is communicated between devices. This reduces clipping and improves picture consistency when mixing standard dynamic range (SDR) and high dynamic range (HDR) graphics in a single frame, such as picture-in-picture windows or in-game user interfaces.

2. Compliance Test Specification (CTS) Updates

A major contribution of HDMI 2.1b is the revision of test procedures used by authorized test centers (ATCs). The update clarifies test criteria for:

  • Fixed Rate Link (FRL): Stricter signaling tolerances to ensure seamless handshakes between sources, repeaters (like soundbars or AVRs), and displays.
  • Variable Refresh Rate (VRR): Standardized parameters to minimize frame drops, flicker, and blackouts during sudden framerate fluctuations in gaming.
  • Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM) and eARC: Refined command protocols to ensure connected audio devices switch states predictably without audio delay or dropout.

3. Cable Testing and Certification Clarifications

HDMI 2.1b reinforces the testing rules for "Ultra High Speed HDMI" cables. It clarifies signal integrity requirements for passive and active copper cables, as well as active optical cables (AOCs). The updated documentation ensures that cables marketed under the Ultra High Speed label consistently sustain the full 48 Gbps bandwidth alongside low electro-magnetic interference (EMI).

4. Addressing Specification Ambiguity

Under HDMI licensing guidelines, features such as 48 Gbps bandwidth, VRR, ALLM, and SBTM are optional rather than mandatory. HDMI 2.1b provides clearer guidance on device capabilities declaration, requiring manufacturers to explicitly state which HDMI 2.1b features a device supports rather than relying solely on the version number for marketing.

Impact on Consumers and Manufacturers

For hardware manufacturers, HDMI 2.1b acts as a technical guideline to streamline firmware development and ensure their devices integrate smoothly with third-party hardware. For consumers, the revision requires no new purchases; existing certified Ultra High Speed HDMI cables and HDMI 2.1-compatible hardware remain fully compatible, benefiting indirectly from improved reliability and fewer HDMI handshake errors.
