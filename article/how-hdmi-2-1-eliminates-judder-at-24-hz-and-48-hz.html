How HDMI 2.1 Eliminates Judder at 24Hz and 48Hz

This article explains how the HDMI 2.1 specification enables smooth, judder-free playback for cinematic frame rates, specifically traditional 24Hz film and modern 48Hz High Frame Rate (HFR) content. By combining massive bandwidth upgrades with features like Variable Refresh Rate (VRR), Quick Media Switching (QMS), and native support for integer refresh rates, HDMI 2.1 eliminates the timing mismatches and video dropouts that historically compromised home theater motion performance.

The Origin of Motion Judder

Cinematic film is traditionally shot at 24 frames per second (fps), and certain modern productions utilize 48 fps HFR. Standard legacy television displays typically run at fixed 60Hz refresh rates. Because 60 is not evenly divisible by 24, legacy systems rely on a process called 3:2 pulldown to display 24fps content. In this process, the first frame is shown for three display cycles and the next for two display cycles. This alternating display duration creates an irregular cadence perceived as motion judder—a subtle stutter visible during sweeping camera pans and horizontal motion. A similar cadence mismatch occurs when attempting to map 48fps content onto fixed 60Hz architectures.

Native Frame Rate Transmission

HDMI 2.1 provides a massive bandwidth ceiling of up to 48 Gbps via Fixed Rate Link (FRL) architecture. This bandwidth allows source devices (such as Ultra HD Blu-ray players, streaming consoles, or media servers) to output native 24Hz, 23.976Hz, or 48Hz video streams at full 4K and 8K resolutions with uncompressed 10-bit or 12-bit HDR color. By transmitting the video at its actual captured cadence rather than pre-converting it into a 60Hz container at the source level, HDMI 2.1 prevents the introduction of uneven frame timing before the signal ever reaches the display.

Display Matching via 120Hz Panels and Exact Multiples

A cornerstone of the HDMI 2.1 ecosystem is ubiquitous support for 4K video at 120Hz. A 120Hz display refresh rate is uniquely suited for cinematic content because 120 is an exact integer multiple of 24 (24 × 5 = 120).

When an HDMI 2.1 source delivers a 24Hz signal to a compatible 120Hz panel, the display can execute a true 5:5 pulldown, repeating each frame exactly five times without temporal distortion. Similarly, for 48Hz content, modern HDMI 2.1 displays can adjust their refresh timing to match 48Hz directly or step up to a multiple such as 144Hz (48 × 3) or 240Hz, guaranteeing that every single frame remains on screen for the exact same duration.

Quick Media Switching (QMS)

Historically, changing a display’s refresh rate to match incoming 24Hz or 48Hz media caused an annoying "HDMI bonk"—a momentary black screen lasting several seconds while the clock and resolution re-synchronized. Because of this disruption, many streaming devices forced all output to 60Hz by default, relying on 3:2 pulldown and inducing judder.

HDMI 2.1 introduces Quick Media Switching (QMS). Built on the foundation of Variable Refresh Rate (VRR), QMS allows source devices to instantly switch between 24Hz, 48Hz, 50Hz, and 60Hz frame rates without any frame loss, audio dropouts, or blank screens. This enables devices to seamlessly match their output to the source material's native frame rate on a per-title or per-scene basis.

Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) Integration

HDMI 2.1 VRR synchronizes the display’s physical refresh cycle to the source’s render rate. While primarily utilized for gaming to prevent screen tearing, VRR also benefits dynamic or non-standard frame rates. By locking the television panel to the exact delivery timing of 24.00Hz, 23.976Hz, or 48.00Hz media streams, the display only paints a new frame when the source outputs one. This hardware-level synchronization guarantees perfectly uniform frame pacing and entirely eliminates the physical cause of display judder.
