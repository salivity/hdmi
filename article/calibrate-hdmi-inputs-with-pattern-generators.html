Calibrate HDMI Inputs with Pattern Generators

Calibration technicians rely on specialized reference pattern generators connected directly to a display's HDMI inputs to establish an objective baseline for video performance. By feeding mathematically precise, uncompressed test signals into the display, technicians eliminate source-device variables and accurately measure how the panel processes video. Through a systematic sequence of test patterns and optical measurement tools, technicians fine-tune HDMI input settings—including brightness, contrast, white balance, and color management—to match rigorous industry standards such as Rec. 709 and DCI-P3/Rec. 2020.

Signal Configuration and Preparation

Before making adjustments, the technician configures the pattern generator to match the intended signal format. This includes selecting the appropriate resolution (e.g., 1080p, 4K), frame rate (e.g., 24Hz, 60Hz), dynamic range (SDR, HDR10, Dolby Vision), color space (YCbCr 4:2:2, YCbCr 4:4:4, or RGB), and bit depth (8-bit, 10-bit, or 12-bit). Matching these parameters ensures the display's HDMI receiver handles the signal under real-world operating conditions without unexpected color space conversions.

Setting Black Level (Brightness)

To set the black level, technicians display a PLUGE (Picture Line-Up Generation Equipment) pattern. This pattern displays reference black along with sub-black (below level 16 in standard 8-bit video) and above-black bars (levels 17 and higher).

  1. The technician adjusts the display's "Brightness" control until the sub-black bars blend seamlessly into the background.
  2. The above-black bars must remain clearly visible. This prevents "crushing" shadow detail while ensuring true black levels are properly rendered without backlight or panel glow.

Setting Peak White (Contrast)

To adjust the display’s maximum luminance without clipping, a high-level step pattern or white clipping pattern is generated (typically showing video levels 230 through 254).

  1. The technician raises the "Contrast" control to maximize dynamic range.
  2. The setting is lowered if the near-white bars (up to level 235 for standard broadcast or level 254 for full range) blend together or if color tinting (discoloration caused by one color sub-pixel clipping prematurely) occurs.
  3. A light meter (colorimeter) is simultaneously used to confirm the peak luminance meets standard targets (e.g., 100–120 nits for SDR in a dark room).

Adjusting Grayscale and White Balance

Precise grayscale tracking ensures neutral tones without color contamination across all luminance levels.

  1. The technician generates standard window patterns representing incremental steps of white, typically in 10% increments from 10% (dark gray) to 100% (peak white). Window patterns, rather than full-field patterns, are used to prevent auto-dimming or power-limiting circuits from skewing readings on modern OLED and LED panels.
  2. A spectrophotometer or colorimeter reads the light emitted from each patch.
  3. The technician uses the display's 2-point (Gain/Bias) controls to correct color temperature at 30% and 80% luminance, aiming for the D65 standard target (x: 0.3127, y: 0.3290).
  4. If available, 10-point or 20-point controls are adjusted to remove residual red, green, or blue errors across the entire grayscale range until Delta E (perceptible error) drops below the visible threshold.

Fine-Tuning the Color Management System (CMS)

To calibrate the gamut, the generator outputs reference primary (Red, Green, Blue) and secondary (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow) patches, typically at 75% or 100% saturation and luminance.

  1. The sensor measures the chromaticity coordinates and light output of each color.
  2. The technician uses the display’s CMS controls to independently shift Hue, Saturation, and Luminance (Brightness) for each color channel.
  3. Adjustments are mapped directly against the target standard's coordinate boundaries to eliminate over-saturation, under-saturation, and phase drift.

Edge Enhancement and Aspect Ratio Verification

Finally, the technician projects crosshatch and multiburst line-frequency patterns.

  1. The display's aspect ratio is verified to ensure pixel-to-pixel mapping (often labeled "Just Scan" or "1:1") with zero overscan.
  2. The "Sharpness" control is reduced until bright white halos (ringing artifacts) disappear from along the high-contrast lines without softening the underlying image resolution.
