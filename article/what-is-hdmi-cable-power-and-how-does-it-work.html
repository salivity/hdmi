What Is HDMI Cable Power and How Does It Work?

HDMI Cable Power is a specialized feature introduced in the HDMI 2.1a specification designed to deliver extra electrical current directly to active HDMI cables without needing a separate external power adapter. Over long distances, high-bandwidth audio and video signals degrade rapidly through standard passive copper. While active cables solve this using built-in booster chips, they traditionally required an unwieldy external USB power connection. This article explains what HDMI Cable Power is, how it supplies supplemental current to long-reach copper cables, and what is required to use it properly.

The Challenge with Long-Reach Copper Cables

Passive copper HDMI cables have physical limits. As resolutions and refresh rates have increased—requiring data rates up to 48 Gbps for HDMI 2.1—the maximum effective length of passive copper cables has shrunk. When transmitting 4K at 120Hz or 8K at 60Hz, signal attenuation and electromagnetic interference cause data loss over distances greater than a few meters.

To overcome this, active HDMI cables integrate equalization chips (amplifiers or retimers) inside the connector housing. These active components boost and clean the high-speed differential signal, allowing copper cables to span much longer distances without degradation. However, these internal chips require dedicated electrical power to operate.

What Is HDMI Cable Power?

Standard HDMI ports provide a nominal 5-volt power pin intended primarily for basic handshakes, such as reading Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) and Hot Plug Detection. This standard supply usually caps out at around 55 milliamps, which is insufficient to power modern, high-speed active signal processors.

In the past, active HDMI cables solved this limitation by incorporating an attached USB pigtail cable at one end, requiring users to find an open USB port or a wall outlet nearby just to power the cable.

HDMI Cable Power eliminates the need for separate power wires. Defined by the HDMI Forum in the HDMI 2.1a update, this feature allows an active cable to draw higher supplemental current directly from the source device’s HDMI port, providing an all-in-one cable solution.

How Supplemental Current Is Delivered

HDMI Cable Power works by upgrading the source device's internal power delivery architecture and utilizing specific pin configurations within the HDMI interface:

  1. Enhanced Current Supply: Source devices supporting HDMI Cable Power are engineered to supply significantly more current over the connector's 5V power line—up to 300 mA at 5 volts—safely powering the integrated signal boosters inside the cable.
  2. Directional Architecture: Because the signal amplification happens inside the cable and relies on power drawn directly from the transmitter, these cables are strictly directional. One end is labeled "Source" (to connect to the PC, console, or media player), and the other is labeled "Display" (to connect to the TV, monitor, or projector). The source connector draws the supplemental power and routes it to the internal microchips that drive the signal down the line.
  3. Overcurrent Protection: The specification outlines safeguards to prevent damage. A source device supporting HDMI Cable Power detects whether the connected cable is standard passive or active. If it detects a compatible active cable, it permits the higher current draw while maintaining protection against short circuits or surges.

System Compatibility and Legacy Support

To use an HDMI Cable Power connection without external cables, the transmitting device must natively support the feature. If you connect an HDMI Cable Power-enabled cable to an older or unsupported HDMI source, the port will not output the necessary supplemental current, and the cable will fail to transmit a signal.

To maintain backward compatibility, manufacturers produce active cables that support HDMI Cable Power but also include an optional, detachable power connector (such as a USB Type-C or Micro-USB port on the connector housing). If the source device does not support HDMI Cable Power, users can plug in an external USB cable to supply the supplemental current manually, ensuring the active copper cable works across legacy hardware.
