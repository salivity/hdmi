Can HDMI Support 192kHz 24-Bit 8-Channel LPCM Audio?

Yes, an HDMI port can natively transport full uncompressed 192 kHz, 24-bit, 8-channel LPCM (Linear Pulse-Code Modulation) audio. This capability has been an integral part of the HDMI specification since HDMI 1.0 was introduced in 2002. However, whether a user actually achieves this level of throughput depends on the type of HDMI connection used and the hardware capabilities of both the source and the receiving device.

Bandwidth and Protocol Capability

Uncompressed 8-channel audio at 192 kHz with a 24-bit depth requires a sustained digital audio bandwidth of approximately 36.86 Mbps (\(8 \times 192,000 \times 24\)). HDMI easily accommodates this data rate because audio is multiplexed alongside video during the video stream's horizontal and vertical blanking intervals using HDMI Audio Sample Packets.

Because the HDMI audio clock was engineered to handle high-resolution multi-channel streams from the standard's inception, standard HDMI input-to-output links (such as a media PC, game console, or Blu-ray player connected directly to an Audio/Video Receiver) natively pass the full, uncompressed stream without downsampling or lossy compression.

Standard HDMI vs. ARC vs. eARC

While standard port-to-port HDMI connections handle 192 kHz 24-bit 8-channel LPCM, differences arise when transmitting audio over Audio Return Channel protocols:

  • Direct HDMI Connections: Fully supports 8-channel, 192 kHz, 24-bit LPCM.
  • eARC (Enhanced Audio Return Channel): Introduced in HDMI 2.1, eARC provides up to 37 Mbps of dedicated audio bandwidth, allowing it to natively transport uncompressed 8-channel, 192 kHz, 24-bit LPCM from a television back to a sound system.
  • ARC (Standard Audio Return Channel): Does not support this stream. Standard ARC is limited to roughly 1 Mbps of bandwidth, restricting uncompressed LPCM transmission to 2.0 stereo channels at 48 kHz.

Practical Hardware Considerations

Although the HDMI physical layer and transmission protocol support this audio standard, three conditions must be met across the chain:

  1. EDID Negotiation: The receiving device (e.g., an AV processor) must broadcast support for 8-channel 192 kHz/24-bit LPCM in its Extended Display Identification Data (EDID). If the display or receiver EDID lacks this capability, the source will automatically downsample or downmix the audio.
  2. Operating System and Source Capabilities: Operating systems (such as Windows, macOS, or Linux) and playback software must be configured to output 7.1 or 8-channel multi-channel audio at 192 kHz without software-level downsampling.
  3. DAC and DSP Hardware: The receiver's internal Digital-to-Analog Converters (DACs) and Digital Signal Processors (DSPs) must possess enough processing overhead to decode eight discrete 192 kHz streams simultaneously, as some budget equipment can only process 192 kHz across two stereo channels.
