Can Firmware Add HDMI QMS to Older HDMI 2.1 TVs?

While Quick Media Switching (QMS) is an official feature of the HDMI 2.1 specification designed to eliminate the temporary black screen when switching frame rates, it cannot be added to older HDMI 2.1 televisions via a retroactive display firmware update. Despite sharing the underlying architecture of Variable Refresh Rate (VRR), QMS requires dedicated physical hardware in the display's HDMI receiver and video processing chipset that older televisions simply do not possess.

The Technical Limitation of Older Hardware

HDMI QMS is built upon the HDMI 2.1 VRR protocol, leading many to believe it is a software-level implementation that can be bridged with an over-the-air update. However, standard VRR and QMS handle timing shifts differently:

  • Standard VRR: Adapts the refresh rate dynamically during active rendering, primarily intended to eliminate screen tearing in video games where frame rates fluctuate rapidly.
  • QMS: Specifically instructs the television to change its base frame rate (such as transitioning from 24 Hz film to 60 Hz broadcast video) without forcing the HDMI clock and video pipeline to reset.

Older HDMI 2.1 processing chips (System on Chips, or SoCs) lack the necessary hardware-level registers and timing controllers to adjust clock boundaries seamlessly. When an older TV receives an altered frame rate metadata packet, the physical hardware is forced to initiate an HDMI renegotiation—known as an AV mute or "HDMI bonk"—to resynchronize the audio and video clocks.

The Difference Between HDMI 2.1 and HDMI 2.1a

QMS was officially clarified under the HDMI 2.1a revision as "QMS-VRR." Displays manufactured before this standard finalized—including flagship HDMI 2.1 sets released between 2019 and 2022—were built with early-generation HDMI receivers (such as older silicon from MediaTek, Novatek, or proprietary custom processors from brands like Sony and LG).

Because these physical interface chips are hardwired at the factory, no level of software or firmware alteration can grant them the capacity to process QMS signaling.

Manufacturer Implementation and Current Support

Real-world deployment confirms this hardware dependency. Television manufacturers only began incorporating QMS into consumer models starting in 2023 (for example, LG's C3 and G3 OLED series).

No television manufacturer—including LG, Samsung, Sony, or TCL—has released a firmware update that retroactively enabled QMS on older displays. Even models with powerful processors and full 48 Gbps HDMI 2.1 bandwidth remain unable to utilize the feature.

If you own an HDMI 2.1 television manufactured prior to 2023, you must upgrade to a newer television explicitly rated for HDMI 2.1a QMS to eliminate frame-rate switching blackouts when paired with supported source devices, such as the third-generation Apple TV 4K.
