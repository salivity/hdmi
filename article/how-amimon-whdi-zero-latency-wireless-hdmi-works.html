How Amimon WHDI Zero-Latency Wireless HDMI Works

Wireless HDMI video transmitters using Amimon’s Wireless Home Digital Interface (WHDI) technology deliver real-time, uncompressed high-definition video with latency under one millisecond. Unlike standard Wi-Fi-based video transmission systems that buffer, compress, and packetize data using codecs like H.264 or H.265, WHDI utilizes specialized Joint Source-Channel Coding (JSCC) and Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) radio frequencies in the 5 GHz band. This direct mapping of raw video data to radio waves eliminates traditional encoding and decoding bottlenecks, enabling instant visual feedback critical for professional video production, medical imaging, and gaming.

The Problem with Traditional Wireless Video

Conventional wireless video streaming solutions rely on standard Wi-Fi networks (IEEE 802.11). In a standard system, the HDMI transmitter accepts an uncompressed digital signal, feeds it into an encoder chip (such as an AVC/H.264 or HEVC/H.265 hardware encoder), compresses the frames into data packets, and sends them via standard TCP/IP or UDP network layers.

This process introduces substantial latency, typically ranging from 30 to over 200 milliseconds. Frame buffering is required at both the transmission and reception ends to reassemble lost packets and smooth out jitter. While acceptable for passive consumer media streaming, this delay makes real-time focus pulling, camera tracking, and interactive control impossible.

How WHDI Eliminates Latency

Amimon bypassed the traditional compression pipeline entirely by developing WHDI, an architecture that treats video transmission fundamentally differently from typical computer data networks.

1. Joint Source-Channel Coding (JSCC)

The core technological breakthrough of WHDI is Joint Source-Channel Coding. Instead of viewing video as generic digital bits that must be encoded into binary packets, WHDI breaks video pixels down into different levels of visual significance:

  • Most Significant Bits (MSBs): These represent the essential structure, luminance, and basic color of the image. The human eye easily detects errors in these bits.
  • Least Significant Bits (LSBs): These represent subtle fine details, micro-gradients, and fine noise. Visual perception is far less sensitive to minor variations here.

WHDI does not wait to assemble full frame buffers. Instead, it processes pixels as they arrive from the HDMI source, applying robust, high-protection digital modulation to the MSBs while simultaneously mapping the LSBs to more direct, analog-like radio channel modulations.

2. Graceful Degradation Instead of Frame Dropping

Because of JSCC, when the radio frequency (RF) environment experiences interference or the transmitter moves farther away, the system does not pause to retransmit lost packets or drop entire frames. Instead, only the least significant data suffers from noise, resulting in a slight, momentary loss of fine detail or minor visual grain—much like analog transmission—while maintaining continuous 60-frame-per-second video synchronization with zero perceptible delay.

3. High-Bandwidth MIMO and 5 GHz Operation

Uncompressed 1080p60 video requires roughly 3 Gbps of bandwidth. To handle this high throughput without full-frame compression, WHDI transmitters operate in the 5 GHz unlicensed band (typically between 4.9 GHz and 5.9 GHz) utilizing 20 MHz or 40 MHz channel widths.

WHDI utilizes advanced 4x5 or 4x1 MIMO (Multiple-Input Multiple-Output) antenna configurations along with Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing (OFDM). By transmitting multiple spatial streams simultaneously across several antennas, the system maximizes data throughput and exploits non-line-of-sight signal reflections to maintain a rock-solid link through obstacles and moving obstacles on set.

4. Hardware-Level Video Handshaking

An HDMI transmitter equipped with WHDI also incorporates dedicated hardware chips for handling High-Definition Multimedia Interface (HDMI) handshakes, EDID (Extended Display Identification Data), and HDCP (High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection) encryption in real time. Rather than running a software operating system that processes connections sequentially, dedicated ASICs (Application-Specific Integrated Circuits) process audio, video, and control signals concurrently at the hardware clock level.

Practical Impact

By combining Joint Source-Channel Coding, direct RF modulation, and 5 GHz MIMO technology, an Amimon WHDI-based HDMI transmitter delivers true zero-latency performance (measured at less than 1 millisecond). This ensures that the monitor receives the frame simultaneously with the camera sensor, providing real-time accuracy for professional directors, focus pullers, drone operators, and live broadcasters.
