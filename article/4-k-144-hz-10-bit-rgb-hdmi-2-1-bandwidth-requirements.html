4K 144Hz 10-Bit RGB HDMI 2.1 Bandwidth Requirements

Transmitting uncompressed 4K video at 144Hz with 10-bit color depth in full RGB (4:4:4) format requires an active video bit-rate of 35.83 Gbps and a total transmission bandwidth between 39.2 Gbps and 42.8 Gbps when including blanking overhead. This technical breakdown explains the exact mathematical calculation for both active and total bit-rates, analyzes timing blanking overhead, and details how the signal fits within the HDMI 2.1 specification.

Active Video Bit-Rate Calculation

The active video data rate represents the raw data required purely for rendering the visible pixels on the screen, excluding blanking intervals, audio, and metadata packets.

  1. Resolution: 4K Ultra HD consists of 3840 horizontal pixels by 2160 vertical pixels: \[\text{Total Active Pixels} = 3840 \times 2160 = 8,294,400 \text{ pixels per frame}\]

  2. Color Depth and Chroma Subsampling: A 10-bit RGB (4:4:4) signal assigns 10 bits per channel across three channels (Red, Green, and Blue): \[\text{Bits Per Pixel (bpp)} = 10 \text{ bits} \times 3 \text{ channels} = 30 \text{ bits per pixel}\]

  3. Frame Rate: The display refreshes 144 times per second (144 Hz).

Multiplying these parameters yields the active payload requirement: \[\text{Active Data Rate} = 8,294,400 \text{ pixels} \times 30 \text{ bits} \times 144 \text{ frames/second} = 35,831,808,000 \text{ bps}\]

The active video payload is 35.83 Gbps.

Total Signal Bandwidth (Including Blanking Overhead)

Digital video protocols require blanking intervals (horizontal and vertical blanking) to synchronize displays, carry audio, and transmit packetized metadata. Because of this, the total transmission bandwidth is higher than the active data rate.

  • With VESA CVT-RB2 (Reduced Blanking v2): Gaming monitors and modern high-refresh-rate displays universally employ reduced blanking to minimize unnecessary overhead. Under CVT-RB2 timing, blanking adds approximately 8% to 10% overhead, resulting in an uncompressed stream requirement of roughly 39.19 Gbps.
  • With Standard CTA-861 Timing: Standard non-reduced timing increases total horizontal and vertical lines, adding around 20% overhead. Under standard timing parameters, the total link requirement reaches approximately 42.77 Gbps.

HDMI 2.1 Capability and FRL

HDMI 2.1 supports up to 48 Gbps of raw physical bandwidth across four lanes using Fixed Rate Link (FRL) architecture:

  • Link Rate (FRL6): 4 lanes running at 12 Gbps per lane provide a total raw bandwidth of 48 Gbps.
  • Effective Bandwidth: HDMI 2.1 employs a 16b/18b encoding scheme, which introduces an 11.11% encoding overhead: \[\text{Maximum Effective Data Rate} = 48 \text{ Gbps} \times \left(\frac{16}{18}\right) \approx 42.66 \text{ Gbps}\]

Because an uncompressed 4K 144Hz 10-bit RGB signal with reduced blanking requires ~39.2 Gbps, it sits strictly below the 42.66 Gbps ceiling. Therefore, HDMI 2.1 can carry an uncompressed 4K 144Hz 10-bit RGB signal natively over FRL6 without requiring Display Stream Compression (DSC). If standard CTA blanking is used instead of reduced blanking, the transmission slightly exceeds 42.66 Gbps, in which case DSC or chroma subsampling (4:2:2 or 4:2:0) becomes necessary.
