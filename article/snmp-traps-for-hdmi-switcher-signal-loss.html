SNMP Traps for HDMI Switcher Signal Loss

Intelligent HDMI switchers deploy Simple Network Management Protocol (SNMP) to provide real-time audiovisual health monitoring across enterprise networks. When a video feed drops or disconnects, the device issues specific SNMP management traps—unsolicited alert messages sent to an SNMP manager or Network Management System (NMS)—to notify administrators of the disruption, identify the failure point, and report automated failover actions.

1. Loss of Video Signal / Sync Trap

The most direct trap generated during a signal loss event is an enterprise-specific video sync loss alert (often defined in vendor MIBs as hdmiInputLoss, videoLossTrap, or syncLoss). This trap triggers when the switcher's receiver hardware detects the disappearance of active video data while the physical cable may still be connected. The payload (variable bindings or varbinds) typically includes the affected physical port number, resolution state, and a timestamp.

2. TMDS Clock Loss Trap

HDMI relies on Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) to transmit audio and video data. If the source stops transmitting or crashes, the physical receiver detects a loss of the TMDS clock signal. Switchers that monitor lower-level physical layer metrics transmit a tmdsClockLoss or rxClockUnlock trap, pinpointing that the digital transmission carrier has collapsed.

3. Hot Plug Detect (HPD) and +5V Signal State Traps

Physical disconnections trigger physical-layer monitoring traps. When an HDMI source is powered off or unplugged, the switcher's input port detects the loss of the +5V Power line from the transmitter, pulling the Hot Plug Detect (HPD) line low. The switcher issues traps such as hpdStateChange or hdmiInputDisconnected to indicate that the physical connection itself has been broken rather than just an empty or corrupted video stream.

4. HDCP Authentication Failure Trap

A signal drop can occur despite a valid physical connection if High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP) handshaking fails. When encryption renegotiation fails, the switcher blanks the output to protect content and transmits an hdcpAuthFailed or hdcpKeyError trap. This distinguishes between content-protection lockouts and hardware or cable failures.

5. Automatic Failover and Source Switch Traps

Most intelligent HDMI switchers feature automated backup switching. Once a primary signal loss is confirmed, the unit automatically switches to a secondary input or fallback media source. Concurrently with the loss alert, the device sends an operational trap—such as autoSwitchTriggered, inputSwitched, or redundancyActive—identifying that the active route has changed from the failed input to an operational secondary input.

6. Standard MIB-II linkDown Trap

For switchers that map individual HDMI ports as virtual network interfaces within standard RFC 1213 / IF-MIB tables, a generic linkDown (trap code 2) trap may be emitted. This indicates to generalized NMS software that an interface transitioned from an operational status of up to down.
