What Is Quick Frame Transport and How Does It Reduce Lag?

Quick Frame Transport (QFT) is an HDMI 2.1 feature engineered to minimize display latency for gaming and virtual reality applications. By transmitting individual video frames across the HDMI cable at much faster transfer rates, QFT drastically cuts down the time between a frame being rendered by a graphics processor and displayed on a screen. This article explains the mechanics of Quick Frame Transport, the concept of transport lag, and how this technology enables a more responsive visual experience.

Understanding Display Latency and Transport Lag

To understand QFT, it helps to examine how traditional HDMI video links operate. When a source device—such as a gaming console or PC—sends a frame to a display, that frame is not delivered instantaneously. Instead, the pixels are transmitted sequentially over the cable line by line, from top to bottom.

Under standard transmission protocols, the speed at which a frame travels over the cable is tied to the active refresh rate. For example, on a standard 60Hz display, a single refresh cycle lasts approximately 16.6 milliseconds (ms). Transmitting a single frame across the HDMI link takes up nearly that entire 16.6 ms window. This duration is known as "transport lag." Transport lag is only one part of total input latency—which also includes controller polling, GPU rendering, and display processing—but it represents a fixed bottleneck in the physical pipeline.

How Quick Frame Transport Works

Quick Frame Transport decouples the frame transmission speed from the display's target refresh rate. Instead of taking the entire duration of a refresh cycle to push pixel data across the wire, QFT increases the transmission bandwidth to send the completed frame in a quick burst.

For instance, if a game is outputting at 60Hz, an HDMI 2.1 connection utilizing QFT can transmit each frame at the physical transport rate of a higher refresh rate, such as 120Hz or 240Hz.

  • Standard 60Hz Transmission: Frame transport takes ~16.6 ms.
  • QFT-Enabled 60Hz Transmission: The physical transfer can take as little as ~8.3 ms (matching a 120Hz transport rate) or ~4.1 ms (matching a 240Hz transport rate).

Once the frame is rapidly transferred into the display’s internal buffer, the transmission link remains idle for the remainder of the refresh period. The display still refreshes at 60Hz, but the time the frame spent in transit over the cable is significantly reduced.

The Benefits of QFT

The primary benefit of Quick Frame Transport is a direct, measurable decrease in input lag. This delivers several practical advantages:

  • More Responsive Gaming: Controller, mouse, and keyboard inputs translate to on-screen actions faster. For competitive titles where milliseconds dictate outcomes, eliminating transport delay improves reaction times.
  • Smoother Virtual Reality (VR): VR systems require ultra-low "motion-to-photon" latency—the delay between physical head movement and the updated image arriving in the headset. Excessive latency causes disorientation and motion sickness. QFT helps keep delivery speeds well within comfortable thresholds.
  • Complement to Variable Refresh Rates (VRR): QFT operates alongside technologies like Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) and Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM) to ensure the entire rendering-to-display pipeline remains as low-latency and tear-free as possible.

Hardware Requirements

To utilize Quick Frame Transport, both the source device (GPU or console) and the receiving sink device (TV, monitor, or VR headset) must support the HDMI 2.1 specification and explicitly implement QFT. An Ultra High Speed HDMI cable certified for HDMI 2.1 bandwidths is also required to facilitate the faster transmission bursts. When properly configured across compatible hardware, QFT functions automatically in the background without requiring manual intervention from the user.
