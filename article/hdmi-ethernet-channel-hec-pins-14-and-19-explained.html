HDMI Ethernet Channel HEC Pins 14 and 19 Explained

This article explores the technical function of physical pins 14 and 19 in the HDMI 1.4 specification, focusing on how they operate as a differential pair to deliver the HDMI Ethernet Channel (HEC). It details the pin reassignment from earlier standards, the signaling mechanisms used to transmit bidirectional 100 Mbps network data, and how this pair simultaneously handles Hot Plug Detect and Audio Return Channel functions without signal interference.

The Role of Physical Pins 14 and 19

In HDMI 1.4, the specification introduced the HDMI Ethernet Channel (HEC) to integrate standard networking capabilities into the existing audio/video interface. To achieve this without altering the 19-pin physical form factor of the standard Type-A connector, HDMI 1.4 repurposed two existing lines:

  • Pin 14 (Utility / HEAC+): Previously designated as "Reserved" in HDMI 1.0 through 1.3, this pin was assigned as the Utility pin, serving as the positive line of the differential pair.
  • Pin 19 (Hot Plug Detect / HEAC-): Previously dedicated solely to standard Hot Plug Detect (HPD), this pin was repurposed to act as the negative line of the differential pair while retaining its original detection duties.

Together, these two pins form a shielded twisted pair known collectively as HEAC (HDMI Ethernet and Audio Return Channel).

Primary Functions of the HEC Differential Pair

1. Bidirectional 100 Mbps Ethernet (HEC)

The primary function of the differential pair on pins 14 and 19 is to carry high-speed, bidirectional Ethernet traffic at speeds up to 100 Mbps (equivalent to 100Base-TX). By operating as a balanced differential transmission line, the pair cancels out electromagnetic interference (EMI) and cross-talk from the adjacent high-speed Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) clock and data channels. This enables internet-connected devices—such as smart televisions—to share network connectivity directly with Blu-ray players, gaming consoles, or audio-video receivers without routing separate Cat5e/Cat6 Ethernet cables to each device.

2. Signal Multiplexing (HEC, ARC, and HPD)

Pins 14 and 19 do not solely carry Ethernet data; they perform multiple functions simultaneously through frequency and signaling separation:

  • Hot Plug Detect (DC Signaling): Pin 19 maintains its traditional HPD role via DC voltage levels. A source device monitors the DC voltage on pin 19 (pulled high to +5V by the sink device) to detect when a display has been connected or disconnected.
  • HDMI Ethernet Channel (Differential AC Signaling): HEC network data is transmitted as an AC-coupled differential signal across both pins (HEAC+ and HEAC-), operating independently of the DC voltage used by the HPD mechanism.
  • Audio Return Channel (ARC): When configured for Common Mode ARC, downstream audio is sent back from the display to an AV receiver using both pins in a common-mode arrangement, or as a single-ended signal via Pin 14, depending on the implementation mode.

Implementation and Cable Requirements

To utilize the HEC functionality of pins 14 and 19, the physical HDMI cable must be wired specifically to support it. Standard HDMI cables left pin 14 unconnected or unshielded. Cables supporting HEC are labeled as "High Speed HDMI Cable with Ethernet" or "Standard HDMI Cable with Ethernet." In these cables, pins 14 and 19 are manufactured as an internally shielded twisted pair with a controlled 100-ohm differential impedance, ensuring network signal integrity over extended cable lengths.
