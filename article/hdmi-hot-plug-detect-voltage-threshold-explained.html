HDMI Hot Plug Detect Voltage Threshold Explained

The HDMI Hot Plug Detect (HPD) line allows a source device to recognize when a sink device, such as a monitor or television, has been connected or disconnected. To trigger and sustain an active HPD state, the HDMI specification mandates an operating voltage threshold between 2.4V and 5.3V. When the voltage on this line reaches or exceeds 2.4V, the source registers the display as connected and initiates the exchange of Extended Display Identification Data (EDID).

Operating Voltage Specifications

The HPD signal operates using standard 5V-tolerant TTL logic levels across Pin 19 of a standard HDMI Type-A connector. The transmitter (source) evaluates the voltage asserted by the receiver (sink) based on standard thresholds:

  • Active (High) State: The minimum threshold required to guarantee an active trigger is 2.4V, with a maximum allowable voltage of 5.3V. While some hardware receivers with standard TTL inputs may toggle as low as 2.0V, HDMI compliance testing specifically requires a minimum of 2.4V to certify a reliable connection.
  • Inactive (Low) State: The signal is considered inactive or disconnected when the voltage drops between 0.0V and 0.4V (with an absolute maximum low threshold of 0.8V).

How the HPD Mechanism Functions

The detection process relies on an electrical handshake between Pin 18 (+5V Power) and Pin 19 (Hot Plug Detect):

  1. Power Supply: The source device supplies a +5V reference voltage via Pin 18 (tolerated between 4.7V and 5.3V, delivering at least 55mA).
  2. Assertion: The sink receives this +5V line, routes it through its internal circuitry, and drives Pin 19 high (above 2.4V) once it is ready to communicate.
  3. EDID Readout: Upon sensing the voltage rise past the 2.4V threshold on Pin 19, the source pulls display configuration data through the I²C-based Display Data Channel (DDC).

Low-Pulse Reset Requirements

In addition to detecting a steady-state high voltage, HDMI sources monitor HPD for state transitions. If a sink needs to force the source to re-read its EDID—such as when switching inputs or changing display modes—it pulls Pin 19 below 0.4V. To register as an intentional disconnect or reset event rather than electrical noise, this low pulse must be sustained for a minimum duration of 100 milliseconds. Any drop in voltage shorter than this specification may be filtered out by hardware debouncing circuits.
