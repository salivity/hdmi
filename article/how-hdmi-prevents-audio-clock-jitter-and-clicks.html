How HDMI Prevents Audio Clock Jitter and Clicks

The HDMI specification prevents audio sample clock jitter and audible clicks by utilizing an Audio Clock Regeneration (ACR) mechanism based on precise numeric fractional dividers, deep First-In, First-Out (FIFO) elastic buffers, and low-bandwidth Phase-Locked Loops (PLLs) in the sink device. Because HDMI does not carry a dedicated physical audio clock line, audio data is packetized and transmitted alongside video during blanking intervals across the Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) lines. To prevent the timing variations and packet burstiness of this approach from causing audible dropouts or pops, HDMI enforces strict synchronization rules that reconstruct a continuous, jitter-free master audio clock at the receiver.

The Decoupling Problem

In an HDMI link, the physical transmission rate is locked to the video pixel clock (\(f_{pixel}\)). Audio sample rates (\(f_s\)), such as 44.1 kHz, 48 kHz, or 192 kHz, are rarely integer multiples of video pixel rates. Furthermore, high-frequency video clock jitter and the burst-like transmission of audio samples inside the horizontal and vertical blanking intervals make direct clock recovery impossible without severe sample timing instability. Left uncorrected, this timing drift causes receiver buffer overflows or underflows, leading to dropped samples that manifest as distinct audible clicks and pops.

Audio Clock Regeneration (ACR) Architecture

To establish frequency and phase lock without a dedicated cable conductor, the HDMI specification defines the Audio Clock Regeneration system. The source device periodically transmits ACR packets containing two integer values:

  • \(N\) (Numerator): A predefined integer chosen to optimize the fractional relationship between the video clock and the target audio clock. The HDMI specification mandates specific \(N\) values for standard video-audio clock pairs.
  • \(CTS\) (Cycle Time Stamp): A measured value representing the exact number of TMDS clock cycles that occur during an integer number of audio clock cycles.

The sink uses these values to reconstruct the 128× audio oversampling clock (\(128 \times f_s\)) using the formula:

\[f_{audio\_clock} = 128 \times f_s = f_{TMDS} \times \frac{N}{CTS}\]

Because the source calculates and transmits \(CTS\) dynamically, any slow drift in the source’s master video clock or audio reference is mirrored across the link, preventing long-term frequency divergence.

Jitter Attenuation via Narrow-Bandwidth PLLs

When the sink receives the \(CTS\) and \(N\) values alongside the incoming TMDS clock, it routes the synthesized reference into a local PLL. To eliminate the high-frequency jitter inherent to TMDS differential lines and video processing:

  1. The sink’s PLL is implemented with a narrow loop bandwidth (typically below a few kilohertz, and often sub-100 Hz in high-end audio receivers).
  2. The low loop bandwidth acts as a low-pass filter, absorbing and ignoring the rapid phase fluctuations of the physical video link.
  3. The voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO) inside the sink produces a stable, smooth, continuous audio clock that drives the internal Digital-to-Analog Converters (DACs) or S/PDIF/I2S transmitters.

Elastic FIFO Buffering

Because audio packets arrive in bursts during video blanking periods rather than continuously, the sink stores incoming audio samples in an elastic FIFO buffer.

  • Audio samples are written into the FIFO at the bursty transmission rate of the TMDS stream.
  • Samples are read out of the FIFO continuously using the clean, regenerated audio clock derived from the ACR PLL.

The buffer depth provides a timing cushion that absorbs transient phase variations, burst timing offsets, and packet jitter. As long as the average frequency derived from \(N\) and \(CTS\) matches the source audio generation rate, the FIFO remains stably filled near its midpoint, entirely avoiding underflow (which starves the DAC) and overflow (which forces dropped samples).

Clock Resynchronization and Soft Muting

If video resolution changes, an HDMI cable disconnects, or the TMDS clock experiences severe instability, the \(CTS\) values will deviate sharply, breaking PLL lock. To prevent the resulting buffer collapse from causing harsh, full-scale digital clicks, the HDMI standard mandates transition handling:

  • The sink detects loss of ACR lock or abnormal \(CTS\) excursions.
  • Sinks apply automated muting or rapid gain attenuation (soft-ramping) to the audio output before the FIFO empties or resets.
  • Audio un-mutes smoothly only after the PLL stabilizes, the FIFO returns to nominal operating capacity, and valid audio sample packets are consistently identified.
